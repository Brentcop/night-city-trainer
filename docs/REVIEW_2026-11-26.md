# Review: how the bots decide, what they know, and how the project's knowledge is used (2026-11-26)

Requested by the owner on 2026-11-26: "Review and determine points that could benefit from improving the ai, knowledge base, rag usage, and how the bots actions decisions are determined."

**How the request was read.** Four parts, each covered:
- **How the bots' decisions are determined:** for every kind of decision the engine asks, what each tier does (search, a fixed rule, or the fast policy inside a play-out), and which of those procedures cost games.
- **The AI:** the bots' own hand-written knowledge (card values, roles, the Gig plan, archetypes, mode rules), the coach's advice and explanations during play, and whether one decision can be measured on its own.
- **The knowledge base:** the knowledge pack (`npm run kb`): retrieval quality and freshness on today's data.
- **RAG usage:** how the project's knowledge reaches the agents that work on it (CLAUDE.md, the kb skill) and the players who use the site.

**How it was done.** Six reviewers (agents), each on one slice, read the code and data and measured where a claim hinged on numbers: the bots' decisions; their card knowledge; the coach's advice; one-decision measurement; the knowledge pack; how the knowledge is used. Five verifiers then tried to disprove the main claims with their own scripts, other seeds and other decks, and audited the harnesses for leaks; the coordinator checked the structural claims at the lines named. In all roughly 165,000 games (about 140,000 of them Medium, about 15,000 Hard, and about 6,000 played by the coach or Hard variants), several hundred thousand play-outs and 92 Game reviews, on up to 16 threads over about three hours, all on SIM_VERSION 13 (the counts are summed from the agents' reports and rounded). Every script and output is outside the repository; nothing in it changed except this file (the gitignored game cache `.cache/games/` grew). Finding ids: H (how the bots decide), K (their knowledge), C (the coach), Y (measuring one decision), P (the knowledge pack), U (how the knowledge is used). Each says whether it was read in code, computed or measured in games, whether it is new or already known, and what the verifiers found (§7).

**What it could not see.** People: every player is a bot, so "better" means more games won by bots. Browsers and phones (timings are Node on a shared machine). Anything a section's "not checked" does not name.

## The short version

**The one-paragraph diagnosis.** Hard's search is narrow in two ways, and each costs games. It plays each main-phase candidate out once, in a single imagined deal of the hidden cards that it keeps for most of the turn; scored over six shared worlds instead (as the coach does), Hard wins 55.6% [52.8, 58.4] against itself. And it searches main-phase actions, reactions and which card to sell, and nothing else: every choice inside an effect (which mode, which card to search for or discard, which Unit to target, yes or no) is a fixed rule in every tier, the coach included, although such choices come up about as often as main-phase actions (32 a game against 37). Deciding them by play-outs, with no information Hard does not have, wins 54.5% [53.4, 55.7] against stock Hard over 1,200 paired deals (53.4% on a second seed); modes and card picks carry it. Part of the mode rule is plainly wrong: a label regex reads "can't be defeated" as removal, Towerfall's single mode picks an option that does nothing, and the Legend Calls take ±2 power over Draw 1 where Draw 1 plays out better. The Gig choices, also by rule, lose nothing to play-outs: the Gig plan earns its keep. The hand-written card values are only weakly related to measured play (rank correlation 0.15), and they make Medium sell Gear too eagerly (+1.1 points without them; no change for Hard, which compares its sells by rollout). The coach, Hard with twice the candidates and those six shared worlds, is the strongest player in the project (58.4% against Hard, on the meta decks and again on the other 64 lists), and the worlds are most of its edge; but it explains itself with more confidence than its play-outs support. On the knowledge side, the pack is right where code answers (`fact`, the version labels on measured records) and has drifted since its last measurement on 11-16: a cue-word change on 11-22 hides a named card behind 19 evidence findings, `fact` and the search disagree on archived events, a rating record counts one title beside two champion finishes, a search takes 3 to 5 seconds (it was 0.8), and the question set asks nothing about the forecast, the evidence pages, rating runs or version 13. And agents barely use it: 64 consumer calls in about 36,700 tool calls over 16 days, while CLAUDE.md, loaded into every session, has grown to about 38,500 tokens, 3.5 times its size on 11-13.

**What to do, in order of value for the cost.** "Bump" means a SIM_VERSION change; as with group C of REVIEW_2026-11-24, each play change lands alone with the strength botcheck, the per-deck report and a corpus rebuild.

## 1. How the bots decide (H)

### The decision map (read in code; `src/ai/heuristic.ts`, `src/ai/tiers.ts`)

| Decision | Easy | Medium | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main action | the fast prior | one step ahead over at most 40 candidates, Easy agents inside the copy, noise | Medium's screen, then the best 3 and passing rolled out once each, in the live random state |
| Whether to sell | rule | rule | rule |
| Which card to sell | rule | rule | the best of the 3 lowest keep values, one rollout each |
| Reaction | fixed power tests; never a Program | one-step resolve, kept if 0.5 better | 3 shared worlds to the end of its next turn |
| Mulligan | rule | rule | rule (plain) |
| Sub-choices (targets, card picks, modes, yes/no, Gigs) | rule | rule | rule |
| Payment sources, effect order | the engine (Eddies first, first in first out) | the engine | the engine |
| Calls | blind pick, −1.5 in the main phase, +0.5 in reactions | the same | the same |

Inside every rollout both seats are Medium-like (no deep search, 14 candidates, no noise, sells by rule), so the same rules decide every sub-choice inside every value Medium and Hard compute (H2).

Where Hard's time goes (measured, 120 Hard games, 1.35 s a game in `decide`): main actions 62.7% (37.4 a game, 22.7 ms each), reactions 23.7% (4.7 a game, 68 ms), sells 11.4% (11.5 a game), the 32 sub-choices a game about 0%.

## 2. What the bots know (K)

Every input to Easy, Medium and Hard play that is not search is hand-written, except the fitted `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS`: `cardValue` (cost and power, role bonuses from 12 text patterns, 9 choom.gg ratings copied by hand), the Gig plan's text patterns and `planScore` constants, the archetype thresholds, `CARD_TAG_POLICY` and the scripts' intent hints, the yes/no rules, the mode label scores and the sell rule's adjustments. Hard has read no card-impact table since SIM_VERSION 6; the measured tables (card impact at 40, package effects, the lift slice) reach the coach, `hard-v1` and the Deck Lab only. What works: every library card has a script or is keyword-only; the 18 imported lists brought no card the older lists lacked; every `pick()` carries a typed intent and every card tag is in `CARD_TAG_POLICY`; the candidate cap dropped 82 plays in 27,165 rollout decisions.

### C. Play changes, each its own measured bump

Order by expected effect for the cost; each lands alone with the strength botcheck (Hard `--per 1` and Medium `--per 4` on all 71 decks, lower bound at least 49.0%), the per-deck report and a corpus rebuild, as group C of REVIEW_2026-11-24 did.
1. **The mode rule** (row 1, K2): "can't be defeated" out of the removal pattern (and out of `modeWhy`'s), a target-less mode at 0, a Call's Draw 1 against its power mode by the one-step resolve. Intended metric: the Calls' Draw 1 rate and Towerfall's casts.
2. **Hard's main-phase rollouts over 6 shared worlds, clamped** (row 3, H5, C5): the largest gain measured in this review for one setting (55.6% [52.8, 58.4]), at about 6× the main-phase rollout time. A cheaper first step, each decision's single world drawn from the bot's own random stream, is unmeasured.

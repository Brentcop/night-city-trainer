# Synergy packages

Many cards are worth more together than alone. The synergy catalogue names those packages, and the synergy report measures how often each one is assembled and comes online in bot games and how games go when it does. The deck builders can keep, require, avoid, seed and move whole packages ("Using packages when building" below). The bots' evaluation has package terms and the coach names packages in its advice ("How scoring uses packages" below). This is [ROADMAP2.md](ROADMAP2.md) §1 steps 1–4.

## The catalogue (`src/content/synergies/`)

- `vocabulary.ts`: the types and the condition vocabulary (`CONDITION_TERMS`).
- `catalogue.ts`: the entries (`SYNERGY_LIST`, 26 since the owner review of 2026-11-12), read off the card text and the card scripts. Every entry is `status: 'draft'`; one is dormant.
- `index.ts`: resolving members, `synergiesIn(deck)` (which packages a list holds, copies per role, built-around, draw odds), `holdOdds` (the hypergeometric odds), `conditionHolds` (the `online` evaluator), and `ACTIVE_SYNERGIES` (every package that is not dormant).

An entry:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `id` | stable kebab-case id; reports are keyed by it, so never reuse one for a different package |
| `name`, `description` | a short name and one line in plain words (paraphrase; never the card or rules wording) |
| `family` | gig-value, street-cred, type, gear, program, trash, legends or tempo (grouping only) |
| `bond` | `requires`: the payoffs do little without the enablers; `helps`: every piece works alone, together better |
| `members` | explicit card ids with a role and a short note |
| `groups` | members by class, only when a payoff's own text names the class exactly ("a friendly ARASAKA Unit", "a BRAINDANCE Program"); `except` drops cards of the class that can't do the job (power 0, can't attack) |
| `propose`, `rejected` | predicates that propose candidates (e.g. text mentions "min Gig"). Every card a proposal matches must be a member or be in `rejected` with the reason, so a new card that fits is noticed by the tests |
| `needs` | the pieces that make it work at all (`enabler`, `payoff`, `any`); what "assembled" and the draw odds count |
| `builtAround` | the minimum copies (per role and in total) for a list to count as built around it; a Legend counts one |
| `online`, `onlineText` | what "online" means, as plain data over the condition vocabulary, and the same in words |
| `dormant` | optional `{ reason }`: the package stays in the catalogue but is switched off by default (see below) |

Roles: `enabler` makes the condition happen, `payoff` is rewarded by it, `both` does both (it fills either role, one at a time), `piece` is for a package with no such split. In a condition, a role filter of `payoff` or `enabler` also matches `both` cards.

Condition terms (definitions in `CONDITION_TERMS`): `all`, `any`, `in-play` (Units on the field, equipped Gear, face-up Legends), `used` (played, Called, gone solo, Legend play, ability, attack or reaction this turn; selling is not a use, and cards an effect plays for free are not seen), `min-gig`, `high-gig`, `max-gig`, `value-pair`, `distinct-values`, `even-and-odd`, `cred-even`, `cred-vs-rival`, `cred-at-least` (an absolute bar: Street Cred 20 for Yorinobu), `gig-cost-match` (a friendly Gig shows the printed cost of a card in your own hand), `gigs-behind`, `equipped`, `legends-up`, `trash`, `in-trash` (named cards in your trash, e.g. Adam Smasher: Metal Over Meat). Add a term only when a condition can't be phrased with these, with its definition and its case in `conditionHolds`; a term that names cards (`in-trash`) is checked against `src/data/cards.json` by the tests.

**Dormant packages.** An entry with `dormant: { reason }` is kept for reference but switched off by default: `synergiesIn` still lists it for a list that holds its pieces but never marks it built around; `builtAroundIds`, the builders' package moves (`packageMoves`) and the default keep leave it out unless it is named by id (`--require-packages`, `--seed-packages`, or `packages` in code); the bots and the coach never score it (their packages come from built-around ones); the Deck Lab's breed picker doesn't offer it; and the report keeps it out of its tables and lists it in a short "Dormant packages (not in any library deck)" section. Today: Corpo and Ganger steals (Evelyn Parker: Beautiful Enigma), a real card that no library deck plays.

**How the owner edits it.** Change an entry in `catalogue.ts` (members, roles, notes, minimums, the condition), then set `status: 'owner'`. `npx vitest run tests/synergies.test.ts` checks that every id is a real card, ids are unique, every term is defined, every proposal is confirmed or rejected, and every active library deck's packages compute. Editing the catalogue changes its fingerprint (`CATALOGUE_VERSION`); the site then says the published report was measured with an earlier catalogue until it is re-run. A deck objective may name the packages its deck is built around in the optional `synergies` field (`src/content/deckObjectives/vocabulary.ts`); the deck profile marks those "in the game plan".

**Draw odds.** `holdOdds` is the exact multivariate hypergeometric chance of holding the needed pieces after seeing the opening 6 plus one card per turn (the engine draws every turn, the first player's first included, so it is the same on the play and on the draw). Legends in the list count as held from the start, although a face-down one still has to be Called. Mulligans, extra draws and searches are not modelled; the measured rates include them.

## Measurement (`src/analysis/synergies.ts`)

`npm run sim -- analyze --synergies` (with `--decisions` if wanted) observes every game; `--synergies-only` writes only the synergy outputs and nothing else (no analysis, review, impact or decisions file). Outputs: `reports/synergies-latest.{json,md}` and, for a whole-field run without `--out`, `public/data/synergies.json` (compact, rates to 4 decimals). None of them is replaced without `--force`, and `--out DIR` keeps them all in DIR. The run manifest is in the report.

How it observes: the decision recorder gets an option, `new DecisionRecorder({ boards: true })`, which also keeps a board snapshot of each player's own view before every decision and at the end of every main phase (`BoardSnapshot` in `src/engine/telemetry.ts`: their Units, equipped Gear, face-up Legends, Gigs, both Street Creds, trash counts and, since 2026-11-12, their trash by card id and the printed costs of their own hand, both optional fields), and the first sighting of each of their main-deck cards in hand or play from turn 1 on (`CardSighting`). The engine calls the recorder at the end of each main phase (`turnEnd`), behind the same single `if` as the other hooks. It only reads; clones never record; the same seed plays identically with it on or off (`tests/decision-telemetry.test.ts`). The decision record schema is unchanged (still 1); the snapshots are a separate, optional output, and a snapshot holds only public cards plus what that player alone knows: their own sightings and their own hand's costs (never card ids from the hand, never the rival's hand; `tests/decision-telemetry.test.ts`).

Per game, per seat and per package its list holds (`observeSynergies`): member copies drawn by role, the first round the needed pieces were all held, the first round the `online` condition held (at any snapshot that turn, with that turn's uses), how many turns it held, own turns played, the result, and every member use with whether a partner piece was held then (another member of the complementary role drawn by that turn, or a partner Legend face-up). Workers aggregate per game; the main thread merges.

## Reading the report

Per list and package: games; **pieces held** (share of games with the needed pieces at some point); **by turn 2 / 4 / 6**: the share with the pieces held, the draw odds in brackets, and the share online so far (out of the games that reached that turn); **online** (share of games it came online at all); **median turn** it first came online; **won when online** versus **won when never online**, each with its game count and 95% Wilson interval; and per member card, the win rate in games it was used with a partner present and with none. "All lists pooled" mixes every list holding a package (more games, different decks, no odds).

Slices under 20 games (`MIN_SLICE_GAMES`, as in the decisions report) show only their size and are not rated.

**Association, not causal value.** A package that comes online mostly in games already going well looks good for that reason alone, and a package that only comes online when behind (e.g. Behind on Gigs) looks bad for the same reason. "Online versus never" is not what the package is worth; it says where to look. Observed "held by turn N" is usually above the odds because of extra draws and searches.

## Using packages when building (`src/lib/deckPackages.ts`)

The deck builders change one card at a time and score cards one by one, so on their own they can cut one half of a pair, strand a payoff without its enabler, or never try a whole package. `src/lib/deckPackages.ts` is the shared, plain-data layer they all use (Node scripts and the browser). On the command line it is **opt-in**: with no package flag every script makes exactly the candidates, lists and reports it made before (pinned in `tests/deck-packages-defaults.test.ts`).

**The plan** (`PackagePlan`, plain data):

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `keep` | The package's pieces in the starting list may not be cut below its **working minimum**: its `builtAround` count when the list meets it, else the pieces it `needs` at all (both roles for a package whose pieces need each other). Cards beyond that minimum stay free to change. A kept package the list doesn't hold changes nothing. |
| `require` | The result must be built around it: a change may not move the list further away, and the tuners always try the moves that add or complete it. |
| `avoid` | The result must not be built around it: no new pieces while it is, and fillers never bring its pieces in. |
| `lockCards` | Card id → copies that may not be cut (a list holding fewer keeps what it has). |

`checkChange(before, after, plan)` says whether one candidate breaks the plan and why; `checkList(deck, plan)` checks a whole list (and the deck-construction rules). Every builder filters its candidates through them, so a candidate that breaks the plan is never played; the forge's log lists the swaps the plan kept out and why.

**Package moves** (`packageMoves`), beside the one-card swaps:
- **complete**: a package with pieces of one side only (a payoff without its enabler, or the reverse) gets the missing side, up to that role's built-around count;
- **add**: a package the list is not built around gets its minimum working set: members legal for the Legends' RAM, by role first, cards already in the list first, then the builder's card score;
- **swap**: a package the list holds for one it isn't built around, when the two moves are within 3 cards of each other in size;
- **remove**: every main-deck piece of a package, refilled with the best legal cards outside it. A package too big to cut whole (more than 10 copies, e.g. Programs as fuel, where every Program is an enabler) loses only the side that dissolves it: its payoffs, or its enablers when they are fewer.

The cards cut to make room go cheapest first: cards in no kept or required package before the rest, then cards in fewer of the list's built-around packages, then the lowest score. Legends never change. Every list a move makes passes `validateDeck`, keeps its size and respects the plan; one move changes at most 10 cards; with a limit, moves are taken in turn from each kind.

**Seeding** (`packageTriples`, `seedAround`, `packageCompatibility`): the Legend trios that can hold one or two packages at their built-around minimums (enough RAM in the members' colours), best first: Legends that are pieces themselves, the Legends' strength, two colours or fewer. The list is filled the way explorers are filled, then each package is topped up to its minimum. Two packages that no trio can hold are refused with the reason in plain words. With the current catalogue every pair has a trio except the Relic loop with any package that needs a second colour: Adam Smasher: Metal Over Meat needs 6 Yellow RAM, so all three Legends must be Yellow (it pairs with the Yellow and Gear packages, e.g. Gig value matches a cost, Block then punish, Units in the trash, and with none of the Blue, Green or Red ones). Four pairs pull opposite ways (Low Gigs with High Gigs, High Gigs with Lower then swap, Behind on Street Cred with Ahead on Street Cred and with Street Cred 20+) and get a plain warning that they will rarely be online together.

**Defaults.** A deck objective's `synergies` field is the default `keep` for that deck when the owner gives no plan: on the command line only once a package flag is on (`--keep-packages none` turns it off), in the Deck Lab always. In the Deck Lab a list without one keeps the packages it is built around.

### Command line

| Flag | Scripts | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `--keep-packages a,b` | forge, optimize, season, breed-preview | keep these (see above); `none` keeps nothing. In the breeder: a bred deck is built around a kept package when a parent was |
| `--require-packages a,b` | all four | the result must be built around these |
| `--avoid-packages a,b` | all four | the result must not be built around these |
| `--lock-cards id[:n],…` | forge, optimize | never cut these cards below n copies (no n = every copy) |
| `--package-moves` | forge, optimize, season (passed to its forge) | also try whole-package moves |
| `--keep-built-around` | forge, optimize, season (passed to its forge) | also keep each list's own packages, the Deck Lab's default keep: the ones its game plan names, else the ones the starting list is built around (beside `--keep-packages`; added 2026-11-12 for Season 2, whose new decks have no game plan yet) |
| `--package-cands N` | forge, optimize | package moves per round (default 3) |
| `--seed-packages a+b,c` | season, breed-preview | the explorer slots are built around these combinations of one or two packages, in turn (the season's forge then keeps them) |
| `--partner-aware` | forge, optimize | rank cuts and adds partner-aware ("Partner-aware ranking" below); **on by itself whenever any package flag is given** |
| `--plain-ranking` | forge, optimize | turn the partner-aware ranking off again (the plain ranking with a package plan) |
| `--out DIR` | forge, optimize | write the forged list and the reports to DIR instead of the repository (for trials) |
| `--lists` | breed-preview | print each bred list and the packages it holds |

Ids are the catalogue ids (`min-gig`, `low-cred`, `program-fuel`, …). An unknown id, a package both kept and avoided, or a seed of two packages no Legend trio can hold exits with code 2, with the list of valid ids. Examples:

```
npx tsx scripts/forge.ts s1-01-mutant-rry --keep-packages high-cred --out <scratch dir>
npx tsx scripts/forge.ts meta-yyb-rogue-gear --package-moves --gens 1 --out <scratch dir>
npx tsx scripts/breed-preview.ts --seed-packages low-cred+program-fuel,arasaka --lists
```

**Provenance.** With package flags the forge writes a `// Packages:` line in the forged deck file (the plan, the built-around packages kept, added and removed, the package moves adopted), the plan and its effect in `reports/forge/<deck>.md`, and a `packages` field in its JSON; the optimizer's report and JSON name the plan and what the best list kept, added and removed; bred deck files get a `// Packages:` line, the season report names each new deck's packages and changes, and the season JSON's `bred` entries carry them. Every run manifest made with a plan has the optional `packages` field (`PackageProvenance`: the plan, seeds, the package moves tried and the catalogue version); the manifest schema stays 1.

### Partner-aware ranking (`src/lib/cutRanking.ts`)

**How cuts were picked before.** Every list tuner picked the cards to cut from one per-card number measured in isolation, and the package plan only forbade cutting below a package's working minimum:
- the forge (`forgeSwaps`, `scripts/builder-moves.ts`) and the Deck Lab tuner (`candidateSwaps`, `src/lib/deckLab.ts`): the 3 cards with the lowest "Δ when drawn" (win rate in this generation's games where the card was drawn minus where it was not; `analyzeDeck`, cards seen in 8+ games), else the format-wide impact, else 0; adds were +1 of the list's 2 best cards by the same number and the 4 best legal cards by format-wide impact; swaps ranked by add − cut;
- the optimizer (`optimizeCandidates`): the 8 (12 with `--sideboard`) cards with the lowest static `cardValue`, against the 14 strongest legal cards by `cardValue`, plus Legend swaps, shuffled deterministically;
- the package moves' room-making (`pickCuts`, `src/lib/deckPackages.ts`): cards outside kept and built-around packages first, then the lowest score;
- the bots' and the coach's sell choice (`sellRanking`, `src/ai/heuristic.ts`): `cardValue` + impact + castability, with `pkgHold` (0) as the package term; the coach then compares the 3 most sellable by rollout.

A payoff looks weak in every game its enabler was missing, and an enabler looks weak because its value shows up on the payoff, so the per-card number undervalues both.

**The score.** For a main-deck card that belongs to a package the list holds (active packages only; dormant ones are ignored):
- **partner split**: the tuner's own baseline games (this list, this generation) are split into games where the card was drawn *with* a partner piece also drawn that game (an enabler for a payoff, a payoff for an enabler, any other member for a `both` or `piece` card; a partner Legend counts once Called or played), drawn *without* one, and *not drawn*. The partner-aware number is `wr(with) − wr(not drawn)`, the same shape as "Δ when drawn" but only over the partner-present games. It is game level (anywhere in the game), from the summaries the tuners already record; the published synergy report splits each use by the turn, which needs board telemetry;
- **shrinkage**: `(n·raw + 20·plain) / (n + 20)` with n the partner-present games (`PARTNER_SHRINK_GAMES` 20: halfway at 20 games); under 8 games on either side (`MIN_SPLIT_GAMES`) it is the plain number. A card in several packages is judged with the partner of the package that depends on it most (the largest stranding drop below; the best partner-aware number when it is surplus everywhere);
- **stranding cost**: cutting one copy lowers the exact odds of holding the package's needed pieces by your own turn 4 (`holdOdds`, opening 6 + 4 draws). The drop × `STRAND_VALUE` (0.10: holding a package's pieces taken as worth 10 win-rate points, below the measured online-versus-never gaps, which are association) × ½ for a `helps` package is added to the score. So cutting one of three Towerfalls (Behind on Street Cred's only payoffs in the BBG ladder list, 59% → 44%) costs 0.75 points, and one of 26 Programs-as-fuel enablers (100% → 100%) costs nothing: surplus pieces stay fair cuts. A cut below a kept package's working set is still refused by the plan; above it the cost is continuous;
- **add credit**: a candidate add that deepens a package the list is built around, or completes one whose other side is at its built-around count, gets the assembly odds it adds × the same weight. A package card with no format-wide impact is a candidate add when it earns credit.

The score that ranks cuts is the partner-aware number plus every stranding penalty; adds rank by their plain score plus credit. Both numbers are kept. The optimizer has no telemetry, so with the switch on it adds only the stranding cost (and the add credit) to `cardValue`, at the same number (a 17-point odds drop = 1.7).

**Where it is on.** The Deck Lab: on by default ("Partner-aware cuts", a checkbox with a line saying so; the run's result panel says it too). The CLI: opt-in, `--partner-aware`, and on by itself with any package flag (the season passes its package flags, so its forge step is partner-aware when they are used); `--plain-ranking` turns it off. With no package flag and no `--partner-aware`, the forge and the optimizer make exactly the candidates they made before (pinned in `tests/deck-packages-defaults.test.ts`), and `tuneDeck` without `partnerAware` plays the pinned games.

**Why a card was or was not offered.** The forge's log and JSON (`gens[].cuts`, `gens[].plainSwaps`) and the Lab ("Why these cuts", under each generation) list every card either ranking offered plus the package cards among the 8 lowest plain scores, e.g. "not offered: the package view moved it up — Δ when drawn −12.1 pts; enabler for Gig value matches a cost: 58% with a partner (26 games), 45% without (33); partner-aware −8.2 pts". The forge log also lists the swaps the plain ranking would have screened from the same games.

**The bots and the coach.** `pkgHold` stays 0, so no bot sells differently and no game result changes (written on SIM_VERSION 5; still true on 9). The coach's sell advice uses the same partner view as an explanation: "Keep Towerfall: it is your only payoff for Behind on Street Cred left, and it pairs with Jackie Welles: Pour One Out For Me in your deck."; when it advises selling a scarce piece itself, "Selling Towerfall gives up your only payoff for Behind on Street Cred left (it pairs with …); the coach still rates it the card in hand you can best spare right now."; and its sell comparison breaks near-ties (within 0.05 Gigs, well inside what its alternatives call a "close call"; `HeuristicOptions.sellKeepScarce`, 0 for every bot) toward keeping a scarce piece: "Selling Psycho Squad or Towerfall comes out about the same (within 0.05 Gigs), so keep Towerfall: it is your only payoff for Behind on Street Cred left."

### The tuner's result against the original (Deck Lab)

## How scoring uses packages (`src/ai/packages.ts`)

The bots and the coach score positions with `evaluate()` and a few rules (docs/BOT_TOOLKIT.md §3, §5). Package knowledge enters there for the player's **own** list only:

- **Which packages.** One answer for every layer since 2026-11-24 (`listPackages` in `src/lib/listPackages.ts`; "Which packages a list has, and when one is there" below): the deck objective's `synergies` when the owner named them for this exact list (matched by `listFingerprint`), else every package `synergiesIn(list)` marks built-around. The bots read the objectives' `synergies` from the generated slim table `src/content/deckObjectives/noteSynergies.ts` (list fingerprint to package ids, written by `npx tsx scripts/note-synergies.ts`; `check:data` and `tests/note-synergies.test.ts` fail when it differs from the notes), never the notes themselves, so the game workers do not carry the notes' text. Computed once per list fingerprint and cached in a module map, never in the game state (bots `structuredClone` it). Every list in the active library is built around at least one package (1 to 8 since the 2026-11-12 review). Dormant packages are never built around, so never scored.
- **Facts per position** (`packageFacts`): own copies of each package's members by role in hand, in play (Units, equipped Gear, face-up Legends), among the face-down Legends, in the trash and still in the deck. Only the player's own hand and deck contents and public cards are read; the rival's list and hidden cards never are, and inside lookahead clones the rival seat's bots get no package weights.
- **Terms** (weights in `EvalWeights`, all 0 by default): `pkgHold` (keep a piece whose partner can still be had; scaled down by how many copies do the same job, so the last payoff counts and one of twelve Programs barely does), `pkgAssembly` (both halves available), `pkgSteer` (own Gigs and Street Cred toward the condition of a package whose payoff is in hand, in play or a Legend; it also chooses the roll-in die and counts in steal, swap, adjust and reroll choices; kinds: Street Cred below / above the rival's, Street Cred 20+, even Street Cred, min Gig, 8+ Gig, value-pair, mixed values. `gig-cost-match` depends on the hand and `in-trash` on the trash, so neither is steered), `pkgProtect` (members in play of a `requires` package assembled in play).
- **What is not rewarded:** being "online" as such. Several packages come online mostly when the deck is already losing (Behind on Street Cred, Behind on Gigs, Units in the trash, Spend then punish), so no term credits the condition holding; `pkgSteer` credits only quantities the deck's own choices move, and only while a payoff is there to use them. Board, trash and tempo conditions are not steered.
- **The coach** names a package when it is the reason, in plain words with the catalogue's names: when advising a sell, "Keep Towerfall: it is your only payoff for Behind on Street Cred left, and it pairs with Jackie Welles: Pour One Out For Me in your deck." (only scarce pieces: hold credit ¼ or more; how scarce, from the player's own hand, play, Legends and deck: "your only …", "one of your last two …"), and it breaks near-ties between sells toward keeping such a piece ("Partner-aware ranking" above); on a Gig choice, "Your deck wants your Street Cred below the rival’s (Behind on Street Cred): d6 (5) would put your Street Cred at or above the rival’s."; and a live warning, "Your Street Cred (14) is not below the rival’s (6); your deck wants it lower (Behind on Street Cred)." These describe the position, so they show whatever the weights are. Since INFO-8 (2026-11-12) they are shown only for a package whose measured lift in the synergy report has a 95% interval above 0 (`src/ai/packageLift.ts`: online-vs-never win rate on this list, or pooled over every list when the list's slices are thin; Newcombe's interval), each followed by what was measured and "an association, not proof that it wins games"; a package without such a lift is left out, and with no report every note says it is an idea from the catalogue, not measured. On the 2026-11-12 report the Behind on Street Cred notes above are left out (pooled −23 points). The sell tie-break note explains the coach's own pick and is not gated. **Since 2026-11-24 the coach can be gated by a paired measurement instead** ("The package's effect, measured in pairs" below): a request that carries the seat's rows (`CoachRequest.effects`) is gated by them and not by the lift, and the Play page sends them (it fetches `public/data/package-effects.json` while the coach is on, in place of the lift slice, which the Card stats page still shows). The reason for a sell the coach chose by card value now names the bot and simulator version of the card tables it read (`COACH_TABLES`, from `src/data/analysis.json`: "by bot-match telemetry on this list: the Deep bot's games, simulator version 9, not the Hard bot the site's ratings use"), as the tips' source line does.
- **Measuring** (`scripts/package-scoring.ts`): every library deck built around a package against every meta deck, weights on vs off on the same seeds, seats and opponents; paired gain overall, per family, per package and per deck; how many of the deck's decisions each weight changes (a weights-off shadow bot asked at every decision); and how often a scarce piece was sold or discarded while its partner was obtainable. ## The package's effect, measured in pairs (`scripts/package-effects.ts`, 2026-11-24)

The coach's lift gate reads online-against-never, which follows what a package's `online` condition marks (conditions that mark winning look good, those that mark losing look bad) and contradicts the held-out record (REVIEW_2026-11-24 S1: Blockers' lift −14.3 while S2-08's swap into Blockers measured +15 points on 1,120 Hard games). The paired measurement asks the question the coach's words imply: does this list do better with the package than without it?

- **The pair.** Each package of a list (`listPackages`) against the same list with that package taken out: `removePackage` (`src/lib/deckPackages.ts`) with the hand-written `cardValue` as the score (bot-free, so the lists do not move when a telemetry table is rebuilt) and the list's other packages kept, so only this package's own pieces are cut and the best other cards take their place. A package whose pieces all belong to another of the list's packages has no such list and is recorded as not measurable, with why.
- **The games.** The forge's layout against the meta decks, as `npm run remeasure` plays them (`remeasureJobs`): game k plays meta deck k mod 7 with seed 9,000,000 + k, each list first in half; the list itself is played once for all its packages. Default 1,120 paired games a package, `--bot hard`. Nothing it plays chooses anything, so every game is held out.
- **The number.** With the package minus without, per paired game: mean, standard error, spread, and an interval corrected for the packages measured on the same list (Bonferroni, two-sided 95% over the list's packages together: `familyZ`), so a list's chance of naming any package with no effect stays at most 2.5%.
- **The file.** `public/data/package-effects.json` (`kind: 'nct-package-effects'`, schema 1, the catalogue version, one row per list, package, bot and simulator version with the cards swapped, and the packages not measurable; read strictly by `readPackageEffects`, checked by `check:data` when present). Written after each list, other lists' rows kept; a list already measured with that bot and version is refused without `--force`.
- **The coach.** `seatEffects` gives a seat its list's rows of the current simulator version and catalogue (the Hard bot's first), `effectGate` names a package only when its corrected interval is above 0, worded as a measured effect in this list ("Measured on this list in the Hard bot's games, simulator version 9: it won 6.1 points more often than the same list with Behind on Street Cred's own 3 cards swapped for others, range +2.0 to +10.2 points, over 1,120 paired games against the meta decks."); a package measured without that is not named, and one this list has no current row for is worded as an idea from the catalogue, not measured on this list. Lists outside the library (a custom or tuned deck) have no rows, so their packages read as ideas.
- **What it measures, exactly.** A package's own cards against the best other cards by hand-written card value, in that list: a large figure can mean the package works, or that its cards are simply better than the replacements `cardValue` picks. It is a paired effect of those cards in this list, not of the synergy alone, and the coach's words say "than the same list with X's own N cards swapped for others".
- **The estimate** (`npx tsx scripts/package-effects.ts --estimate --threads N`, 2026-11-24, at 1.3 s a Hard game and 0.044 s a Medium game a thread): the 71 library lists hold 228 packages by `listPackages`, 187 measurable (41 not: their pieces are all pieces of another package of the list); 288,960 games at 1,120 a package, about 8.7 hours of Hard on 12 threads (35 hours on 3) or 18 minutes of Medium on 12 (1.2 hours on 3). The 37 lists whose notes name packages alone: 82 measurable of 97, 133,280 games, about 4 hours of Hard on 12 threads or 8 minutes of Medium. At the assumed spreads (0.60 a game Hard, 0.55 Medium) a package's corrected range is about ±4.5 to ±4.9 points, and an effect of about 6 points is found 80% of the time.
- **The first run** (2026-11-24, Medium bot, SIM_VERSION 9, all 71 library lists, 1,120 paired games a package, seeds 9,000,000 + k, 288,960 games, under an hour on 10 threads of a shared machine): 187 packages measured, 41 not measurable. **106 pass** (corrected interval above 0) in 60 of the 71 lists; 57 of 82 in the 37 lists whose notes name packages. 11 are shown worse with the package (interval below 0), among them Block, then punish in GGY Gear n Go (−9.5 [−14.0, −5.0]), Arasaka Units in S2-03 (−7.9), Programs as fuel in NC-03 Static Shock (−7.0), Ahead on Street Cred in RRY Smasher (−5.4) and Gig value matches a cost in S2-01 and S1-02. By package: High Gigs passes in 26 of 33 lists, Braindance 15 of 22, Value-pairs 12 of 19, Programs as fuel 7 of 10; Gear on Units 2 of 11 and Gig value matches a cost 0 of 6. Largest: Braindance in Ladder RRG V / Smasher / Saburo +30.4 [+26.5, +34.2], Programs as fuel in Ladder BBG +26.8. Median per-game spread 0.553 (the assumed 0.55 for Medium). **Against the lift** (`public/data/lift.json`, Hard, the same 187 list-package pairs): the same sign in 127; the lift's gate passes 57, the paired gate 106, both 33, the lift alone 24, the paired measurement alone 73. ## When a deck change is adopted (`src/lib/evidence.ts`, `src/lib/deckSearch.ts`)

Until 2026-11-11 the forge, the optimizer and the Deck Lab tuner adopted a change on any measured gain. Most of those gains were noise (for example 76.3% → 77.2% over 224 games). Every builder now uses one shared rule, the same discipline as the bot-weight tuners (ROADMAP §7).

**Screening only nominates.** Candidates are still screened cheaply on the baseline's own games (same seeds, opponents and seats); with the forge's `--screen-rounds` (below) on a seed block of their own, each round against its own baseline. The best ones that beat the list by 2+ points become nominees (forge and Lab: 1, optimizer: 6 among those above the list). Screening never adopts anything, and its numbers are never reported as the gain.

**Confirmation is held out, paired and staged.** Each nominee then plays fresh seeds that screening never used, game for game against the current list (the same seed, opponent and seat for both). The evidence is the per-game difference in score, with an interval: not two win rates measured apart. The first stage is the builder's old confirmation size (forge 2 × `--games`, optimizer `--confirm`, Lab 2 × games per test). While the interval still straddles the bar, another stage of the same size is added, up to 4 stages.

**Three outcomes.**
- **adopted**: the lower bound of the held-out paired gain is above **1 point**, with at least **200** held-out games;
- **rejected**: the upper bound is below 1 point;
- **could not tell**: neither by the last stage. The change is **not** adopted, and reports say "could not tell" with the interval and the games played. It is never called a gain.

**The futility stop.** A change with no real gain can almost never be formally rejected at these sample sizes: the two lists play the same game about 90% of the time, so even at 896 games the interval is still about ±2.2 points around zero, and the nominee used to run to the cap and pay 4× the confirmation for nothing. So, after **2 stages**, a nominee that is neither adopted nor rejected and whose held-out paired mean gain is **0 points or less** stops there. It ends as **could not tell**, recorded with `stopped: 'futility'` and shown as "could not tell (stopped early: no gain after N games)"; its games and interval are the ones at that point. It never adopts anything, so it cannot raise the false-adopt rate, and the boundaries of the looks (below) are left as they are (the looks it saves are not spent back). With several nominees (the optimizer's 6, or `--nominees`) each one is stopped on its own; a later stage is played only while one is still live, the current list still plays it once, and every game played is the same game it was without the stop (same seeds). The legacy `any-gain` rule has no stop. On synthetic paired games (`tests/evidence.test.ts`, 1,000 runs each, off → on):

| Setting | Adopted | Mean games (both lists) | Stopped early |
|---|---|---|---|
| no gain, stages of 224, 90% same game | 0.1% → 0.1% | 1,684 → 1,238 | 57% |
| +1 point (at the bar), same | 1.0% → 1.0% | 1,765 → 1,588 | 21% |
| +5 points, same | 100% → 100% | 596 → 596 | 0% |
| no gain, stages of 140, 60% same game | 0.3% → 0.3% | 1,095 → 791 | 59% |
| +5 points, same | 37.8% → 37.7% | 997 → 970 | 5% |

The runs are coupled (the stop only skips later stages of the same random games), so every adoption with the stop is also an adoption without it.

**The correction for looking more than once.** Each look uses a wider interval than the plain 95% one. The nominees split the error rate α = 0.05 evenly (Bonferroni), so each nominee gets a one-sided α / (2 × nominees). Since 2026-11-12 (SIM-5) that share is spent over the planned looks by an O'Brien–Fleming-type spending function (Lan–DeMets; `src/lib/groupSequential.ts`), at equal information fractions 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 1: the early looks are strict and the last one is close to the unsplit bound. For one nominee and 4 looks the z's are 4.33, 2.96, 2.36, 2.01; for the optimizer's 6 nominees 5.61, 3.89, 3.11, 2.66. Before, α was split evenly over looks and nominees (Bonferroni), so every look used z = Φ⁻¹(1 − α / (2 × looks × nominees)): 2.50 for one nominee and 4 looks, 3.08 for 6 nominees; `--boundary bonferroni` (Lab: `boundary: 'bonferroni'`) keeps that rule for reproducing old runs, and the manifest's `adoption.correction` says which ran (`obrien-fleming`, `bonferroni`, or `none` for any-gain), with `adoption.lookZ` the z of each look. Either way, whatever stage a decision stops at, the chance of adopting a change whose true gain is 1 point or less stays below α / 2 = 2.5%. The bound assumes equal stages, which the builders always play. When several nominees are confirmed, the adopted one is the one with the highest corrected lower bound, and its held-out figure is the one reported.

| same | +1 point (the bar) | 1.1% → 2.1% | 1,577 → 1,585 |

### Pooling held-out games across runs (`src/lib/changeLedger.ts`, `scripts/ledger-store.ts`)

Until 2026-11-14 every forge, optimizer and season run started from nothing: a change that ended "could not tell" after 896 held-out games was forgotten, and the next run that nominated it measured it again from zero (the S2-05 swap −1 We Gotta Live Together → +1 Yorinobu was confirmed for 896 games in Season 2, screened again later, and is worth about +2 points on 2,800 games; docs/TUNING_STUDY_2026-11-13.md, cause 3). The change ledger keeps those games and pools them, so repeated runs converge on an answer. It is opt-in (`--ledger`); without it every line, report, file and manifest is exactly what it was.

**What the ledger keeps.** Every screening game and every held-out game a builder plays, one by one: the seed, the opponent (its list fingerprint), who went first, and both lists' scores. A change is named by the lists themselves, not by its label: the base list's fingerprint plus the cards cut and added, with repeats, over main deck and Legends. So the same change found as a card swap or as a package move is one change, a renamed list keeps its changes, and card order does not matter. Games are kept per **entry**: change + simulator version + bot (by the agent it builds: `fast` is Easy, `lookahead` and `best` are Medium) + field (a fingerprint of the opponents' lists). Games of another version, bot or field are another entry and are never pooled; `hard-v1` is refused, because it reads `src/data/impact.json`, so its games depend on more than the simulator version. A game is the same game when its seed, opponent and first player are the same; it is kept once per entry, and a game that comes back with other scores (it can happen when the same list is played with its cards in another order, since the shuffle starts from the array order) keeps the earlier scores and is counted as a conflict. **Screening games are kept but never pooled**: the screening winner reads 2 to 4 points too high (the winner's curse), so only held-out games are evidence. The Node store is `data/ledger/<base list fingerprint>.jsonl` (committed), one batch per line, append-only; a repeated run appends nothing. A game costs about 13 bytes, plus a few hundred bytes of header per batch (19 bytes a game in all on a real run's 1,344 games, whose screening batches are small).

**The rule, in plain words.**
1. A nominee with no earlier held-out games is confirmed exactly as before: the same games, the same looks, the same outcome.
2. A nominee with earlier held-out games is judged on them first, before any new game: if they already adopt or reject it under the current rule, it is decided with 0 new games. Otherwise it plays stages as before, and after each one the earlier and new games are pooled (each game once) and judged again, up to the usual 4 new stages, then "could not tell".
3. A pooled look uses an interval that stays valid however often it is looked at: the one-sided normal-mixture boundary of Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon (2021), "Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences", used with an estimated variance: z(n) = √( 2 · (1 + ρ/n) · ln( √(1 + n/ρ) / (2a) + 1 ) ), with n the pooled games, ρ = 250 games (`POOL_RHO`) and a = α / (2 × nominees), the same one-sided share the staged rule gives each nominee. For one nominee (a = 0.025): z = 3.77 at 224 games, 3.32 at 448, 3.11 at 896, 3.04 at 1,792, 3.06 at 3,584, 3.12 at 7,168. Adopt when the pooled lower bound is above the bar with at least 200 pooled games; reject when the pooled upper bound is below it.
4. The spread of a pooled look is estimated with two pseudo-games added, one +1 and one −1, so a swap that rarely changes a game never reads as having no spread at all: with n′ = n + 2 and m′ = Σd / n′, v = (Σd² + 2 − n′·m′²) / (n′ − 1), and the interval is the plain mean ± z(n) · √(v / n), clipped to [−1, 1]. The staged rule's own interval is unchanged.
5. The futility stop works on the pooled games: after a new stage other than the last (never before the first), a nominee whose pooled games reach 2 stages' worth and whose pooled mean gain is 0 or less stops as "could not tell". The Deck Lab's Stop and `--adopt any-gain` behave as before; any-gain never pools.
6. A screened change whose earlier held-out games alone already reject it is not nominated: the next best takes its place, and the log says so with the pooled figure.
7. The earlier games handed to a confirmation leave out every game it may play (all stages to the cap) and the screening games that nominated the change, so no game is counted twice, the screening that picked a change never vouches for it, and a repeat of the same single confirmation decides the same way and adds nothing. Within one run a later generation that nominates the same change again (the list was kept) pools the earlier generation's held-out games, as a later run would; so a repeat of a run of several generations finds those later games too and may decide its first generation on the pooled games (seen on a scripted two-generation tuning run: the first time generation 1 was a first confirmation on 896 games and generation 2 pooled 1,792; repeated on the same store, both generations judged the pooled 1,792, and nothing was added).
8. A game that has screened a change is never kept as held-out evidence for that change afterwards. Within one run screening and confirmation seeds never overlap, but two runs with different seeds can overlap, so a later run can play as a held-out game one that an earlier run screened the change on; the ledger does not store it as evidence, and the run's summary line counts the games left out. (In the run that plays it, such a game still counts once in that run's own decision. It needs two runs' seed ranges to overlap with the opponents in step, which is rare, and none has been seen.)

**Why not the plan's "next look".** The study's plan judged a pooled look as the next look index, that is at the last staged z (2.01) every time. For a change worth exactly the 1-point bar that adopts it more often with every re-run, because each re-run is another chance at the same z: 2.4% after 1 run, 8.2% after 5, 13.5% after 20 (SD 0.24, below). The owner chose the strict rule (2026-11-14), knowing it needs about 1.9 times the pooled games.

**What it guarantees and what it costs.** The first confirmation of a change can wrongly adopt it with probability at most α / 2 = 2.5% per decision, as before. All its pooled looks together, however many runs there are, add at most another α / 2, so a change's whole life stays under 5%; with the variance estimated that bound is approximate, and the simulation below measures it. ### A shortlist carried across runs (`src/lib/shortlist.ts`)

Until 2026-11-14 the forge and the Deck Lab tuner picked each run's candidates from that run's own 112 screening games of card statistics, which are mostly noise: five forge runs on one deck screened 37 to 39 different changes of 40, and five Lab runs nominated five different changes (docs/TUNING_STUDY_2026-11-13.md, causes 1 and 3). So a promising change was rarely tested twice, and the ledger rarely had anything to pool. The shortlist keeps a few promising changes from run to run and skips the ones earlier games already say are not worth it. It is opt-in and needs the ledger: `--pooled-ranking` on the forge (and the season's forge step), and in the Deck Lab it is on whenever "Use earlier held-out games" is. Without it every line, report, file and manifest is exactly what it was, with or without `--ledger` (checked on a real forge run against the commit before; the Lab's pins in `tests/deck-lab-ledger.test.ts` are unchanged).

**The rule, in plain words.** Everything is per ledger entry: this base list, simulator version, bot and field.
1. A change's **pooled games** are its screening and held-out games in the ledger, each game once (the same seed, opponent and first player is one game). Its **pooled gain** is the mean per-game difference over them, with the plain standard error.
2. A change is **dropped** when (a) its pooled gain plus half a standard error is below the nomination bar (2 points: the forge's, and the Lab's `minGain`), (b) its held-out games alone are rejected by the strict pooling rule, or (c) it has held-out games and would need more than 10,000 held-out games in all (the **reach**) to be adopted at their present mean and spread (`gamesToSettle`). Held-out games that already adopt a change never put it out of reach.
3. A change is **carried** when it has pooled games, is not dropped, is a one-card swap (one main-deck card out, one in) and still makes a legal list from the current base list that the package plan allows. The best 2 by pooled gain are carried (ties by change key); the list is rebuilt from the change key the way the generator builds it, so a carried change plays the same games it played before.
4. Each generation screens the carried changes first, then the generator's picks in its own order (today's ranking on this generation's telemetry), skipping carried and dropped ones, until `--cands` in all. The generator now offers its whole order (up to 3 cuts × 6 adds), not only its first `--cands`; when it runs out, fewer are screened and the log says so. Package moves and combined candidates are made as before and are neither carried nor skipped.
5. Screening is as before: every candidate plays this generation's games, and they are recorded.
6. **Nomination** is by pooled gain over all of a candidate's pooled games, this generation's screening included, best first, for every kind of candidate, at the same 2-point bar. A candidate with no earlier games keeps exactly its own screening figure, so it is judged as before. A candidate is not nominated when its earlier held-out games reject it (P1's check) or put it out of reach (c).
7. Confirmation is P1's, unchanged. The same ledger state, seeds and flags make the same candidates and the same decisions.

**Why a light carry and not a fixed set.** The study's plan said to keep the candidate set fixed across runs. Under P1's strict rule that does worse than today: a fresh candidate is cheaper to settle than a doubtful old one, and a fixed set keeps paying for old ones. Synthetic runs (`npx tsx scripts/shortlist-sim.ts --lives 4000 --seed 11`: 4,000 lists a row, 10 runs each, a pool of 60 candidate one-card swaps per list whose true effects follow the study's E3 measurements, mean about +1.0 and SD 1.3 points; 5 candidates a run on 112 screening games, stages of 224 up to 4, paired SD 0.24; a new pool after each adoption; every rule through the library's functions):

| Policy | Adoptions per list | True gain adopted per list | Wrong (true gain under +1) | Games per list | Gain per 10,000 games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### Until settled, and what a run can detect (`src/lib/evidence.ts`, `src/lib/deckSearch.ts`)

Under the strict rule a confirmation that ends "could not tell" after its 4 stages is usually a real change the run was too small to settle: a first confirmation adopts a +2-point card change only about 1 time in 4 (docs/TUNING_STUDY_2026-11-13.md, "Power"). On real games the shortlist kept two changes really worth +3.4 and +4.0 points for three runs without settling them (above). "Until settled" (plan P4, built 2026-11-15) lets such a confirmation go on in the same run instead of stopping at the cap. It is opt-in on the forge and the optimizer (`--until-settled`), on by default in the season since 2026-11-22 (`--no-until-settled` turns it off; it passes it to its forge step) and on by default in the Deck Lab ("Keep confirming until settled"). Without it every line, report, file and manifest is exactly what it was, with or without `--ledger` and `--pooled-ranking`; the one new line is what a run can detect (below).

**The rule, in plain words.**
1. A nominee is confirmed exactly as before up to the cap: the same stages, looks, futility stop and outcome. Only a nominee still undecided after its last capped stage goes on; one decided earlier, or stopped early for no gain, does not.
2. It goes on in further stages of the same size, on fresh seeds that follow the capped ones. Its staged test is over at the cap: from then on only the strict pooled rule looks. After each further stage all its held-out games (from earlier runs with the ledger, the capped stages and the further ones, each game once) are judged by `judgePooled`, exactly as a later run would judge them. This holds for a first confirmation too.
3. It stops when that look adopts or rejects it; when the futility rule fires on the pooled mean (pooled mean gain 0 or less once it has 2 stages' worth of games, as on the pooled path); when it is **out of reach**, that is, it would need more than the reach (10,000 held-out games in all, the shortlist's reach: one default, `DEFAULT_REACH`, and one `--reach` flag for both) to be adopted at its present mean and spread (`gamesToSettle`, checked before each further stage, so a change already out of reach at the cap plays nothing more); when this run's limit is reached; or on Stop (the Deck Lab).
4. **This run's limit** is the seeds the builder has for a generation's confirmation, and the reach: the forge's 50,000 confirmation seeds a generation (stopping before any seed the next generation of the same deck will use, its screening block or its confirmation block, whose first seed is known in advance), the optimizer's 50,000 seeds after its first confirmation seed, and the Deck Lab's 5,000 (about 4,900 held-out games a confirmation in one run; what is left is for a later run, when the shortlist carries the change). A further stage never plays a seed twice. Within the reach, the forge's default stages of 280 allow up to 32 further stages, the Lab's Standard 18.
5. Each nominee goes on by itself: a further stage plays the current list once with the nominees still going, and the others are not replayed. With the ledger every further stage is recorded as held-out games, and the earlier games handed to a confirmation leave out every game it may play up to its limit, so a repeat of a run decides the same way and adds nothing. Without the ledger the pooled rule sees this run's own games only.
6. The record says what happened in plain words: how many further stages, and why it stopped: adopted or rejected ("confirmed until settled: 6 more stages"), out of reach (with the games it would need in all, said as "more than 100,000" past ten times the reach, since a mean barely above the bar asks for hundreds of millions), stopped for no gain, this run's limit (with about how many more held-out games may settle it), or Stop. With one or more further stages the reported held-out gain is the pooled always-valid interval over all its games. `Confirmation.settle` and `NomineeRecord.settle` (optional: `stages`, `ended`, `toSettle`, `reach`) carry it into the forge and optimizer logs and JSON (with a top-level `untilSettled`: the reach, the most further stages and what they may add; and per forge generation `settleStages`), the Lab's nominee table and generation summary (a wrapping note), its saved notes, and the saved tuned record (`changes[].heldOut.settle`, which the Tuned decks page shows beside the held-out figure); the manifest's `adoption.untilSettled` records the reach.

**Error accounting is unchanged.** The first confirmation can wrongly adopt a change worth exactly the bar with probability at most α / 2 = 2.5% per decision; every pooled look after it, in this run's further stages and in every later run, shares the strict rule's other α / 2, whose always-valid boundary holds however often it is looked at. So a change's whole life stays under α = 5% (approximately, as the spread is estimated). On synthetic paired games (20,000 confirmations each, a change exactly at the +1-point bar, stages of 224, the default rule, "until settled" to the reach with no seed limit) it was adopted in 2.34% → 2.54% of confirmations at SD 0.24 (off → on), 2.52% → 2.69% at SD 0.35, 2.53% → 2.77% at SD 0.50, and 2.12% → 2.36% with stages of 112. `tests/until-settled.test.ts` checks the first case on 3,000 seeded confirmations against the 2.5% and 5% bounds.

**What a run can detect.** Before a run starts the forge and the optimizer print one line (in their output, and in the log or report under the rule line) and the Deck Lab shows it under Search depth, for example: "A first confirmation here adopts a real gain of +3.8 points 8 times in 10 (+6.2 for a package move); with until settled, about +3.0 (+4.8 for a package move) within 10,000 held-out games. (the wording of 2026-11-15; since 2026-11-24 the default one-card spread is 0.256 and the line names where each default comes from)
- `detectable()` (`src/lib/deckSearch.ts`) is the smallest real gain a first confirmation adopts 8 times in 10, for the stage size, cap, per-game SD, rule and nominees. It is computed, not simulated: under the normal model of the paired differences the density of the running sum over the still-undecided region is carried from look to look by a Gaussian convolution on a grid (as the O'Brien–Fleming boundaries are), following the rule look by look: the staged z's, the 200-game floor, rejection and the futility stop. `tests/detectable.test.ts` checks it against a seeded Monte-Carlo of `confirmStaged` itself (2,000 confirmations a point) for the Lab's three efforts and the forge's default: in each, fewer than 8 in 10 adopt at 0.25 points below it and more than 8 in 10 at 0.25 points above.
- With "until settled" on, the line adds the gain adopted 8 times in 10 within the reach, from the same computation carried on past the cap with the pooled boundary, the futility stop and the out-of-reach stop. It is approximate: the true spread stands in for the guarded estimate; "out of reach" is read as a bound on the mean (what `gamesToSettle` decides when the spread is the true one); and one run's seed limit is ignored, as if a later run carried the change on with nothing in between (in the Lab one run gives a confirmation about 4,900 held-out games). The test checks it against the Monte-Carlo too, within 0.25 points.
- `gamesFor()` gives the held-out games (to the cap) a first confirmation needs to adopt a given real gain 8 times in 10: at SD 0.30, 1,804 for +3 points, 7,204 for +2 and 28,812 for +1.5 (at SD 0.24: 1,156, 4,612 and 18,440).

With one run's limit in the Lab applied (about 4,900 held-out games, which the figure ignores) the largest difference is 2.8 points, and 79% to 80% are still adopted at the printed gain.
- Two cases the line words differently: under `--adopt any-gain` it adds that a change with no real gain at all is adopted about half the time too (that rule adopts a small gain easily, which says nothing about its worth), and a run too small to adopt anything (its cap under the 200-game floor) reads "does not adopt even a real gain of +60 points 8 times in 10".

### By opponent, and re-measuring a change (`src/lib/pairedSplit.ts`, `scripts/remeasure.ts`)

The tools screen and confirm with the Medium bot; the recorded events are played by Hard. Two things help tell how far a measured change carries (plan P6, first half, 2026-11-15). Neither touches a decision: `confirmStaged`, `judgePooled` and every rule above are unchanged, and so are every candidate, list, log line and pinned hash.

**By opponent and by seat.** Each nominee the forge, the optimizer or the Deck Lab confirms now keeps its held-out games by opponent (list fingerprint) and by seat (`splitPaired`), and the χ² test of whether the per-opponent figures differ by more than chance (`heterogeneity`: the means around their precision-weighted mean, opponents with 20 or more games, Wilson–Hilferty p-value; the evidence builder's test, moved here unchanged). With earlier runs' games pooled in (the ledger), the split covers all the held-out games the figure covers, the earlier ones first (`HeldOutTracker`, `priorGames`). The change ledger's own summary (`summarize`) is the same split over its games, so there is one definition.
- Where it shows: the forge's and optimizer's JSON (`nominees[].byOpp`, `bySeat`, `heterogeneity`); one line in their log, report and console for a nominee whose p-value is under 0.05, for example "depends on the opponent: +9.1 against RRG Dexter Midrange, −3.0 against YYB Rogue Gear (χ² 18.3 on 6 df, p 0.006)" (the two extremes); the Deck Lab's nominee table, the same line under the held-out gain, only when it is flagged (the split is kept beside the logs, `TuneProgress.splits`, so the logs and their pinned hashes are as before); and the evidence page, where a forge finding takes the split and the `depends-on-opponent` flag from the report (older reports have none, and their findings are unchanged).
- On the forge's layout every opponent gets the same games on each seat, so a pooled figure that depends on the opponent is an average over matchups that differ; the line says so rather than letting the one number stand for all of them. With 7 opponents and about 5% of null changes flagged by chance, a flag on a single nominee is a reason to look, not a finding by itself.
- `stratifiedInterval` gives the paired mean with a standard error from the opponent × seat cells (each cell weighted by its share of the games, so the estimate is exactly the pooled mean; the variance Σ w²·s²/n, a one-game cell taking the pooled variance). Where the cells differ it is narrower than the plain interval; where they do not it is about as wide (a little wider, by the cells' own degrees of freedom). It is printed beside the plain interval for reading; no rule uses it.

**Re-measuring a change: `npm run remeasure`.** One change to a library deck on fresh paired games, by default with Hard: `npm run remeasure -- <deck> --cut <card> --add <card>` (or `--change "<label or change key>"` from the ledger, or `--before <deck file>` for the change that made a list), `--bot`, `--games` (default 1,120, a multiple of 14), `--seed` (default 5,000,000), `--threads`, `--cache`. The layout is the forge's (game k plays meta deck k mod 7 with seed `seed + k`, the seat alternating every 7 games), so games already played this way are found in the game cache. It prints the paired difference with its plain and stratified intervals, the per-game spread, the share of games that came out the same, both win rates, the per-opponent and per-seat figures and the χ² test, and appends one row to `data/evidence/` for the evidence builder (`--dry-run` writes nothing). docs/EVIDENCE.md "Making one" has the details; the evidence page then names each bot of a change measured with two, and says in one sentence what the Hard measurement shows about the Medium one, from their strength words and ranges only.

**What Hard says about changes measured with Medium (2026-11-15; `reports/tuning-study-2026-11-13/p6-hard.ts`, `p6-transfer.ts`, record in `p6-transfer.txt`).** The changes, the game counts and the analysis were written into the two scripts before any Hard game was played. Thirteen changes, one per card added per deck (changes that add the same card to the same list are nearly one experiment): three each on S2-05 and S2-08, and the seven recorded changes of experiment E2. Each got 560 paired Hard games on the forge's layout, and each has its Medium figure on 2,800 games under the same simulator version. The change that read highest with Medium on S2-05 and on S2-08 was then taken to 2,800 Hard games. 21,280 Hard games and 39,200 Medium, 65 minutes on 12 threads; a Hard game took about 20 times as long as a Medium one.
- **Hard shows most of a Medium gain.** Mean gain over the 13 changes: +2.80 points with Medium, +2.10 with Hard. The ratio of the summed gains is 0.75 ± 0.19 (95% from 0.40 to 1.07 by a bootstrap over the changes), and the slope of Hard on Medium through the origin is 0.81 ± 0.15. 11 of the 13 Hard figures have Medium's sign, and the changes do not differ in how much carries over by more than chance (χ² 11.8 on 12 degrees of freedom). The study's earlier "about half survives" rested on three changes at 560 games under version 8; this replaces it, and its own range still includes both a half and all of it.
- **One Hard measurement of 560 games settles little.** 4 of the 13 Hard ranges exclude zero, against 12 of the 13 Medium ranges at 2,800 games. The per-game spread is larger with Hard for every one of the 13 changes (for a one-card swap a median of 0.34 against 0.30 with Medium on the same changes, a ratio of 1.18), so the same range takes about 1.4 times the games with Hard, and a plain range of ±1 point about 4,500 paired games. With the smaller gain, seeing a change with Hard takes about 2.5 times the games it takes with Medium, at about 20 times the time per game. (As first written on 2026-11-15 this bullet said the spread was the same with both bots: it had compared the medians of two different sets of changes.)
- **The two taken to 2,800 Hard games.** S2-08, −1 T-Bug → +1 Rockn' Rockerboy: +3.5 points [+2.3, +4.7] with Hard (+4.7 with Medium), clear of the tools' 1-point bar. S2-05, −1 Gunpoint Diplomacy → +1 Yorinobu: +1.4 [+0.3, +2.4] with Hard (+2.9 with Medium), a gain that is not shown to clear the bar.
- **Packages.** Only two package swaps are in the set (S2-03: +5.4 with Hard against +5.8; S2-04: +0.9 against +4.8; both at 560 Hard games), too few to say whether packages carry over less than single cards.
- **On the evidence page** the Hard figures are re-measurement rows (`data/evidence/remeasure-2026-11-15-p6.json`), and a Medium finding now says what Hard shows for the same change instead of losing its caveat (docs/EVIDENCE.md "Changes"): 12 read "Hard agrees", 15 "Hard not settled", 1 "Hard decides", and 19 are still "Medium only". In the overview a change is named by its Hard measurement when that one is strong or moderate.

### Confirming with another bot (`--confirm-bot`, `TuneOptions.confirmBot`)

The tools pick changes with the Medium bot and, by default, confirm them with it too; the recorded events are played by Hard, and a gain measured with Medium shows at about 0.75 of its size with Hard (above). The forge's `--confirm-bot <bot>` (passed on by `scripts/season.ts`) and the Deck Lab's **Confirm with the Hard bot** (`TuneOptions.confirmBot`; offered for a Medium run, off by default) keep the cheap part with the screening bot and play the part that decides with another (plan P6, second half, 2026-11-15).

**What it does.** Each generation's baseline, its screening and their telemetry stay with the screening bot (`--bot`). The confirmation stages (the current list and the nominees, paired), "until settled"'s further stages and, in the Lab, the comparison of the tuned list with the original are played by the confirming bot. The seeds, the layout, the rule and the stage sizes are the same, so a confirmation is the same deals with another pilot. A confirming bot equal to the screening bot (by canonical name: `lookahead` is Medium) is no option at all: every game, line and file is what it was, and nothing names a bot (checked on real games against the commit before, and in `tests/forge-confirm-bot.test.ts` and `tests/confirm-bot.test.ts`).

**What it costs.** The games are counted as before; one more sentence beside the estimate says how many of them at most are the confirming bot's, and for Hard after Medium that a Hard game took about `HARD_TIME_RATIO` times as long as a Medium one when measured (20 on 2026-11-15; 25 since 2026-11-24, measured 25 to 30). In a one-generation forge run on S2-08 (`--games 28 --cands 3`, 8 threads) 112 Medium games and 448 Hard ones took 2 minutes 17 seconds. Hard's per-game spread is larger (0.34 for a one-card swap and 0.60 for a package move, measured on 11 one-card swaps and 2 package swaps; Medium's 0.30 and 0.55; since 2026-11-24 the defaults are the ledger's medians, `HARD_SD` 0.295 and `DEFAULT_SD` 0.256 for a one-card swap, packages unchanged, so the figures in this paragraph are of 2026-11-15), so a confirmation with Hard needs a larger gain to adopt: at the forge's default stages (280) a first confirmation adopts a one-card change 8 times in 10 at +3.9 points with Hard against +3.5 with Medium (+3.2 against +2.9 until settled), and at the Lab's Standard stages (224) +4.2 against +3.8. Since a Medium gain shows at about 0.75 of its size with Hard, the same change also reads smaller there. The detectability line says all of this when the bots differ: it names the confirming bot, uses its spread (its own ledger changes when the list has enough, else the defaults above) and adds the 0.75.

**When it is worth it.** When the change will be judged by recorded events, which Hard plays: a change confirmed with Hard is measured on the bot the deck's results come from, so its held-out figure needs no translation. It is not worth it for a first sweep over many candidates (screening stays cheap either way, but every confirmation stage costs about 25 times as much), and it adopts less often for the same real gain.

**With the ledger.** Screening batches go to the screening bot's entry and held-out batches to the confirming bot's; the priors, the pooled judgement and "until settled" read the confirming bot's entry only, so games of two bots never pool. A deal is the same deal whichever bot plays it, so the rule that a game that screened a change is never held-out evidence for it holds across the two bots: a held-out game whose slot (seed, opponent, seat) screened the change with the other bot is left out when it arrives (`LedgerCache.append`), and one already kept is left out of a prior once the other bot has screened that slot (`ChangeLedgerRun.prior`). With one bot's entries only, as in every run before this option, both are what they were. The Lab's two entries share one run id and one total.

**What is refused.** `--pooled-ranking` with a confirming bot other than the screening bot (exit 2, one sentence, `confirmBotProblem`): the shortlist would rank two bots' figures in one list, a change with earlier held-out games by the confirming bot's and one without by the screening bot's. (It was allowed from 2026-11-22 to 11-24, when this document still said it was refused; REVIEW_2026-11-24 §7.) In the Lab the shortlist is simply off for such a run, and the line under "Use earlier held-out games" says so. `--ledger` and `--until-settled` work with it; a confirming bot the ledger cannot use (`hard-v1`) is refused with `--ledger`.

**What says so.** Only when the bots differ: the rule line (`describeRule`, "…; screened with the Medium bot, confirmed with the Hard bot"), hence the forge's log, report, console and summary and the deck file's `// Adoption:` line; the forge JSON's and the run manifest's `adoption.confirmBot` (optional, schema 1) and the JSON's `ledger.heldoutBot`; in the Lab the estimate, the detectability line, the confirmation caption ("played by the Hard bot"), the rule line, the saved tuned record (`confirmBot`, and `rule.confirmBot`) and the Tuned decks page. On the evidence page a forge finding's bot is the report's confirming bot, and a run (forge report or ledger run) names both bots. A change the ledger holds under two bots, screened by one and confirmed by the other, is one finding: the confirming bot's held-out games, with the other bot's screening figure beside it, named by that bot ("Screening with Medium read +7.1 pts over 28 games: not the result."). It is one open question at most, the confirming bot's, which reads the held-out games alone and says who screened it; the screening bot's entry raises no "only screened" question of its own, and a run's trial gives no figure over all its games where two bots played them (docs/EVIDENCE.md "Changes").

**Checked on real games.** A one-generation forge run on S2-08 with `--confirm-bot hard --ledger` kept its screening batches under Medium and four held-out stages of 56 games under Hard; `npm run remeasure` on the same seeds gives the run's held-out figure exactly with Hard (+1.8 points over 224 games, 43.3% against 45.1%) and +0.0 with Medium (41.5% both). A Quick Deck Lab run in a browser (BBG Cunningham Econ against the meta, Hard confirming, 448 Hard games in about a minute and a half) kept 224 screening games that replay exactly with Medium in Node, and 224 held-out games that replay exactly with Hard and not with Medium (65 and 67 of each stage's 112 games the same).

### A bred deck's test against its parent (`src/lib/breedGate.ts`, `scripts/season.ts`, `npm run gate`)

The tuning study found that bred decks rate 11.5 points below the mean of their parents under the Hard bot, and that nothing tested a bred deck before it took a season slot (docs/TUNING_STUDY_2026-11-13.md, cause 7 and plan P7). The gate is that test. Its rule was written down before any game was played.

**The reference.** By default a season tests every bred deck against the middle meta deck by strength (the 4th of the 7, best first): one fixed bar for all, recorded as `fixed-bar` for a deck with parents and `explorer` for one without. With `--gate-reference parent` each deck is tested against its best parent instead, of the parents the breeder names the one ranked highest by strength; an explorer, which has none, still plays the bar, and so does a deck none of whose parents is in the ranked list (recorded as `no-parent-available`). The record keeps the mode in `gate.settings.reference`.

**Why the bar was the default (2026-11-15 to 2026-11-24; since then the parent, with Hard alone deciding: below).** The plan declared the parent as the reference, and the gate was built that way. The check on the recorded bred decks below found that with the parent as reference 2 of the 16 would have entered and the five best-rated bred decks would have been kept out (the best, S1-01 Mutant, rated fourth of 47, is shown worse than its parent, the top deck); against the bar 9 enter, those five among them. The owner chose the bar as the season's default on 2026-11-15. The Deck Lab always tests a bred deck against its best parent, because that comparison is its purpose (`chooseReference` and `planGate` keep the parent as their own default for it), and `npm run gate` names its reference.

**Checked on the recorded bred decks (2026-11-15; `reports/tuning-study-2026-11-13/p7-validate.txt`).** The plan and its decision rules were written before the games (`p7-plan.txt`). The 16 decks bred in Seasons 1 and 2 each played the reference the rule as first built gives them (their best parent by today's ratings; an explorer, the middle meta deck), every stage, with both bots: 20,200 paired games with Medium and 8,080 with Hard. The decks whose reference was another deck then played the fixed bar the same way.
- The tool itself: the gate's first stage for one pair is exactly the 560 games `npm run remeasure` plays for the same lists, seeds and bot, with the same figures by opponent and by seat.
- Medium keeps out 11 of the 16 and Hard 3 of the other 5, so two enter. The gate's differences follow the ratings (rank correlation 0.90 with Medium, 0.81 with Hard), and all 10 decks rated 10 or more points below their reference are kept out. The staged looks used 49% of the Medium games.
- The parent rule keeps out the strong bred decks. The five best rated, from S1-01 Mutant (69.6%, fourth of the 47 decks) to S2-04 Cross (55.6%), each have a reference rated 69.6% or more and are shown worse than it; the two that enter are rated 47.7% and 51.6% and have weak references. Against the fixed bar, the middle meta deck (rated 53.1%, also the 24th of the 47), nine enter, those five among them, and six of the seven decks rated under 48% are kept out. That is why `--gate-reference` exists; the owner chose the bar as the default on 2026-11-15.
- The two bots disagree deck by deck. Hard minus Medium has a standard deviation of 8.2 points over the 16 decks where game noise alone gives 3.2 (4.5 against 3.3 when every deck plays the same bar), and Medium is the kinder one to a bred deck by about 3 points on average: three of the five decks it let through are shown worse by Hard. By the conditions fixed in advance, Medium first and Hard second stays the default. Hard alone is `--gate-bot hard --gate-confirm-bot none`: at most 2,800 Hard games a deck with its reference, about 22,400 for a season of eight before early stops and shared references cut it.
- A trial of Season 3 at the real sizes (`--gate-only`, nothing recorded) gave the same picture: with the parent as reference 2 of the 8 bred decks enter, the two explorers, in about 7 minutes on 12 threads; against the fixed bar 6 of 8 enter, in about 12 minutes. Two of its decks' figures replay exactly from the lists in its record.

**The games.** The field is the meta decks other than the reference (and other than the deck). The bred deck and its reference play the same games, the forge's layout: game k plays field deck k mod n with seed seed0 + k, the deck going first when floor(k / n) is even. The measurement is the score difference per game, bred deck minus reference. A stage is 20 double rounds: each field deck, each seat, 20 times, so 280 games with 7 opponents and 240 with 6.

**The rule.** The first bot (Medium by default) plays up to 5 stages. After each stage the difference so far is judged by the forge's staged machinery (`confirmStaged`) with the bar at 0 points, α = 0.05 spent over the looks by the O'Brien–Fleming-type boundary (z 4.88, 3.36, 2.68, 2.29, 2.03 for five looks), no futility stop and no minimum beyond the first stage:
- **shown worse** when the upper bound is below 0;
- **shown better** when the lower bound is above 0;
- **could not tell** at the cap.

The futility stop is off on purpose: a deck with no gain has to stay in the test, because "could not tell" enters. A deck the first bot did not show worse then plays the confirming bot (Hard by default), up to 2 stages (z 2.96, 1.97) on its own seeds, by the same rule. With `none`, or a confirming bot that is the first bot under another name (`lookahead` is Medium), only the first bot plays.

**Entry.** A bred deck enters unless one of the two bots shows it worse. "Could not tell" enters. The words never say an entered deck is as good as its reference, only that it was not shown worse. Each bred deck is its own decision: α is not split between the eight decks of a season. A reference that several decks share plays each block of games once.

**Entry since 2026-11-24: only the confirming bot keeps a deck out** (docs/REVIEW_2026-11-24.md B.6, row 14; `GATE_DEFAULTS.keepOut` `'confirming'`). Medium under-rates some kinds of deck that Hard rates well (the BBG control family by about 11 points, with a split-half reliability of 0.87), and until then a deck Medium showed worse was kept out without Hard ever playing it. Now every deck plays Hard's stages, also one Medium showed worse, and a deck is kept out only when Hard shows it worse; "could not tell" by Hard enters, and Medium's verdict is kept in the record and the words as information ("entered: not shown worse than X by the Hard bot, which alone can keep a deck out"). The guarantee becomes about 2.5% or less in all, from Hard alone. The cost: Hard plays for every deck, at most 2 stages each; for 8 decks against the fixed bar on the 6 other meta decks, at most 10,800 Medium and 4,320 Hard games (`gatePlanCost`, printed before the gate plays; the season-3 gate played about 9,100 and 3,840). `--gate-keep-out either` (npm run gate: `--keep-out either`) is the rule of 2026-11-15; with no confirming bot the first bot decides either way; a record without `keepOut` ran `'either'`.

**Reference and field, since 2026-11-24** (the owner's choices of the same day). The season's default reference is the best parent again (`--gate-reference parent`, "Parent, with Hard": a bred deck must not be shown worse than its stronger parent, and with the rule above only Hard can show it so); an explorer, which has no parent, plays the middle meta deck. `middle` is the fixed bar of 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-24, and `median` tests every bred deck against the library deck at the median by rating (parent strength's order, `medianLibrary`). The season's default field is the 7 meta decks and the library's top 7 lists by rating (`--field-top 7`, `SEASON_FIELD_TOP`, "Meta 7 + top 7"; `src/lib/tuningField.ts`), one entry per list (a list already in the field is passed over), for the screen, the gate and the forge step (the forge takes the same `--field-top N`; its own default stays 0). The change ledger keys an entry on the field's lists, so the wider field starts new ledger entries: its games never pool with games against the 7 meta decks. A stage holds 2 × rounds × opponents games, so the season's default rounds halve on the 14 decks (`seasonGateRounds`: 10, a stage of 260 games against 13 opponents or 280 against 14, as 20 rounds were on the meta decks), and the gate detects what it did. The forge's games per test, its `--forge-budget` check and until settled's cap do not depend on the field, so a default season passes its budget checks as before.

**What a season costs with the defaults of 2026-11-24** (at most; the review's §4 figures beside them). Gate: 14,300 to 18,200 Medium and 5,720 to 7,280 Hard games for the bred decks of Seasons 4 to 7 as bred today (8 decks with 3 to 6 distinct parents; each reference's blocks are played once), against about 9,100 Medium and 3,840 Hard that Season 3 played against one fixed bar (at most 10,800 and 4,320); the parent reference, not the field, is what costs more, and Hard now plays for every deck. Forge 6,720 Hard + 4,032 Medium, round robin 5,040 Hard, finals 1,764 Hard and the breed-many screen (off) unchanged. In Hard-game equivalents (25 Medium games to one Hard), the gate's most goes from about 4,200 to 6,300–8,000, and a season's from about 18,000 to about 20,000–22,000.

**What it guarantees.** A bred deck that is truly no worse than its reference is kept out about 2.5% of the time or less by each bot (one side of α = 0.05), so about 5% or less by the two together. "About", because the boundaries are the normal approximation: by simulation with no real difference it came up 2.3% to 2.8% of the time (2.52% over 5 looks of 280 and 2.55% over 2 looks of 280, 2.32% and 2.58% for stages of 240, 40,000 runs each with paired differences of −1, 0 and +1; 2.6% to 2.8% with independent win/loss games at base rates of 30% to 70%, 16,000 runs each; `tests/breed-gate.test.ts` runs 6,000 of the first kind and 10,000 at a 30% base rate). "Or less", because a deck that is truly better than its reference is kept out less often.

**What it can detect.** Printed before it plays, each bot at its own per-game spread (`GATE_SD`, `gateSd` by canonical bot name): 0.59 with Medium and 0.60 with Hard, the medians measured on the 16 recorded bred decks with every stage played (2026-11-15, reports/tuning-study-2026-11-13/p7-validate.txt; Medium 0.55 to 0.63, Hard 0.58 to 0.65). They replaced the 0.70 assumed before the games, as declared in advance (the measured median replaces the assumption when they differ by more than 0.05); a bot that was not measured takes Hard's 0.60, and the sentence says so. With 7 opponents the Medium bot shows a deck worse 8 times in 10 when it is really 4.5 points worse, and the Hard bot, with its 2 stages, when it is 7.1 points worse (4.8 and 7.7 with 6 opponents). By symmetry this is `detectable()` (src/lib/deckSearch.ts) for a gain at bar 0; a Monte-Carlo of the rule agrees (`tests/breed-gate.test.ts`, within 0.25 points). After the games the spread actually seen is printed too, as the median over the decks.

**What it costs.** At most 5 × 280 = 1,400 games for the deck and 1,400 for its reference with Medium, and 2 × 280 = 560 each with Hard, per bred deck; less when a deck is decided early, and a shared reference plays its games once (against the fixed bar every deck shares one, so a season plays the bar's blocks once). For a season of 8 decks that is at most about 22,400 Medium games and 9,000 Hard ones, the study's estimate (about 35 minutes).

**Seeds, and what the games are for.** A season's gate plays from seed S·1,000,000 + 700,000 with the first bot and + 800,000 with the confirming bot; `npm run gate` from 7,000,000 and 7,100,000. Gate games are a selection step: they count for no rating, leaderboard, card impact, change ledger or evidence file.

**The season (`scripts/season.ts`).** The new order is: breed 8 decks (or more, screened: below), gate them, write the deck files of the decks that enter, forge them, round robin, finals, leaderboard. The round robin now comes after the forge, so the round robin, the finals and the leaderboard all measure the forged list; before, the leaderboard pooled a forged deck's round robin from before the forge with its finals from after it. The per-list card impact from the round robin is keyed by the list that played, which is the forged one.
- Flags: `--gate on|off` (default on; off enters every bred deck as before), `--gate-bot` (medium), `--gate-confirm-bot` (hard, or none), `--gate-rounds` (the rounds that keep a stage near 280 games: 10 on the default field, 20 on the meta decks alone), `--gate-stages` (5), `--gate-confirm-stages` (2), `--gate-reference` (parent since 2026-11-24, middle or median), `--gate-keep-out` (confirming, or either), `--field-top` (7 since 2026-11-24), `--retire` (on) and `--retire-earlier` (off). Bad values exit 2, as do seed blocks that would overlap or reach the next season's seeds.
- Breeding more than the slots (2026-11-22, off by default): with `--breed-count N` (default 0: breed exactly 8; screening needs more than 8) the season breeds N, screens them (`--screen-games N`, 70 Medium games each against the meta decks, from seed S·1,000,000 + 600,000) and gates the best 8 (`pickScreened`). When the gate keeps decks out, the open slots go to the next runners-up by the screen, as many as there are open slots, each gated the same way on the same games before it takes a slot (`gateSlots`, round by round; a shared reference's blocks are played once across the rounds), until the slots are full or the runners-up run out. Until 2026-11-24 the runners-up entered ungated. The gate record lists every deck tested, a runner-up with `refill` (its round), and `refills`; a runner-up kept out is handled like any deck kept out. The screen's gain has not been measured.
- Counters: a counter is bred from the decks with the best shrunk head-to-head estimates against the top 4, and those estimates today follow the ratings (rank correlation 0.995 to 0.999 on 2026-11-24), so a counter is in effect a crossover of the top decks. The words say so (`COUNTER_NOTE`, `BRED_METHOD_WORDS` in `src/lib/bredDecks.ts`). The counter slots were kept; dropping them is the owner's call.
- A deck that does not enter gets no deck file. Its list (official export text), Legends, reference, figures and verdict are kept in `data/seasons/season-N.json` under `gate.decks`, beside the decks that entered, each with its bot, seeds, games, per-opponent and per-seat figures, and the simulator version (`gate.simVersion`), so a later re-measurement can show drift. `bred` keeps the shape it had and lists the decks that entered.
- The console and the report say "8 bred, M entered" and why each deck kept out was kept out. The season runs with fewer than 8 entered, or none (then nothing is forged).
- The Legend trios of decks kept out in earlier seasons count as seen when later seasons breed (`keptOutTriples`, scripts/breed-data.ts), so the breeder does not build the same trio again. Since 2026-11-24 only under the simulator version the season ran on (its gate record's `simVersion`, else its manifest's): one 40-card fill's result on one version is no reason to close a trio for good, so a bump opens them again (`expiredTriples` counts them, and the season prints how many).
- The breeding policy since 2026-11-24 (`BREED_POLICY` in `src/ai/breeder.ts`; docs/REVIEW_2026-11-24.md B.8, finding R7), passed by the season, `scripts/breed-preview.ts` and the Deck Lab (without it `breedSeason` breeds exactly as before, pinned in `tests/deck-packages-defaults.test.ts`):
  - Legends are scored by their marginal effect (`legendEffects`: a ridge regression of the ranked decks' strength on Legend indicators, penalty `LEGEND_RIDGE` 2, the old score's K): the three Legends of a deck are fitted together, so a Legend is not credited with its partners. On 2026-11-24's ratings (71 decks), marginal against the old average score: Smasher 54.5 (55.2), V: Streetkid 54.2 (55.7), Jackie Welles: Pour One Out (the BBG lists) 53.6 (49.6), Padre 52.4 (55.3), Dexter 51.9 (51.5), Saburo 51.4 (55.2), Viktor Vektor 51.3 (49.5), Hanako 50.9 (50.6), an untested Legend 50.0 (50.0, now below Dexter), River Ward 45.3 (44.7), Dum Dum 44.4 (44.2), Alt Cunningham 42.7 (43.9), Wakako 41.6 (44.5). `npx tsx scripts/breed-preview.ts --legend-scores` prints the table.
  - Two colours at most for every method (`maxColors` 2), not only explorers: 6 of the 22 bred decks of Seasons 1 to 3 were three-colour, all rated 48.6 or below.
  - What it changed, without a game (40 breeding seeds of Season 4 on the same inputs, 320 lists): three-colour lists 13% before (the old parent strength and policy), 12% with the new parent strength alone, 0% with the policy; lists with Blue or Yellow 39%, 37%, 34%; Saburo in 47%, 42%, 38% of the lists, Dexter in 33%, 35%, 40%. The pull toward Red and Green stays (RGG and RRG 57% before, 63% after): it comes from the parents (Dexter and the V / Smasher ladder lists hold the top six places), not from the Legend scores.
- Retirement (since 2026-11-24, `scripts/season-retire.ts`; the owner's choice "From next season on": each season retires its own bred decks that end below the bar, and today's library stays as it is). The rule is "shown below the bar": a deck is retired when its 90% range on the win rate against the library lies wholly below the meta decks' median; a deck that could be at the bar stays, as "could not tell" does everywhere else. By default (`--retire on`) the season judges, at its end, only the decks it bred and entered, on a fit that pools its own round robin's rating run as the ratings will (`fitPooledRatings`: the ratings' pool, fit and numbers, equal to `src/data/ratings.json` while that file is current); without the rating run (`--rating-run off`, or a bot other than hard) nothing is judged. `--retire-earlier` (opt-in) judges earlier seasons' bred decks on `ratings.json` before breeding: on 2026-11-24, with the bar at 51.6, it would retire 9 of the 22 (the point estimate alone would have taken 17, S1-05 Cross at 51.3 among them). A retired deck moves to `data/decks/archive/<stem>-v<version>.txt` (a "// Retired:" line added), its note moves to `RETIRED_OBJECTIVES` in `src/content/deckObjectives/archived.ts` with a line saying when and why (a deck retired at its own season's end has no note yet: a one-line `retiredPlaceholder`), the season file lists it under `retired`, and its trio counts as seen like a kept-out one. Its rating-run games stay in the pool (a deck no longer in the library keeps its games).
- The gate plays each bred list in the order its deck file reads back (`asWritten`, scripts/gate-run.ts). The order of a list's cards changes the games a seed deals it (the shuffle starts from that order, and the list fingerprint does not see it), and the export regroups the cards by type and cost. So the gate's games are exactly the games anyone replays from the recorded list or from the deck file: `tests/season-gate.test.ts` replays two decks' games from the record, and fails when the breeder's own order is played instead.
- `--gate-only` breeds and gates, then stops; with `--out DIR` it writes `season-N-gate.json` and `season-N-gate.md` there. No deck file, leaderboard, impact file or report in the repository changes. It may look at a recorded season again; it then breeds as a `--force` re-run would.
- The round robin's bot is `--bot`, `hard` by default since 2026-11-16 (Season 3 was played with it; Seasons 1 and 2 with the old `deep` bot, which was the default until then).

**The breeder's card impact (`data/seasons/impact.json`, `data/seasons/deck-impact.json`).** The breeder scores a card by its card value plus 100 × its "Δ when drawn" from two tables: one pooled by card, and one per list keyed by list fingerprint (a parent's own numbers). Since 2026-11-16 both files keep the games of each simulator version and bot apart: an entry per pair (`simVersion`, the bot's canonical name as the change ledger uses it, so lookahead is Medium), with its sums and the runs they came from (the seasons, and for the oldest pooled entry the review telemetry it was seeded from). A season adds its round robin to the entry of the current simulator version and its `--bot`, and only to that one. The breeder (the season, `--gate-only`, `scripts/breed-preview.ts` and `scripts/theorize-ladder.ts`, all through `loadImpactTables` in `scripts/breed-data.ts`, logic in `src/lib/breederImpact.ts`) reads the numbers of one pair, never a sum of two:
- the current simulator version and the season's bot, when that pair has numbers (a season entry, or the review telemetry `src/data/analysis.json` when its manifest names the pair; the two add together, being the same pair). Nothing else is read.
- Otherwise the closest pair there is, with a warning: the current simulator version with another bot first, else the highest other simulator version; within one version the season's bot first, then the pair with the most drawn-games. The per-list table follows the same pair. This happens after every simulator version change until a season's round robin has been played on the new version: that season's numbers go to the new pair, and the next season reads them.
- With no numbers anywhere, empty tables: the breeder ranks cards by card value alone, with a warning.

The breeder prints one line saying which case applied and which pair it read (simulator version, bot, cards, drawn-games, lists, sources) and how many entries of other versions or bots are kept unread; a fallback line starts "Warning:", says that numbers measured on another simulator version or bot can mislead, and says what makes current ones. The other entries are kept and not read. Until 2026-11-24 the review telemetry was the `deep` bot on version 9, so a Hard season on version 9 read Season 3's entry and not it. **Since 2026-11-24** (REVIEW_2026-11-24 B.2) it is the Hard bot on all 71 library lists, so the (9, hard) pair is Season 3's round robin plus that run, and one table serves every reader: the breeder; the forge's ordinary additions, which read the breeder's pooled table through `loadImpactTables` and print the pair (`forgeAddsWords`; until then `src/data/analysis.json` alone, the `deep` bot on the 7 meta decks); and the coach and the Deck Lab, whose `src/data/impact.json` is now the per-list table of that pair (`impactDataOf`, `scripts/build-impact.ts`, checked by `check:data`), rounded to 4 decimals. The run's games were not written into the season files: `readImpactTables` already adds the telemetry file when its manifest names the pair, so writing them too would count them twice. Why a fallback rather than nothing: the table is an input to the breeder's card choice, not a reported result, and the measurement below found that which reasonably current table it read mattered little while no numbers at all cost about 8.5 points per list on Medium (5.5 with Hard), and numbers from the old simulator still beat none by about 6 on Medium (2.5 with Hard, from the games alone). The minimums are the project's: 60 drawn-games pooled, 30 per list (the preview used 40 pooled until then). Before 2026-11-16 the two files were single pools: Season 3's Hard games on version 9 were added to the sums of the baseline and Seasons 1 and 2 (`deep`, version 5). `scripts/migrate-season-impact.ts` split them exactly (the earlier entry is the file before Season 3; Season 3's is the difference), and `check:data` checks the shapes.

**Parent strength since 2026-11-24** (docs/REVIEW_2026-11-24.md B.5, findings L4 and R8). A deck's strength is its power rating's estimate (`wrField`, the expected win rate against the library), for decks with `PARENT_MIN_GAMES` (100) or more games on their current list; no leaderboard blend and no lower-bound discount. The blend mixed a win rate against the 7 meta decks (shrunk toward 50% over 60 games) into one against the library, and moved Ladder: V / Smasher / Padre from 2nd to 5th parent, out of the breeder's four bosses; the lower bound ranked a deck on 60 games 3 to 6 points below an equal one on 400. A deck under 100 games is left out (the season prints which) rather than given a lineage prior from its parent list: since the same day a season's round robin is a rating run, so a new list has 240 or more games (16 against each of the 15 or more other decks of its round robin) as soon as the ratings are rebuilt, and the series plays unrated lists first, while a prior would carry the parent's rating across a change the forge measured with Medium. Today all 71 library decks have 301 to 446 games, so the change moves only the order: the top six by estimate are Dexter (73.9), Ladder: V / Smasher / Padre (67.1), Ladder S1 #5 (67.0), #2 (66.9), #3 (66.6) and #1 (65.1). **Parent strength (`src/lib/parentStrength.ts`, 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-24).** The season breeder and the Deck Lab now rank parents with one function. A rated deck counts at its conservative rating (the expected win rate against the field, halfway down to the lower bound of its range). A season leaderboard entry is blended in at 35% only when it is current: every season that added to it ran under the current simulator version. The seed data ("season 0") and seasons recorded without a manifest are old. A stale entry is not used at all, so a deck with only a stale entry is no parent candidate. Today every leaderboard entry is stale (seasons 1 and 2 ran under version 5), so the ratings alone rank. `staleLeaderRow` says whether a leaderboard row is stale from what `src/data/seasons.json` already holds. The Lab used to blend without the discount and without the staleness; it now gives the script's numbers.

**`npm run gate`.** `npm run gate -- <deck> --ref <deck>` gates one library deck against another with the same rule and words: `--bot`, `--confirm-bot`, `--rounds`, `--stages`, `--confirm-stages`, `--seed`, `--confirm-seed`, `--threads`, `--cache`, `--json`, `--out DIR`. `--no-early-stop` plays every stage of both bots (the confirming bot's too, for a deck shown worse) and still reports where the staged rule would have stopped and with which verdict, beside the figure over all the games. It prints the per-opponent and per-seat figures and writes nothing unless `--out` is given.

**The Deck Lab's test (Breed tab).** Each bred deck's "Test against <reference>" replaces the old quick test (56 Medium games at one seed, unpaired, about ±13 points). It is the gate's rule at the Lab's sizes (`LAB_TEST` in `src/lib/breedGate.ts`): stages of 10 double rounds (140 games with 7 opponents, 120 with 6), up to 4, the Medium bot alone, from the Lab's fixed seed 77,000, so every deck and reference play the same games. The reference is the deck's best parent by the Lab's ranking (`rankedParents`, the same parent strength); an explorer, or a deck none of whose parents is ranked, gets the middle meta deck. Before it runs, the card says what it may play (at most 1,120 games, deck and reference) and what it can tell: shown worse or better 8 times in 10 at about 7.1 points with 7 opponents and 7.6 with 6 (at the Medium bot's measured spread, 0.59), so "could not tell" is the likely answer for smaller differences. The result is the verdict ("shown worse than X", "shown better than X", "could not tell: not shown worse than X"), the paired difference with its range, both win rates and the games. The Lab enters nothing, so it never says "entered" or "kept out", and never "as good as". A reference's blocks are kept per panel (`keepReferences`, keyed by `referenceKey`: list, bot, seeds, games, field) and reused by every deck and press that shares it; a block that fails or is stopped is forgotten. Stop stops every running test; a saved deck keeps the manifest of its test (field mode `deck-lab-breed-test:meta`). The sentences are `labReferenceWords`, `labBeforeWords` and `labResultWords`; the view is `src/ui/components/LabBreedTest.tsx`.

**The Seasons page.** Each season's panel has a table "Bred decks, then and now" (`src/lib/bredDecks.ts`, light: no gate statistics): per bred deck, how it was made and from what ("a new Legend trio" for an explorer), its win rate in its own season's round robin (that season's bot and simulator), its power rating now with its 90% range and games ("not rated" without one), its best parent now by today's parent strength (`bestParent`, the first choice of `chooseReference`; none for an explorer) with that parent's rating, and the test at entry when the season recorded one ("not tested before entering" for seasons 1 and 2). The two win rates are different measures and stand side by side; no difference between them is computed. Decks a season's test did not let in are listed under the table with its words and figures. Leaderboard rows whose games come from an older simulator are marked "older simulator" (`staleLeaderRow`), and the page says the breeder no longer uses those numbers to choose parents.

**What is not claimed.** A deck that enters is not shown to be as good as its reference, and "shown better" by one bot is not a rating. The test is against one reference on the meta field (by default the middle meta deck, not the deck's parent), not against the whole library. A deck kept out may be a fine deck that its reference happens to beat on these games; that is the about 2.5% above when it is truly no worse, and more often when it is a little worse. The detect figures assume the spreads measured on the recorded bred decks; the spread seen is printed so a reader can tell when a run differs from them.

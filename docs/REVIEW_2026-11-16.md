# Review pass for gains: bots, coach, deck tools (2026-11-16)

Requested by the owner on 2026-11-16, after Season 3 and the review of the roadmap ([ROADMAP2.md](ROADMAP2.md) §10 and §11): "another review pass for potential performance gains on bots, coach, and deck improvements". Performance is read here as how well each part does its job (playing strength, the quality of advice, the quality of the decks the tools produce), and how fast it runs where speed limits that.

**How it was done.** Three reviewers (agents) read the code and the earlier reviews and measured where a measurement was cheap: about 25 minutes of machine time in all, on 4 threads each, with every script and result kept outside the repository. The coordinator then re-checked the claims the recommendations rest on: the re-bred decks' gain was recomputed from the raw results, the bots' speed-up was run again on 20 fresh games, and the code and data claims quoted below were read at the lines named. Nothing in this pass changed how any bot plays, and nothing was changed in the code: this document is findings and a ranked list.

**What it could not see.** No browser timing (Node only). The coach's noise figures come from positions that bots reached, not people. Deck measurements here are with the Medium bot unless they say Hard. Samples are small where the text says so, and a range is given wherever one was computed.

## The short version

Ranked by expected value for the cost. S is under an hour of an agent, M a few hours, L a day or more or long simulations. None of the first eight needs a SIM_VERSION bump.

The first two are the ones to do first: the first makes every Hard run after it cheaper (seasons, confirmations, events, the benchmark), and the second is the largest measured gain in deck quality for the least work.

**Both are done (2026-11-16): see "Done since" at the end.** Gain 1 landed as measured. Gain 2's +4.4 held with Hard for Season 3 (+5.4) but turned out to be Season 3's case: for the next season the fix adds nothing shown, and the measurements changed what was built.

## Bots

**Where a Hard decision's time goes** (8 Hard-against-Hard games, about 1.5 s each): main-phase rollouts 70%, reaction rollouts 27%, the first screen 3%. Each rollout runs about 38 one-step simulations inside it. By function: copying the state 26%; the evaluation 25%, of which about 6% recomputes the whole-list Gig plan on every copy (its cache is keyed on each copy's cards, `src/ai/eval.ts`); power and keyword lookups about 16% (`rawPower` and `hasKw` in `src/engine/game/mods.ts` build a list of sources for every query, for 4 cards with a power hook and 1 with a keyword hook in the whole pool). Re-dealing hidden cards is 2%. A Hard decision takes 5.6 ms at the median and 119 ms at the 99th percentile in Node; in the browser the practice bot runs on the main thread.

**The speed-ups** (gain 1). Five edits, built as a prototype outside the repository: power and keyword lookups walk the board without building a list; the Gig-plan score counts in a reused array; the plan and archetype caches are shared by a game and its copies; the state copier writes each card as a literal in the engine's own field order; the bots inside a rollout stop building explanations nobody reads.
- Same seed, same game: 250 games over all 53 library decks (30 Hard, 20 mixed, 100 Medium, 100 Easy) ended in the same state, log included, before and after. The coordinator ran 8 Hard and 12 Medium games on other seeds: identical again.
- Hard 1.45× faster side by side, 1.67× back to back (1.54× in the coordinator's run); Medium about 1.3×; Easy unchanged. The slowest decisions fall from 236 ms to 124 ms.
- To land it safely: record final-state hashes of a few dozen games before the change and pin them as a test; keep the card literal in the engine's field order with a test that compares it with the generic copier on real states; expect the shared game cache to start again once.
- What it buys: about a third off every all-Hard run (an event, the season's round robin and finals, Hard confirmations in the forge and the Deck Lab, the benchmark rebuild from 35 to about 24 minutes), and shorter pauses in the browser.

**Is Hard's search earning its cost?** Not known by component. The only firm figure is the ladder (Hard beats Medium 67.0%, last measured on SIM_VERSION 8). Deeper search alone (5 candidates rolled out against 3) could not be told from Hard on 126 paired deals: 52.0% [48.4, 55.5]. Three ablations would say where the 67% comes from, each about 4 minutes on 15 threads: Hard without reaction rollouts (27% of its time, last measured on an old version), without the sell lookahead, and without main-phase rollouts. If reaction rollouts earn little, dropping them saves a quarter of Hard's time (that change would need a bump).

**Not worth doing again** (already measured, in the earlier reviews): more worlds per rollout, counting rollout wins, the turn planner, the sell search, a wider clamp, the per-deck weight set, card impact for Hard. Easy: nothing found; its policy is also the fast policy inside every lookahead, so a change there moves Medium and Hard.

**The benchmark as a yardstick.** It would need a Hard continuation (4 to 5 times the cost), positions where the tiers pick differently and about 100 paired worlds an option: about 35 minutes on 15 threads, and the one measurement there is (the sign of Easy minus Hard agreed on 12 of 32 positions) says it may still not separate them. Paired games already separate the tiers in about a minute. Keep the benchmark for diagnosis.

**The fitted evaluation.** A re-fit on SIM_VERSION 9 is unlikely to matter much: after the fit was switched on, the evaluation was within 0.029 log-loss of its own re-fit. One thing never compared in play: the fit used the view of the player who just moved, while Hard scores a rollout from the decider's view after the rival's reply (`--view next`). About 25 minutes of games on 4 threads, then paired games; a bump if adopted.

## Coach and game review

**How the coach values a move.** A one-step screen keeps the 6 best candidates and passing; each is played out in 6 shared worlds by one-step bots; the pick is the highest mean. The play-outs are paired between options, and the paired spread is used for the grade but not for the pick.

**Noise** (193 real decisions from 8 games, each asked under 5 seeds; judged against 48 fresh worlds valued the coach's own way, so this is consistency, not accuracy). The pick was not the same under all 5 seeds in 25 of 193 decisions (13%), and the picks that differ cost little: 0.028 Gigs a decision on average. Twelve worlds everywhere halves that (0.016) for twice the time; adding 6 worlds only where the top options are within noise gets most of it (0.018) for 55% more time. Neither is worth much until the coach is faster.

**Is the advice good?** Two checks that do not use the coach's own evaluation as the judge. On the position benchmark the coach's moves lose 3.9 win-rate points against the labelled best, Hard's 4.8 (paired difference −0.9 [−1.9, +0.1]; the benchmark does not separate the tiers, so this is weak). Playing as a bot against Hard, the coach's configuration won 58.3% [48.9, 67.7] of 84 paired games: promising, not settled. A run of about 336 games (10 minutes on 4 threads) would give ±4.7.

**Waiting time.** A main-phase answer takes 150 to 200 ms at the median in Node and 350 to 600 ms for the slowest tenth; sells and reactions under 120 ms; other choices about 1 ms. Copying the state is 30% of it. The bots' speed-ups (gain 1) apply here too, and the play-outs of one decision are independent, so they can run on 2 to 4 workers with the same answer.

**Coverage.** Of about 37 decisions a game that are worth asking about, 13 get no grade and about 10 no reason: Gig picks when rolling in and stealing (8 a game, the commonest), Gig values, modes, targets, card choices. Payments made by hand (20 a game for a player who pays by hand) are never advised. The reason is empty for those choices, not the bot's last thought as the earlier review said: each piece of advice comes from a fresh coach.

**The grades** (gains 3 and 4).
- The review does not confirm the player's own moves. Moves the coach graded during play are skipped by the review and keep their 6-world grade; the 16-world confirmation runs only on the moves the review itself grades, which are mostly the rival's. `docs/BOT_TOOLKIT.md` §8 presents the confirmation as the review's guarantee.
- "Wide" close calls are never confirmed: a gap of 0.375 Gigs or more that the 6 worlds cannot tell from noise is shown as a close call. On 48 worlds, 23 of 35 of them were Mistakes. A Mistake the coach does show is rarely wrong (167 of 176).
- Main-phase grades and reaction grades are on different scales: the coach's main-phase play-outs are not clamped, its reaction play-outs and the review's are. In 34 of 86 decisions at least one world ended the game, and one such world can stretch the spread until a real difference reads as a close call.
- A sell the coach chose by rule, with nothing measured, is graded as agreed and counts as fine on the scorecard (18 of 45 sells in the sample).
- The same small gap is "Best" in the review (up to 0.1 Gigs) and "Close call" from the coach.

## Deck tools

**Why Season 3's decks were weak.** The breeder can be re-run exactly from the files it read, so the causes can be tested.
- Its card table is old. `data/seasons/impact.json` pools "difference when drawn" from every run with no simulator version or bot attached; before the season it held the baseline and Seasons 1 and 2, all on SIM_VERSION 5 with the old deep bot. It ranks Johnny Silverhand: Never Stop Fighting 68th of 90; the current analysis ranks it 4th of 58. The two tables agree only loosely (rank correlation 0.52), and the table weighs heavily in how a list is filled.
- Breeding loses most of a parent's strength. S3-05 keeps 30 of its parent's 40 cards and scored 48.3% where the parent scored 61.9% in the same round robin; the one change that matters is a Legend. The Legend is chosen by the average strength of the decks that use it, never by whether it suits the cards. The mutants keep 16 and 19 of 40 cards.
- The counters were never predicted to beat their target: their parents' records against RRG Dexter Midrange are 38% to 46%, and nothing predicts the merged list, which took 17% and 20%.
- No list is played before it is picked: the breeder takes the first legal candidate for each of its 8 slots, and a deck the test keeps out is not replaced.

**A current card table** (gain 2). Season 3 was bred again with the current analysis as the card table, and each new list played the recorded one (Medium, 1,120 paired games a slot, the same seeds for all). Seven of eight came out better: +9.3, +8.3, +0.7, +3.5, −0.8, +3.8, +5.4 and +5.3 points; +4.4 on average (±0.9 from game noise for these eight lists; across lists the gains spread by 3.4 points, so the method's average is less certain than that: roughly +1.5 to +7.3). Limits: Medium, not Hard (for single cards Hard has shown about three quarters of a Medium gain; for whole lists that is not measured); and the current table covers the 58 cards the 7 meta decks play, from the deep bot, so a card outside them scores nothing. The fix is to keep the table by simulator version and bot and read only the current one; the check is the same eight pairs with Hard (7,680 games).

**The forge's search** (gain 5). With 5 candidates on 112 Medium games each, the best candidate comes first 9 times in 10 when it is really 4 points better, 6 times in 10 at 2 points. The candidates are narrow: one cut crossed with the top additions (all five of S3-02's cut the same card, and all five of S3-03's), and the additions come from one ranking over the 7 meta decks' cards, which is why one card was offered to all six lists. The cuts are chosen from the same 112 games the candidates are then screened on, which lifts every candidate: the season's 30 candidates read +1.9 points when screened and +0.6 on fresh games (each deck's leader +5.1 and +1.5). Twenty candidates by halving (56 games each, the best 5 on 168 more) would make the pick worth about +2.7 points against +1.7 today for 1,960 Medium games: about 100 Hard-game equivalents, where one nominee's Hard confirmation can cost 1,792 Hard games.

**What the kept games say.** 8 lists, 89 changes, 110 screened candidates; 14 changes have both screening and held-out games, reading +5.6 and +2.4. Additions that keep coming out ahead: Yorinobu Arasaka: Steel Dragon in S2-05, Rockn' Rockerboy in S2-08 (three different cuts), and Johnny Silverhand: Never Stop Fighting in five lists (pooled over Season 3's four Hard confirmations: +2.6 points [+1.5, +3.7] on 2,912 paired games). The evidence for an addition is kept by list, so what four lists learned about one card helps no fifth; a cross-list figure for ranking and nominating (not for adopting) would use it.

**The test at entry.** The breeder is the bottleneck, not the bar: a bar at the weakest returning deck would have let nobody in. Requiring Hard to read a deck at least 2 points above the bar would have kept out exactly the two decks that finished below it. Medium read 3.2 points higher than Hard on average and was wrong about one deck; its stage cost about as much as it saved, so those Medium games would be better spent screening more bred candidates (gain 6).

**Deck Lab** (gain 7). After the first adoption, the "against your original" block replays the comparison the confirmation has just made, on fewer games: up to 896 of a Standard run's 6,272 games.

**Cost of Season 3's results.** The test at entry: about 716 Hard-game equivalents per deck that entered. The forge: 6,720 Hard and 4,032 Medium games for two adoptions worth 7.7 points together, about 900 Hard-game equivalents per confirmed point.

## Found wrong on the way

Not fixed in this pass (each is small; they are in the next goal):
- `scripts/season.ts`: the default `--bot` is still `deep`, the bot from before the tiers. A season run without the flag would quietly use it.
- The decision report's calibration (`public/data/decisions.json`) is for that same old bot playing itself. Its "kept 52.5%, overturned 50.1%" says nothing about the worth of Hard's search; Hard's own numbers need a run with `--bot hard`.
- `data/seasons/impact.json` carries no simulator version or bot, and now mixes SIM_VERSION 5 deep games with SIM_VERSION 9 Hard games.
- `src/lib/deckSearch.ts`: the assumed per-game spreads (0.30 for Medium, 0.34 for Hard) are above what the kept games show (0.256 and 0.295), so "what a run can detect" reads 20 to 30% too many games.
- The forge's and the builder's comments call the additions' ranking "format-wide"; it covers the 7 meta decks' cards.
- The counter's note names three of its four target decks.
- `scripts/breed-preview.ts` asks for 40 games a card where the season asks for 60.
- The coach items listed under "The grades" above.

## Done since (2026-11-16, later the same day)

The owner said to start with gains 1 and 2. Both are in, each built by an agent and then checked again by the coordinator on its own seeds and scripts. Gain 2 came out differently from what this review expected, and the difference is worth reading.

**Gain 1: the bots' speed-ups.** Landed with no decision changed and no SIM_VERSION bump (docs/BOT_TOOLKIT.md §6 has the detail).
- The same games: 62 seeded games over every library deck are now pinned as a test (`data/benchmark/game-corpus.json`, `tests/game-corpus.test.ts`). The file was built from the code before the change, and both the old and the new code end all 62 in the recorded states. Beyond it, the agent's 308 games at every tier name and the coordinator's 518 on other seeds ended byte-identical, logs included, and so did the position benchmark's picks (Hard, Medium, the coach, Easy), two exported game records and two coach reports over 150 decisions.
- The speed, on a quiet machine, the same seeded games on the old and the new code in turn, three rounds: 14 Hard games 13.5 s → 8.8 s (35% less), 70 Medium games 2.3 s → 1.5 s (a third less), 300 Easy games 2.1 s → 1.6 s (a quarter less). A Hard game still costs about as many Medium games as before, because Medium got quicker too.
- In the form it landed, two prototype edits changed: the state copy uses a spread of each card (the prototype's field-by-field literal could reorder a card's keys, which the saved position hash depends on), and there is one definition of which cards are active sources instead of two more copies of it.
- In the browser a practice game against Hard with the coach on plays with no error, and the live site serves the new code. Browser timing was not measured.

**Gain 2: the breeder's card table.** The two tables are now kept by simulator version and bot with the runs each entry came from, read through one function, and checked by `check:data`; the committed files were split exactly (docs/SYNERGIES.md, "The breeder's card impact"). What the measurements showed:
- This review's finding holds with Hard. Season 3 bred again from the current simulator's telemetry, against the recorded lists: +5.4 points on average with Hard, better in 8 of 8 (games alone +3.9 to +6.9; across lists +3.5 to +7.3; 560 paired games a list). The Medium figure above was +4.4. So the old table cost Season 3's decks about 5 points each.
- For Season 4 the gain is already banked, and reading only the current entry adds nothing shown. Season 3's own Hard games had gone into the pooled table, and Season 4's eight lists bred from the current entry alone are not shown to differ from those bred from the pooled table: +0.6 on Medium and +0.4 with Hard, both intervals across zero. "No extra games for +4.4" was Season 3's case, not the next season's.
- The rule first built (read the current entry or nothing) had a cost this review did not see. With no numbers at all the breeder made clearly weaker lists: 8.5 points worse on Medium, 5.5 with Hard (7 of 8 lists). That is what the first season after every simulator change would have got. Old numbers still beat none (+6.4 on Medium; +2.5 with Hard from the games alone).
- So the rule that landed is: read the current simulator version and bot when they have numbers; otherwise, with a warning, the closest numbers there are (the current version with another bot first, then the newest other version); never a sum of two. Under it Season 3 would have read the telemetry, which is the +5.4 above.
- Cost of the measurements: about 44,000 Medium games and 25,000 Hard games.

**Found wrong on the way, fixed with gain 2:** the season's default bot is now `hard`; the two tables carry simulator version and bot; the preview and `theorize-ladder.ts` read the tables through the season's own reader, so their minimum of 40 games is gone; the forge's and the builder's comments say which decks the additions' ranking covers; a season stopped after its round robin no longer fails at the end of its next run.

**Still open from that list:** the decision report's calibration for Hard; the assumed spreads in `src/lib/deckSearch.ts`; the counter's note (its text is part of a pinned hash in `tests/deck-packages-defaults.test.ts`, so it waits for a change that re-takes those pins); the coach items.

**Found while landing these:**
- `HARD_TIME_RATIO` in `src/lib/deckSearch.ts` says a Hard game costs 20 Medium games. Measured on this machine it is 25 to 30, before and after the speed-ups (Medium got quicker too).
- The speed words on the Play setup's bot tiers (`src/ai/tiers.ts`) are older measurements.
- `src/data/analysis.json` on SIM_VERSION 9 was measured with the old `deep` bot. The coach and the deck builders read those numbers, and a Hard season's breeder reads them only when it has no season entry.
- `src/lib/deckLab.ts` and the deck header `scripts/theorize-ladder.ts` writes still call the meta decks' card numbers "format-wide".
- `tests/net-relay.test.ts` could fail about once in a hundred runs on a long game: its in-memory relay's clock never moved, so a connection had 200 frames for the whole game. Fixed with gain 1.

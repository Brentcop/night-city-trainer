# Review: how the AI rates a player's moves (2026-11-25)

Requested by the owner on 2026-11-25: "Review current ai turn rating process and effectiveness for improvements".

**How the request was read.** "Turn rating" is the AI's rating of a player's moves, which the site does in two places, and both are covered:
- **The live coach** grades each move during a game (agree, Best, Close call, Slip, Mistake, Blunder) and sums them into the scorecard and its letter (`src/ai/coach.ts`, `src/lib/coachGrading.ts`, `src/lib/coachWords.ts`, `src/lib/practiceLog.ts`).
- **The Game review** rates every move of a finished game (Inspired, Sharp, Best, Close call, Slip, Mistake, Blunder, not counted) and gives each side an accuracy (`src/lib/gameReview.ts`, `src/sim/gameReviewRun.ts`, `src/ai/reviewer.ts`).

"Effectiveness" is read as: do the ratings measure skill, are they stable, and does a bad rating mean the move actually cost games.

**How it was done.** Two reviewers read the code and data (the coach; the Game review) and measured where a claim hinged on numbers; a third agent measured effectiveness as a whole (players of known strength rated by both instruments, re-rating noise, and every costly rating played on to the end both ways); two verifiers then tried to disprove the main claims with their own scripts, other seeds and the library's non-meta decks. In all about 2,000 rated games and some 3,700 positions played on to the end, at most 3 threads per agent (the machine was shared with group C of the 11-24 review), on SIM_VERSION 9. Each finding says whether it was read in code, computed or measured, and whether the verifiers confirmed it; §6 lists every verdict. Nothing in the repository changed except this file and one fix made during the review (finding R1, commit e77459a).

**What it could not see.** People. Every rated player is a bot (uniform random, Easy, Medium, Hard), so "skill" means bot tier, and whether the grades help a person play better was not measured (§7).

## The short version

**The one-paragraph diagnosis.** The ratings measure skill: on the same deals the Game review rates 43% of a uniformly random player's counted moves Mistake or worse, Easy's 24%, Medium's 13% and Hard's 5%, one game ranks Easy below Hard about 85–90% of the time, and re-rating a game moves its accuracy by only 1–4 points. A move rated Mistake or worse does cost games when played on. What is weak is everything around that core. A third of Game reviews on the meta decks failed outright until tonight. The live coach shows unconfirmed six-world verdicts, so its grades carry a winner's-curse bias and a 10–20% chance of calling an equal move a Slip, and about a third of its "close calls" are Mistakes on more worlds. Its scorecard letter barely separates skill (a random player averages a B). The size of a rated error is overstated: a Gig of rated gap is worth about 4 points of win chance with a Hard continuation, and gaps above 1 Gig hardly predict more. Hard's own flags (about one a game) are mostly overstated, accuracy does not predict who wins a mirror match between equal players, mulligan grades are noise, and "Sharp"/"Inspired" are not skill markers (Easy earns more Inspired than Hard). Finally, every simulator bump makes every unreviewed game unreviewable, and tonight's group C work bumps the version.

## 1. How a move is rated

### The live coach (read in code; `src/ai/coach.ts`, `src/ai/heuristic.ts`)

- **Candidates.** In the main phase the coach sells first by rule when it would (comparing only the 3 most sellable cards against each other, never selling against not selling), else screens up to 40 actions one step ahead with Easy agents and rolls out the top 6 plus passing. Reactions: every option. Skipped: payments, effect order, one-option decisions (`coachWorthAsking`); every choice that is not an action or a reaction (targets, modes, Gig picks, the mulligan) is "unscored".
- **Worlds and horizon.** 6 shared worlds (`COACH_WORLDS`, fixed seed 7, so a position always gets the same grade). After a main-phase move: the rest of your turn and the rival's whole turn; after a reaction: the rest of the attack, the attacker's turn and your whole next turn. Both seats are played by Medium-like clones (no lookahead of their own beyond one step, no noise); `rolloutWins` is off.
- **Value.** `evaluate()` with the matchup-profiled weights, plus 0.05 × the one-step value, −1.5 for a Call; each world clamped to ±600 for the grade. Gap = (pick − move) / 40 Gigs; SE = the paired SE over the 6 worlds.
- **Verdicts** (`gradeMove`): agree (same option); Best (gap ≤ 0.1); Close call (gap < 0.15 or ≤ 2 SE, an SE under 0.025 taken as 0.25); Mistake (≥ 0.375 outside the noise); else Slip; shown as Blunder from 1 Gig. **Scorecard:** fine = agree, Best or Close; % fine → A/B/C/D at 85/70/55.

### The Game review (read in code; `src/ai/reviewer.ts`, `src/lib/gameReview.ts`)

- **Candidates:** the move made, the top 4 of Medium's one-step screen, End turn and the sell alternatives; reactions all options; since 2026-11-22 (D25) mulligans (whole Medium games, 8 worlds, the result only) and Gig dice choices.
- **Play-outs:** 8 per candidate, Medium-like bots on both seats, scored with `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` (no profiles, no offset), clamped to ±600. Slip or worse is confirmed on 16 fresh worlds. Your moves the coach graded during play are re-measured when costly (since REVIEW_RULES 3).
- **Horizons differ by decision type:** main phase to the end of the rival's next turn; reaction to the end of your next turn; Gig dice the rest of the active turn and the next; mulligan the whole game. All are summed on one Gig scale and one accuracy.

### Where the constants came from (read in code and history)

| Constant | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `BEST_GIGS` | 0.1 | the reviewer's 4-point noise floor, 2026-11-10 |
| `CLOSE_GIGS`, `SE_FACTOR`, `MISTAKE_GIGS`, `BLUNDER_GIGS`, the review's cap | 0.15, 2, 0.375, 1, 3 | by hand (0.375 is the reviewer's 15-point "blunder" of the hand-weights era) |
| `FIXED_SE_GIGS`, `FIXED_GAP_SE_GIGS` | 0.025, 0.25 | by hand; the median review SE on 12 Medium-vs-Hard games, SIM 8, 2026-11-13 |
| `COACH_WORLDS`, coach `deep` | 6, 6 | response time on one thread, 2026-11-12 |
| review play-outs, confirmation worlds | 8, 16 | re-run stability, 2026-11-12/13, SIM 8 |
| `GIG` | 40 | 2 × the `gig` weight; never calibrated to win chance |
| scorecard letters | 85/70/55 | by hand, 2026-11-12 |

Every measured constant was measured for stability on Medium-vs-Hard meta games; none for validity. This review is the first validity measurement.

## 2. Effectiveness, measured

### The ratings separate skill (measured; confirmed by the verifier on other decks and seeds)

The same deals, each played by four players against a fixed Medium opponent; the player's seat is rated by the Game review and by the coach.

| Player | Win rate | Mistake-or-worse per counted move | Review accuracy | Coach % fine |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uniform random | 0% | 44.2% [40.2, 48.5] (verifier 42.8%) | 42.5 (40.8) | 72.8 (75.4) |
| Easy | 36% | 24.4% [21.2, 27.5] (24.5%) | 64.9 (64.8) | 81.6 (82.3) |
| Medium | 47% | 13.9% [10.6, 17.5] (13.1%) | 78.7 (81.3) | 89.1 (89.4) |
| Hard | 73% | 5.2% [3.8, 6.7] (5.1%) | 91.8 (91.4) | 95.8 (96.4) |

- One game ranks Easy below Hard 90% of the time (verifier: 84% ± 9 on non-meta decks), Medium below Hard 70–75%.
- Re-rating a game on another oracle seed moves its accuracy by 2.5–4.5 points (verifier: mean change 1.2 to 3.5 by tier); the same rating comes back about 90% of the time. Noise is small next to the 26-point Easy–Hard gap.
- The coach and the review agree on 93.4% of moves in three classes (kappa 0.80), less for strong play (Hard: kappa 0.55).
- A first run had Medium rated worse than Easy against a Hard opponent; the verifier refuted it on 105 deals (Medium 16.0% vs Easy 21.6% Mistake-or-worse): it came from the crash of R1, which dropped the Meredith Stout decks' games non-randomly.

### A bad rating costs games, but less than its size says (measured; verified with a Hard continuation)

Every costly rating was played on from the real position to the end, once with the move made and once with the recommended move, over paired worlds.

| Continuation | Flags | Gain of the recommended move | Per Gig of rated gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium, all Mistake-or-worse (mostly random and Easy players) | 555 | +12.8 points [10.2, 15.5] | 6.1 [4.2, 8.0] |
| Medium, the verifier's sample (Medium's and Hard's flags) | 72 | +10.4 ± 4.9 | 6.2 |
| **Hard, the same 72 flags** | 72 | **+6.4 ± 3.9** | **3.9** |
| Hard, Hard's own flags | 28 | +4.9 ± 6.3 | — |
| Close-call controls (Medium) | 179 | +4.5 [1.9, 7.2] | — |

- Under a Hard continuation 64% of flags show no gain or a loss, and gaps of 1 to 3 Gigs gained +2.7 ± 4.1: the rated size is a poor guide to the cost.
- A rating's Gig scale was never tied to win chance; the play-out clamp alone would put a Gig at about 3.3 points. A Hard-continuation calibration says about 4.
- Close calls are not free: the controls gain +4.5 points, consistent with the misses of finding R6.

### Accuracy does not say who played better in a close game (measured; confirmed)

In 60 Hard-vs-Hard mirror matches (same list both sides, both seats reviewed), the side with the higher accuracy won 29 (48% ± 13); a logistic regression gives −0.08 ± 0.40 log-odds per 10 accuracy points. Across tiers accuracy is higher in wins, but that follows the game state (a lost position offers fewer good moves), not the play. So a game's accuracy separates a weak player from a strong one, not two good players.

## 3. Findings: the live coach

**C1. The coach shows unconfirmed six-world verdicts, so its grades are biased and noisy.** Measured; confirmed (verifier: 4 player types, 1,004 games, non-meta decks).
- Re-measured on 48 fresh worlds: 92–95% of coach Mistakes stay Mistakes, but their gap is inflated by +0.19 ± 0.04 Gigs on average (+0.25 to +0.32 for Mistakes with a paired SE of 0.1 or more): the pick is the best of about 7 noisy means on the same worlds (winner's curse).
- 31% [29, 33] of coach Close calls, and 54% of "wide" close calls (gap ≥ 0.375), are Mistakes on 48 worlds; Slips are the least stable grade (of 172, 85 became close calls on 48 worlds).
- Since 7ae04a5 only the Game review re-measures them; the toast, the scorecard, the practice log and Profile's averages show the unconfirmed verdict.
- Fix (S): confirm Slip-or-worse and wide close calls on 16 fresh worlds in the coach worker before the toast settles ("Checking your move…" exists); about 32 rollouts, 0.1–0.2 s, for 3 to 8 flagged moves a game.

**C2. The 2-SE test on 6 worlds is far looser than it reads.** Computed; confirmed (200,000 trials a setting).
- It is a t-test with 5 degrees of freedom against the maximum of k candidates chosen on the same worlds: when every option is truly equal, Slip or worse is called 10.2, 12.8, 14.8, 16.5, 18.2 and 19.6% of the time for k = 2 to 7 (about 4–8 times the 2.5% a z of 2 suggests); the 0.15-Gig floor cuts it to 4–10% when the per-world noise is small.
- Fixed by C1's confirmation; failing that, a t(5) bound with a split over the candidates.

**C3. The scorecard letter separates skill only weakly.** Measured; confirmed.
- A uniformly random player scores 73–75% "fine" (letters: B 117, C 51, A 28, D 7 of 203 games); Easy 82%, Medium 89%, Hard 96% (253 of 257 Hard games are an A).
- 80% of the random player's "fine" moves were decisions where every scored option was within the noise of the pick (Hard: 48%); counting only decisions where some option differed, the fine shares are 50% / 64% / 80% / 93%: there is the skill signal.
- Fix (S): an `unclear` verdict (every scored option within the noise, or every world decided) left out of the scorecard, as the review leaves out "No clear difference"; headline the confirmed costly moves (count and their cost) rather than a letter.

**C4. A coach "Gig" is not quite the review's Gig.** Read in code; measured as small.
- The coach scores rollouts with matchup-profiled weights (`gig` × 1.1 to 1.3 by archetype) plus 5% of the one-step value but divides by 2 × the unprofiled `gig`; the oracle uses `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` with no offset.
- On the same worlds and moves: coach minus oracle +0.034 ± 0.009 Gigs, slope 0.93, 20% of pairs differ by 0.1 Gig or more, 2.4% land on opposite sides of the Mistake line.
- Fix (S): grade on the unprofiled scale (or divide by the profiled `gig`); the coach still decides with its profiles.

**C5. Smaller defects.** Read in code; confirmed.
- Dead wording: grades are clamped to ±600 (at most 30 Gigs of gap) while `DECIDED_GIGS` is 125, so "wins or loses the game in the play-outs" (`altTag`, `vsNothingWhy`, `decidedWords`) never shows; a lost-game alternative reads "more than 3 Gigs behind", and BOT_TOOLKIT §7 still describes the old wording.
- The pick is decided on unclamped means and graded on clamped ones, so the coach can call an alternative it now values higher a "close call".
- `hideInfo` re-deals the rival's whole hand, forgetting cards the rival revealed into hand (tutors).
- Coverage: about 13 of 37 decisions a game are unscored for a person (Gig picks, targets, modes); the review now grades mulligans and Gig dice, the coach still says "not measured" for them.

**C6. Not a finding: Mistakes from fixed evaluation terms are real.** A reviewer reported that coach Mistakes with a paired SE under 0.1 (gaps from Calls, Go Solo, the hand and Eddie terms, the `blocker` weight) do not carry to results; the verifier refuted it on 750 such Mistakes: they cost +0.106 ± 0.015 games against +0.127 ± 0.010 for the others (pure fixed-term ones +0.115 ± 0.021). Do not suppress them.

## 4. Findings: the Game review

**R1. A third of meta-deck reviews failed (fixed tonight).** Measured; fixed in e77459a.
- Since D25 (2026-11-22) the review grades Gig dice choices; `oracleGigChoice` changed the Gig and played on without resolving pending effects, so a Gig change that triggered Meredith Stout's "when a Gig changes" made the next bot copy throw and the whole review failed ("The review failed: Error: clone() …"). Meredith is in 4 of the 7 meta decks and 14 other lists: 18 of 51 meta-deck reviews failed (35%), 35 of 240 game reviews in the measurement run; `scripts/game-review-check.ts` stopped at the first.
- The fix resolves pending effects and checks state after the change, as the engine does; a decision whose grading throws is now "not measured" with its reason, and the review carries on (`GameReview.failed`); `REVIEW_MEASURE` 3. 21 games that reviewed before are byte-identical after; the verifier's 630 reviews had no failure.
- Lesson: D25 shipped a measurement change with no test of a Gig-triggered effect and without bumping `REVIEW_MEASURE`; a new oracle path needs a test on each kind of state change it makes.

**R2. Hard's own costly flags are mostly overstated.** Measured; confirmed with a Hard continuation.
- Hard's moves get about one Mistake-or-worse a game (1.00 [0.75, 1.27] from the review, 1.08 from the coach); played on, they gain +4.1 points [−3.5, 10.5] (Medium continuation) and +4.9 ± 6.3 (Hard), against 12–16 points for weaker players' flags. Of 50, 12 were clearly confirmed and 3 clearly reversed.
- The oracle's Medium continuation favours moves a Medium bot can follow up; a strong move with a long plan reads worse.
- Proposal (M, measure first): confirm costly flags with Hard play-outs (about 25 Medium games' time each, for 1–3 flags a game) or say in the words that a flag on an otherwise accurate game is uncertain.

**R3. Mulligan grades are noise.** Measured; confirmed.
- The mulligan oracle plays whole Medium games and scores only the result: paired SE median 5.67 Gigs, 87% of 480 mulligans "No clear difference", the smallest non-zero gap 3.75 Gigs on screening (1.875 after confirmation); mulligan Blunders do not follow skill (random 1 of 60, Hard 3 of 180), and a random mulliganer is not flagged more.
- It adds about 1 s to a 4–9 s review, and the move shows as "No"/"Yes" rather than "Keep hand"/"Mulligan".
- Fix (S): stop grading mulligans ("not counted").

**R4. "Sharp" and "Inspired" are not skill markers.** Measured; confirmed.
- They are read off the screening pass and never confirmed. Easy earns them at 0.43–0.45 a game, Hard 0.65–0.93; Easy earns more Inspired than Hard (0.25 vs 0.13 a game, 29 of 30 of them attacks on Gigs); Medium can never earn them (the "quick read" is Medium's own policy); both come more often in won games (Easy 0.88 vs 0.32 a game).
- Fix (S): confirm them on 16 worlds before showing, or drop them; never present them as a sign of skill.

**R5. Ratings that rest on one evaluation weight: the size is wrong, the advice is right.** Measured; corrected by the verifier.
- 6–16% of a non-random player's costly ratings have a gap identical in every play-out (paired SE under 0.025), so the evaluation's weights alone set their size. Example: playing Meredith Stout (Blocker, power 5) instead of 6th Street Recruits (power 6, same cost) is a Blunder at 1.43 Gigs, exactly `blocker` −44.1 plus one point of `unitPower`.
- The reviewer read these as artefacts of a stale weight; the verifier played the 16 Blocker flags on: the recommended move gains +7.8 ± 4.3 points under Hard, as calibrated as measured flags. So the move really is worse; the 1.43 Gigs (a "Blunder") overstates it, and the 11-18 refit's −3.7 would understate it.
- Follows from 6: say the cost in win chance, not in the weight's units.

**R6. Late-game errors are mostly missed.** Computed.
- With the review's rule (8 screening worlds, 16 to confirm, 2 SE), a true 0.5-Gig error is called Slip or worse 73% of the time with normal play-outs but 17% when 4% of worlds swing by 15 Gigs, which is the late game (44% of play-outs end the game from turn 10); at a true 1 Gig the simulated rates are 50% (Blunder, normal play-outs) and 28% (heavy tails).
- So late-game mistakes read as Close calls, consistent with the close-call controls gaining +4.5 points.
- Proposal (M): from turn 10, more worlds or score decided play-outs with `rolloutWins`, and say in the words when a move's worlds were mostly decided.

**R7. Every simulator bump strands unreviewed games.** Read in code; known in part (REVIEW_2026-11-13 assumptions), the scope is new.
- A practice or online game not yet reviewed is refused once `SIM_VERSION` changes (the replay re-plays the bot), so up to 25 replays per browser become unreviewable, and their stored coach grades show nowhere; online games are refused too, though both seats are recorded. Stored reviews stay visible; Play-out details is refused for them.
- Records carry no `simVersion` and `REVIEW_MEASURE` is not tied to it, so a review measured with older bots still counts as "measured as now".
- Fix (M, about half a day): save the bot seat's answers in the practice replay (a few kB; the position hash still guards it), so a bot or evaluation bump no longer strands a game and old games can be reviewed with the current oracle and a note; show stored coach grades when a review is refused; save `simVersion` in review records. Tonight's group C bumps land in one push, so players lose their unreviewed games once.

**R8. Your moves and the rival's are measured by different instruments, and the texts say otherwise.** Read in code; confirmed.
- Your moves: the coach's "agree" (6 worlds) is never re-checked, and a costly verdict needs the coach, the oracle's screen and the confirmation to agree; the rival's need two. "You vs rival" accuracy compares different instruments.
- The screen still says "Your moves the coach graded during play keep the coach's grade" (`GameReview.tsx`), as do BOT_TOOLKIT §8, CLAUDE.md and `rateCoach`'s comment; since REVIEW_RULES 3 (7ae04a5) costly coach grades go back to the oracle, so a move the player saw as a Mistake can show as Best.
- The Play-out details tab runs the oracle again with no confirmation, so it can contradict the main tab and doubles the cost.
- Fix (S): correct the texts; rate both seats the same way (re-check the coach's agrees or rate the player's moves with the oracle as the rival's are).

## 5. Cost

A Game review of both seats takes about 4 to 9 s on one thread (median 8.4 to 11.8 s on a loaded machine; the 11-18 measurement had 5.6 s); the coach about 50 ms per decision it answers. Confirming the coach's costly grades (C1) adds about 0.1–0.2 s for each of 3 to 8 moves a game; dropping mulligan grading saves about 1 s a review.

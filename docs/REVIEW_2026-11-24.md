# Review: how decisions are made, and what they do to the decks (2026-11-24)

Requested by the owner on 2026-11-24: "Review the method of how things are integrated and how we make short and long term decisions and how it impacts decks and the scoring used in tuning/breeding", then, the same hour, "make the review you are doing 10x as in depth".

**How the request was read.** "Short and long term decisions" is read at two levels, and both are covered:
- **Inside a game:** what a bot sees when it picks a move (the next action, the next turn, the end of the game), and how that turns into the numbers the deck tools optimise.
- **Across the project:** the short-term decisions (screening, nominating, a gate stage, one event) and the long-term ones (held-out confirmation, the change ledger, ratings, seasons, the constants and rules the project keeps), and how they are wired together.

**How it was done.** Seven reviewers (agents), each on one slice, read the code and the data, and measured where a claim hinged on it. Three further agents then tried to disprove the main claims with their own scripts and seeds. The coordinator re-checked the structural claims at the lines named; each section says which. In all about 175,000 games (some 40,000 of them with Hard or a variant of it), on up to 16 threads over about two and a half hours; every script and output is outside the repository, and nothing in it changed except this file (the gitignored game cache `.cache/games/` gained about 83,000 games, so the commands in §8 replay from it).
- Slices: the bots' decision horizon; the tuning pipeline (forge, optimizer, Deck Lab, the season's forge step); breeding and the season loop; the rating model, Sim tier and forecast; synergy packages through every layer; a register of every constant the decisions rest on, with how the project decides about its methods; and a measurement run (rank stability across the three bots, and where Hard's edge comes from).

**What it could not see.** Browser timing. Human play (every figure is bots against bots). Real regionals. Anything a measurement below does not name.

## The short version

**The one-paragraph diagnosis.** The decks are optimised for what a one-move bot can see, against seven reference decks of which three now rank 53rd to 55th of 71, and scored with card tables from a third, older bot. Long-horizon value (tricks held for the rival's turn, combos set up and paid off within a turn, packages assembled over several turns, Gig values) is mostly invisible to the bots that make the games, so it is invisible to every number built on those games, and the deck tools drift toward self-contained Units. The measurement run shows the bias is real and structured, not noise: going from Medium (the screening bot) to Hard (the rating bot), the BBG control family (few Units, many Programs, much interaction) gains about 11 points against the rest of the library, red V/Smasher lists and several bred decks lose, and that per-deck change is a stable property of the list (split-half reliability 0.87). No bred deck is BBG. Hard's edge over Medium is almost entirely its main-phase rollouts. The long-term numbers (ratings, Sim tier, parent strength, counters, the forecast) are one-dimensional: real matchup effects are estimated away by a biased estimator, so "counter" breeding is the rating order under another name, and a deck's letter depends on what else is in the library. Breeding has stopped adding strong decks: since Season 1 every season's offspring rate below their parents, and the library's top 15 is Dexter, ladder lists and imported player lists. The error rates of the adoption rule are honest; what limits the decks is what the search can see and where it looks. Finally, the method changes landed on 2026-11-22 were not measured, and the roadmap describes code that does not exist; one of them lets ungated decks into a season.

## 1. How the pieces fit

Every number about a deck comes from games two bots play. Which bot, against which field, and which table is read, differs by tool:

Coordinator checks: the manifests of `analysis.json`, `synergies.json` and `decisions.json` name `bot.level: "deep"`, SIM_VERSION 9; `src/ai/coach.ts:35` sets `impact: 40`; `scripts/event-pool.ts` never reads a season file.

**What flows back.** A season's round robin feeds the card table that breeds the next season; events and rating runs feed the ratings that pick the next parents; the ledger keeps every tuning game for later runs. So whatever the playing bot misreads is fed back as data, and a card the bot misuses gets a low figure, is not picked, and is then not measured again. Noise in the card table corrects itself over seasons (a card picked for a lucky figure is measured again in new lists); a systematic blind spot of the bot does not.

## 2. Short horizon: what a bot sees when it decides

### The decision procedure per tier

- **Easy** (`fastMain`, `src/ai/heuristic.ts:895-908`; `fastReaction`, 1022-1043). Sells first by rule; then plays the option with the highest static prior (a card's `cardValue`, 30 for any attack on Gigs, 12 + target for a winning attack on a Unit, Go Solo 8, a Legend 6). It never activates or Calls in the main phase. Reactions: blocks on fixed power tests and Calls; **never plays a reaction Program** (checked). Horizon: none.
- **Medium** (`chooseMain`, 444-625). Sells by rule (lowest static keep value first, every turn). Every other action (up to 40) is applied in a copy with hidden cards re-dealt and **Easy agents on both seats**, and the copy is scored the moment the action resolves. Reactions are one step too. Horizon: one action (for an attack, including the defender's Easy reaction).
- **Hard** (`src/ai/tiers.ts:36`: deep 3, deepReactions 3, sellLookahead 3, plain mulligan, profiles). Medium's screen, then the top 3 plus pass are each played out **once**: the rest of its turn and the rival's whole turn, by one-step Medium-like bots on both seats, then scored by the static evaluation (+ 0.05 × the one-step value). Unclamped; a finished game is ±10,000. Reactions: each option over 3 shared worlds, to the end of Hard's next turn, clamped to ±600. Which card to sell is chosen among the 3 most sellable by one rollout each; whether to sell is still the rule.
- **Coach** (`coach.ts:35`): 6 candidates over 6 shared worlds, the same rollout bots, `impact: 40`.

A correction to the earlier review (REVIEW_2026-11-16, "Easy's policy is the fast policy inside every lookahead"): Hard's rollouts are played by Medium-like bots, and Easy's policy is inside every one-step projection: Medium's whole decision, Hard's screen, and every move a rollout bot chooses. So in all of those the rival never answers with a reaction Program.

### The horizon, measured (14 Hard games on the meta decks, 2,424 main-phase rollouts, 546 reaction play-outs)

- A main-phase rollout makes about 11 decisions and crosses exactly one turn boundary. The game ends inside it in 0% of rollouts on turns 1–4, 2.6% on turns 5–9, 44% from turn 10. Overall about 83% are scored by the static evaluation (the verifier, 40 other games and 7,059 rollouts: 85.7%, and every rollout started before turn 7 was static).
- Hard runs with `rolloutWins` off, so a rollout that reaches a decided next-turn check (the seat to move holds 7 Gigs) is still scored statically (6.6% of rollouts, verifier; 3.7%, reviewer), while one that reaches game over returns ±10,000 unclamped: with one world per candidate, a single game-over play-out decides that candidate outright.
- At the cut, the terms that vary most between rollouts are `threat` (SD 111 points: the static next-turn steal estimate), unit power (106), closeness to 7 Gigs (81), Gigs (65), unit bodies (59), Blockers (36).
- Hard's deep pick differs from its one-step pick in 18% of decisions; the median gap between the best and the second option is 3.8 points (0.1 Gig), and 74% of decisions are settled by under half a Gig on one play-out each. That is noise, not bias; paired tuning averages it out (known, BOT-2).
- Reactions late in a game are near coin flips: in a traced turn-12 reaction 7 of 9 world values were clamped to 600, and the three options averaged 535.3, 534.7 and 534.7.

So the only forward signal beyond one turn is the static evaluation at the cut, and the largest forward term in it is one hand-built model of the rival's next-turn steals.

### The evaluation

`DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` (`src/ai/eval.ts:52-81`) is the fitted set `calibrated-2026-11` (`src/ai/weightSets.ts:42-64`): a ridge logistic of the final result on the features at the end of every turn of 4,324 Hard games over 47 decks, fitted on 2026-11-12, before SIM_VERSION 9, with the games played under the hand weights, from the view of the seat that just ended its turn, with no deck controls. Since then: `goSolo` 0 (by argument), `rivalLethal` 400.

| Term | Weight | Immediate or forward | Stands in for |
|---|---|---|---|
| gig, gigNear | 20 (the scale's anchor), 50.8 per Gig over 5 | immediate | the win condition |
| unitPower, unitBase, unitCost, stealsTwo | 13.2, 50.3, −4.81, 42.8 | immediate (a proxy for future fights) | board strength; with deck controls 9.8, 32, ≈0 |
| blocker | −44.1 | residual association | a Blocker beyond what `exposed` credits; −18.9 with deck controls; −3.7 in the SIM 9 refit |
| hand | 4.54 a card | forward | card advantage, by count only; the refit says 21.9 |
| eddie | 26.9 an Eddie | forward | economy; the refit says 52.5 |
| faceUpLegend | 8.99 | forward | Legend abilities and Go Solo |
| plan | 0.915 × `planScore` | forward | Gig values that fit the deck's payoffs (card-text patterns) |
| exposed, threat, rivalLethal | 0.886, 0.971, 400 | forward, one turn | the rival's next-turn steals (`stealableNextTurn`) |
| pkgHold, pkgAssembly, pkgSteer, pkgProtect | 0 | forward | synergy packages: switched off |
| unitGear, legendGear, spentRisk, lowDeck | hand values | — | not identified by the fit |

Because the fit's outcome is the final result, it can price long-term value only for things that are features, and only as the bot that played the games realised it: a resource the bot misuses gets a low weight, which then makes the bot misuse it more. It never saw mid-turn positions, which are all Medium evaluates.

### What the bots cannot see

- **Mana held open.** Paying a cost does not remove Eddies, and the evaluation counts Eddies whether spent or not, so keeping an Eddie up for a trick is worth nothing.
- **The rival's tricks**, in every one-step projection (the Easy defender never plays a reaction Program). Only Hard's three rollouts see a rival use one.
- **Effects that last without power.** "Next time … this turn", "may attack ready Units", cost reductions, "can't ready": no evaluation term; `cantAttack` counts only through the next-turn steal model.
- **The trash and the deck.** Recursion and self-mill are worth nothing until they reach the board.
- **Card quality in hand.** Draw and tutors count as hand size × 4.54.
- **Gig values**, except through `plan` × 0.915 (a few points). In a traced turn-4 decision Industrial Assembly (+4 to a Gig) scored −101 against −106 for passing.
- **Anything after the rival's next turn**, except the static next-turn terms.

### Findings

**B1. Reaction tricks are Eddies to every tier, because no bot keeps Eddies ready for the rival's turn.** New on the mechanism; measured twice (the reviewer's games and the verifier's on other seeds), plus existing data.
- The reviewer: Quick Programs sold in 76% / 88% / 82% of the times a copy was held (Easy / Medium / Hard); Safety Override and Synapse Burnout had no main-phase plays at any tier. The verifier (decision telemetry over the 63 library decks that hold one, 600 Easy, 600 Medium and 150 Hard games): of every use of the 7 Quick Programs, the share that was a sell is 63–99% (Easy), 81–100% (Medium), 64–100% (Hard). The `deep` bot's analysis agrees: Detonate sold in 96–99% of the games it was seen, Synapse Burnout 96–97%, Safety Override 93%.
- The main cause is economic: the bots are rarely able to cast one on the rival's turn. Medium had a Quick Program castable in a reaction window 43 times in 600 games (used 10), Hard 49 times in 150 (used 15: Take Control 5 of 17, Safety Override 5 of 6). When the chance comes, the live reaction code does value them. Behind that: paying a cost does not remove Eddies, and the evaluation counts Eddies whether spent or not, so spending everything in the main phase costs nothing and holding mana open is worth nothing.
- Secondary: every one-step projection uses the Easy agents (`fastReaction` and `simulate()`), which never react with a Program or a quick ability (Goro's Blocker grant, Dum Dum, Overwatch), so an attack into a trick is valued as if there were none; the sell rule acts first on static `cardValue` (a Program is cost × 1.1 + 3).
- Effect: a trick's Δ-when-drawn measures it as sell fodder. The forge cuts on those numbers and the breeder fills on them, so lists drift toward Units, and decks built around Quick Programs are likely under-rated. How much this costs in standings is not measured.

**B2. Combos set up and paid off within a turn are invisible to one step.** New; measured.
- Gunpoint Diplomacy (a main-phase card, not a Quick Program; choom.gg's rating 78): the reviewer saw Medium play it 0 of 30 times offered (Hard 4 of 27, Easy 15 of 33) on the meta decks; the verifier, on 63 library decks and other seeds, saw Medium play it on 14 of 535 turns it was castable (2.6%) and Hard on 5 of 127 (3.9%), and sell it 90% of the time. Its +3 power loses to "attack now", and after the attack the pump is worth nothing (`evalPower`); when behind on Street Cred, the copy's fast rival picks the weaker mode.
- Effect: Medium screening gives such cards no value, so a Hard confirmation never sees them nominated. The forge then cut Gunpoint Diplomacy on its measured figure (S3-03, +4.6 on Hard), which is right for the bot and says nothing about the card.

**B3. No bot ever assembles a package, and scarce pieces are sold more on v9 than on v5.** Known that the weights are 0; the v9 numbers are new.
- Hard, v9, 490 paired games over 47 lists: when a scarce package piece was sellable, Hard sold it in 364 of 624 sells (58%; v5: 55%). The sell lookahead added in v7 did not help.
- `pkgHold` 8: the rate falls to 46%, and the games score about 0 (+1.2 [−1.7, +4.2] on 161; −0.6 [−2.3, +1.1] on 329). `pkgAssembly` 6: +0.6 [−2.3, +3.5]. At the fitted scale (40/20/20/8, Medium, 71 decks) standings did not move beyond chance (3 of 71 decks; Spearman 0.968). So the weights are not the lever.
- `pkgAssembly` counts hand, play and Legends only (`src/ai/packages.ts:187`), so using a one-shot enabler (a Program, a sacrificed Gear) lowers the credit at the moment it pays off; the experiment that was meant to test assembly partly measures a hoarding penalty, and its null result is weaker evidence than it looks.
- The lever is the horizon: score the hand at the cut by what it holds (hold credit), or extend sell decisions to the end of the seller's next turn.

**B4. A bug: the Gig plan steers decks built around an even Street Cred toward an odd one.** New; checked by the coordinator.
- `PLAN_PATTERNS.parity` (`src/ai/knowledge.ts:95`, `/even value|odd value|even number|is an even/`) is meant for cards that want one even and one odd Gig (Bootleg Black Sapphire Show, Jackie Welles). It also matches Field Operator, Memory Relapse and Pacifica Netrunner ("if your ☆ (Street Cred) is an even number"). `planScore` then rewards holding an even Gig and an odd Gig (`eval.ts:218-232`). Street Cred is the sum of your Gig values (glossary, CR 11.2), so with two Gigs, one even and one odd always make an odd Street Cred.
- All 11 library decks built around `cred-even` get a parity weight of 0.68–1.40, and their plan prefers Gigs {2,3} (Street Cred 5) over {2,4} (6) by 1.2–2.5 evaluation points. The plan decides roll-in, steal, adjust and swap choices, not only scores them.
- Effect: Even Street Cred is the package the tuner's held-out games favour most often (S2-04's adopted move into it: +8.9 claimed, +4.8 re-measured; S2-05's: +6.1 and +4.0), and it is played by a bot nudged against it. Its measured lift is −8.1, so the coach hides it. Its value is probably understated everywhere.
- Fix: a separate pattern for Street Cred parity that `planScore` maps to an even Street Cred; better, derive the Gig plan from the catalogue's `online` conditions so one definition serves both. A bump.

One hand-built next-turn model, and one fitted association weight, each reorder the decks.** New; measured twice, by the reviewer and by the verifier on other seeds (Medium round robin of all 71 decks, every ordered pair, 2 deals, the same deals for every setting, both seats changed; 9,940 games a setting; a deck's paired SE about 2 to 2.6 points).

- The direction and the correlation hold on both runs; the size of the next-turn effect is smaller on the verifier's seeds (14 decks, −3 rather than −6 points for Blocker-heavy decks), so read it as "10 to 20 decks, a few points each". Blocker count and archetype are partly confounded and were not separated.
- For scale: two independent base runs at 140 games a deck agree at Spearman 0.895, and Medium against Hard is 0.899 (known). The bot's evaluation choices move deck standings about as much as the choice of tier does.
- Effect: the forge, the optimizer and the gate measure "good for this bot's evaluation". A change adopted under the current weights can flip after a refit, and changes that add or cut Blockers are the most exposed; Sim-tier letters near a band edge will move with any refit. Re-rate after a weight change, and do not carry old adoption verdicts across it.

**B6. The matchup profiles could make the objective jump, but on Medium they barely matter.** New; computed, read in code, and measured by the verifier.
- `profiles.tuned.json` was tuned on the hand weights (its only commit is 2026-11-10) and now multiplies the fitted weights (`src/ai/archetype.ts:74-80`; checked), for both seats, by each deck's archetype, which is a threshold classifier (`archetype.ts:36-37`).
- In 37 to 40 of 71 decks a single one-card swap can change the archetype (the reviewer 40, the verifier 37; median distance to the nearest threshold 0.15, the closest deck 0.005). A flip changes both seats' weights in profiled matchups.
- But profiles on against off moved only 5 of 71 decks beyond 2 SE (chance about 3), none beyond 3 SE, Spearman 0.988. So the flip is close to harmless on Medium. Not tested on Hard. Low priority: drop the profiles at the next bump only if a Hard check agrees.

**B7. The evaluation is a stale association, and the next refit is parked behind a bundled bump.** Partly known.
- Fitted on pre-v9 games played under the hand weights. The 11-18 refit is within 0.029 log-loss overall but far off on single terms: blocker −3.7 against −44.1, hand 21.9 against 4.5, eddie 52.5 against 26.9.
- `spentRisk` applies to the active seat's spent Units (`eval.ts:365`); at Hard's leaf the active seat is the decider, whose Units are about to ready: a small penalty for a risk that has passed (new).
- Sub-choices rank Units by the hand weights (`heuristic.ts:1171-1175`: a Blocker +2) while the evaluation scores the same Blocker −44.1 (new).
- `hand` at 4.54 against a Unit's ~60 points makes the one-step screen nearly always prefer dumping the hand onto the board.

**B8. Consistency between the bots that pick, measure and confirm.** Medium screens, Hard confirms, `deep` filled the tables. Medium's blind spots (B1, B2) decide which nominees reach Hard, so Hard cannot rescue a trick or a combo. Measured in the ledger: Hard's held-out gain is 0.30 [−0.02, 0.59] of the Medium screening figure (5 changes; new), and 0.75 ± 0.19 of a Medium held-out gain (known, P6).

**B9. Medium misjudges whole deck families, and the misjudgement is stable.** New; measured (§8: all 71 decks, 568 games a deck under each of Easy, Medium and Hard on the same deals).
- Medium against Hard: Spearman 0.854; 32 decks change win rate beyond noise; only 6 of Medium's top 10 are Hard's top 10.
- Each deck's Hard-minus-Medium change is a property of the list: split-half reliability 0.87, a true spread across decks of about 5.3 points; it replicates the 11-18 run on other deals deck by deck (BBG Cunningham Econ +11.1 then, +10.5 now; Ladder: V / Smasher / Padre −6.8, −8.4).
- What gains under Hard: control lists with few Units, many Programs and much interaction, above all the BBG Jackie/Alt/Hanako and Cunningham family (+10.8 ± 3.3 points against the rest, 7 decks). Across decks the change correlates −0.40 with Unit count, +0.37 with interaction, +0.30 with Programs. ## 3. Tuning: short-term screening, long-term confirmation

### The chain (the forge; the others differ as the table shows)

1. The objective is fixed before the run: the 7 meta decks (`scripts/forge.ts:185`), the Medium bot (`:132`), game *k* plays `field[k % 7]` on seed `seed0 + k`, seat alternating every 7 games; win 1, tie ½. Every comparison is paired game for game against the current list.
2. A baseline with telemetry gives each card's Δ-when-drawn.
3. Candidates (`forgeSwapsExplained`, `scripts/builder-moves.ts:66-128`): cuts are the 3 lowest Δ-when-drawn cards **from the same baseline games**; adds are +1 of the list's 2 best own cards plus the 4 best legal cards by `GLOBAL_IMPACT` (the `deep` bot's analysis of the 7 meta decks); cut × add pairs ranked by add − cut; the top `cands` kept.
4. Screening on the baseline's own seeds; optional successive halving, combinations.
5. Nomination: the best change whose screening gain is at least +2 points (`NOMINATE_BAR`, `forge.ts:197`); with the ledger, changes whose earlier held-out games reject them are skipped.
6. Confirmation (`confirmStaged`, `src/lib/evidence.ts:510`): fresh seeds, stages of 2 × GAMES, up to 4; O'Brien–Fleming z 4.33 / 2.96 / 2.36 / 2.01; adopt when the lower bound is above +1 point with 200+ games, reject when the upper bound is below +1; futility after 2 stages at a mean of 0 or less. With ledger priors, every look uses the always-valid z; with until-settled, an undecided nominee goes on alone.
7. Adoption replaces the list; the next generation starts from it.
8. In a season, the round robin follows with Hard against the meta, 8 incumbents and the newcomers; ratings later come from Hard over the whole library.

## 5. Long-term numbers: ratings, tiers, matchups, the forecast

### What each number is

- **Rating** (`src/lib/ratingModel.ts`): Bradley–Terry with a going-first term, P(A beats B, A first) = σ(rA − rB + h); a tie counts half; a Gaussian prior of SD 1 on each rating (`:45`, checked), none on h; ratings re-centred to sum to 0; covariance from the full Hessian. Today h = 0.309: going first is worth +7.7 points.
- **Win rate against the field** (`expectedVs`): the unweighted mean of σ(rk − rj) over every other fitted deck. The field is every deck with a game on its current list: today exactly the 71 library decks, 30 of which this trainer made. Because ratings sum to 0, the field's mean is exactly 50%: the number is relative to the library's own average.
- **Head-to-heads** (`src/lib/h2hModel.ts`): the seat-averaged prediction + (W − E)/(g + m), where m = 1/ρ − 1 from a moment estimate on the same fit's residuals (`:64-77`, checked), capped at 1,000. Today ρ = 0.0032, m = 308; the median pair has 6 games.
- **Pooling** (`scripts/event-pool.ts`): only the newest simulator version and bot; a game counts only when both lists are the library's current ones.
- **Sim tier** (`src/lib/simTier.ts`): fixed bands on win rate against the field (S ≥ 64, A ≥ 55, B ≥ 45, C ≥ 36), "±" only when the 90% range reaches two bands away.
- **Upsets**: one fit on earlier events with the same version and bot; judged at 18+ earlier games each; an upset at a winner's chance of 0.30 or less.
- **Rating series**: every deck's half-width at most ±4 (`automation.width`); the widest deck plays 10 games against its least-met opponent; refit every 300 games; at most 12,000 games a run.
- **Parent strength**: see §4 step 3.
- **Forecast** (`src/lib/forecast.ts`): pair chances from the h2h model with parameter draws; Bo3, loser goes first, no draws; the default field is uniform over the 71 lists.

### Findings

**L1. Matchups differ beyond the ratings, and the shrinkage throws that away because its estimator is biased toward zero.** New; computed.
- `fitShrinkage` measures each pair's spread around predictions from a fit to the same games, which absorbs residual variance; nothing corrects for it (the evidence builder applies a P/(P−K) correction to the same quantity, `scripts/evidence-build.ts:613`).
- Parametric null (simulate every game from the fit, refit): ρ̂ has median −0.0059 over 30 runs (reviewer); −0.0048 over 150 runs, 90% band −0.0119 to +0.0017 (verifier). Measured against the true ratings without refitting, the median is +0.0004, so the bias comes from the refit. The observed +0.0032 is reached by only 3.3% of no-spread runs: the data do show real pair spread. Adding pair effects of logit SD τ and refitting, τ ≈ 0.20 reproduces it: about 4.4–4.8 points SD of real matchup deviation, and a prior weight of about 100–115 games rather than 308. τ is poorly pinned (anywhere from 0.1 to 0.25, a prior weight of 70 to 400 games). Pearson X² = 2,362 on about 2,135 df. On 2026-11-18 ρ̂ was ≤ 0 and clamped at the cap, which is where FORECAST.md's "pairs spread no more than chance" came from: an artefact of the bias.
- Today's effect of the estimator alone is small: at 6.4 games a pair, a prior of 115 instead of 308 moves the shrunk pair estimates by 0.6 points RMS (2.8 at most) and does not change the breeder's counter picks. What it gets wrong is the conclusion that there are no matchups; the forecast reuses `fitShrinkage` (`forecast.ts:35`) and so inherits it.
- The evidence page's leave-one-event-out calibration slope is 0.79 ± 0.05: held-out predictions are overconfident.
- The bigger issue is the design: the series picks the least-met opponent on purpose, spreading games thin (median 6 a pair), so matchup effects cannot be learned. A matchup block among the top 16 (120 pairs × about 100 games, 12,000 games, about 25 minutes at run 1's pace) would show 4–5 point effects where breeding and the forecast care.
- The forecast's ranges include parameter uncertainty only. With τ ≈ 0.2, each matchup carries about 4.7 points of spread the ranges leave out: it averages out against a broad field (about 0.6 points over 70 opponents) but not in a narrow field or a top-cut bracket (verifier).

**L2. The Sim tier looks certain but is not.** New; computed.
- 54 of 71 decks' 90% ranges cross a band edge, and none carries "±": the mark needs a range two bands away, which cannot happen at ±4 with bands 9–10 points wide (the rule was set when ranges were 15–24 points).
- Adding run 1's 9,900 games moved 21 of 71 letters while only 6 of 71 new estimates fell outside the old 90% ranges: the intervals are honest, the letters churn because many decks sit near an edge.
- `SIM_TIER_BANDS` (`simTier.ts:18`) and `TIER_BOUNDARIES` (`ratingModel.ts:261`) are two copies of the same four numbers with no test tying them.

**L3. The Sim tier depends on the field's make-up**, though its header says a letter "means the same expected win rate however the field changes". New; computed.

So the order is robust and the letters are not: every bred or imported deck added raises everyone's letter, and pruning weak decks lowers them. The field also counts near-duplicates separately (55 Legend trios among 71 decks; Smasher in 34 of 71).

**L4. Parent strength mixes two scales and penalises decks with few games.** The blend is partly known (TUNING_STUDY §5); the sizes are new.
- The blend adds 0.35 × a leaderboard rating (win rate against the meta, shrunk toward 50% over 60 games, `season.ts:287-289`) to 0.65 × the conservative win rate against the library. Only 8 rows are current today (all Season 3's). The reviewer's "the meta figure averages 5 points below" was refuted by the verifier (across the 36 decks with both it is 5.3 points *above*; for the 8 current rows 1.0 below the library figure and 1.0 above the conservative one). What holds is the mechanism: a few decks get a figure on another field, shrunk toward 50%, which compresses the top end.
- It changes a decision: Ladder: V / Smasher / Padre (RRG) drops from 2nd to 5th parent (conservative 65.1, leaderboard 61.1, blend 63.7), which takes it out of the breeder's four bosses (Dexter and Ladder S1 #5, #2 and #3 take the places) and makes it a counter candidate instead. The weaker S3 decks gain 0 to 4 places.
- The lower-bound penalty is 7.8 points at 20 games, 5.0 at 60, 3.2 at 150, about 2 at 400. Today every deck has 301–446 games, so it hardly matters; a new deck at 20–60 games is ranked 3–6 points below an equal deck with 400.
- Among ranks 2 to 10 the gaps are 0.003–0.07 logit against a gap SD of about 0.155: P(correct order) 0.51–0.68. Parent choice below #1 is largely a coin flip, which the blend then perturbs.

**L5. After a forge edit or a bump, old ratings are used silently or the deck vanishes.** Read in code.
- A one-card edit leaves the deck unrated until the next series run: no parent strength, no Sim tier, silently left out of the forecast's uniform field (it lists only h2h decks), no strength finding; about 400 games on a near-identical list are thrown away.
- After a bump, `parentStrength`, `strengthOf`, the Sim tier, the forecast and the anchored "Your decks" ratings never check `ratings.json`'s version (only evidence and leaderboard rows do). A season run in that window breeds from stale parents with no warning. The series re-rates everything in one to two daily runs; if an event is recorded on the new version first, the pool switches to that event alone (about 23 games a deck, ±13 points).

**L6. The prior is about twice too wide.** New; computed, and confirmed by the verifier (EM refit: 0.511). Empirical Bayes: var(r̂) 0.270 and mean se² 0.025 give a prior SD of 0.50 against the 1 the fit uses. Negligible at 400 games (refitting with 0.5 moves no deck more than 1.4 points and changes no letter); for decks at 20–60 games (a new deck, a backtest fold, the upset yardstick fitted on 1–4 events) the estimates are about 1.5 times too spread, and the upset threshold and its 18-game minimum were tuned under that fit.

**L7. The forecast backtest no longer runs, and never used the live model's data.** New; checked. It stops on event 25 ("not a plain Swiss event"); it reads events only (no rating runs, no list gate), while the live forecast reads `h2h.json`, which includes run 1 (70% of the games) and the gate. The published Brier skill belongs to a different model from the one on the page.

**L8. The upset yardstick and the raw head-to-head counts read other game sets than the ratings.** New. The upset fit passes events, never runs (`src/lib/kb/facts.ts:92`), with no list gate; `forEachGameOf` does not skip played events while `eventRatedGames` does (latent: no played event is in the pool today).

**L9. The series' target is chosen for letters and capped by a page-size budget.** New. What breeding and the forecast need is the order at the top and matchups among strong decks; ±4 everywhere leaves the order among #2–#10 unresolved and pairs at 6–15 games. `siteConfig.ts:94-97` says a width of 3 would break the site search's first-search budget, so a statistical target is tied to a page-size limit. `planExtraGames` already supports weighted objectives: weight the top and the top-16 pairs.

**L10. Events as a rating sample are fine.** Swiss pairing and "loser goes first" depend only on observed results, so the likelihood is valid: simulated coverage 88.0% against a nominal 90%. Titles, top 8s and average finish are Swiss luck with no interval.

**L11. The forecast's ranges are too narrow.** The pair uncertainty is about 2.6 points with m = 308; the real spread is about 4.4 (L1), and the 0.79 calibration slope is not applied (L6), before the admitted human-play gap. The default field is uniform over 71 lists, 42% trainer-made, nothing like a regional.

**S1. The measured lift is mostly a proxy for the game state, and it contradicts the paired games.** New pattern; the association caveat is known.
- Pooled v9 lift: 8 packages above 0, 8 below, 7 unsettled. The sign follows what the `online` condition marks: conditions that mark winning ("payoff in play", high Gigs) are positive; conditions that mark losing (behind on Gigs, Units in the trash, low Street Cred, Blockers up) are negative. It does not follow assembly difficulty.
- The "never" slice is thin exactly when a package is reliably online, so the best-integrated packages cannot pass the coach's gate.

**S2. The catalogue is too broad for "protect the pieces" to discriminate.** New. Across the 22 forged lists, 70% of main-deck copies are pieces of some held package; lists are built around 1–8 packages each. **S3. Seven definitions of "the package is there", four answers to "which packages".** New. Report "assembled" (pieces ever drawn), report "online" (the condition), evaluation "assembled" (in hand, play or Legends), `holdCredit` (trash excluded), cut ranking "partner present" (drawn at any point), `holdOdds` (by turn 4, no mulligan), the Gig plan (card text, outside the catalogue). A forged list loses its note mapping (the fingerprint changes).

**S4. ### D. How decisions are made

1. **A method change lands with a measurement, or says it has none.** The 11-11 to 11-18 stretch shows how it works when done (measured, pinned, a rule written first); the 11-22 batch shows the cost when not (two unmeasured gains described as measured, a guarantee quietly undone). A commit that changes a deck tool's or a bot's behaviour names its measurement or "unmeasured, opt-in".
2. **A register of the constants with provenance**: the table §7 summarises, kept as a file with each value's simulator version, bot, date and source, and a `check:data` line that lists the constants measured on another version than `SIM_VERSION`.
3. **Every evaluation or bot change ships the per-deck sensitivity report** beside the strength figure (15): strength alone hides which decks a change favours.
4. **One measured change per bump** (16): bumps now cost about a day of the series' machine time, so bundling is no longer the cheaper path.
5. **Tests that pin a statistical output state their tolerance and why** (the forecast round trip widened to 2e-3, a seed changed to keep a "sharp" rating): so the next widening is a decision, not a fix.
6. **The series' unattended commits report what moved**: letters changed, the top-8 order, parent ranks; a large move waits for a person.

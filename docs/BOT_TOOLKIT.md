# Bot toolkit — how the practice bots think

This is the reference for what the bots know, which tools they have, and how they decide. Game terms are defined in the in-app **Glossary** (`src/content/glossary.ts`), with Comprehensive Rules references.

## 1. Perception (what a bot may look at)

| Information | Own | Rival |
|---|---|---|
| Hand | ✔ | count only. Lookahead clones re-deal the rival's hand at random from their unseen cards |
| Deck order | ✘ (clones shuffle it) | ✘ |
| Face-down Legends | ✘ unless revealed by an effect (`known`), e.g. Kiroshi Optics. Clones shuffle the identities of the ones not looked at | ✘. Clones shuffle their identities |
| Field, Gigs, fixer dice, Eddies, trash, face-up Legends | ✔ | ✔ |
| Own deck list (for the Gig plan) | ✔ every card it owns, wherever it is (since SIM_VERSION 9) | Only the public cards: field, trash and face-up Legends |
| Deck archetype (for the matchup profile) | ✔ the whole list | ✔ the whole list, "scouted" as from open decklists |

## 2. Tools (engine API the bots and cards share)

Everything goes through `Game` (`src/engine/engine.ts`):

- **Turn actions:**
  - `legalActions(p)` returns sell, call, play, goSolo, activate, attack and pass.
  - `legalReactions(p)` returns call, block, quick program, quick ability and endReact.
- **Simulation:**
  - `clone(agents)` makes a deep copy of the state for lookahead.
  - `doAction`, `doReaction`, `resolvePending`, `mainLoop`, `endPhase` and `startPhase` let the bot play out part of a turn or a whole turn inside a clone.
- **Combat math:**
  - `power(c)` includes Gear, auras, mods and "while attacking" effects.
  - `fightOutcome(a, b)` applies win/lose overrides such as Johnny vs CORPO.
  - `stealCount(att)` is floor(P/10)+1, adjusted by Take Control, Gorilla Arms and similar.
  - `attackTargets(c)` covers Lag, Adrenaline, Go Solo, ready-unit permissions, and bans such as Ruthless Lowlife and MaxTac Suppression.
- **Economy:**
  - `playCost(uid)` returns the cost after discounts (min 1) and taxes such as Riot Shield.
  - `available(p)` counts ready Eddies plus ready Legends.
  - `pay()` auto-pays in this order: Eddies, then Legends carrying spend-trigger Gear, then face-down Legends, then face-up Legends without {Spend} abilities. A Legend paying for its own Go Solo is spent first (it enters ready anyway); one paying for its own ordinary play (`playLegend`) is spent last (it would enter spent).
- **Gig state:** `gigsOf`, `streetCred`, `lessCred` / `moreCred`, `valuePairs`, `distinctValues`.

## 3. Evaluation function (`src/ai/eval.ts`)

The evaluation scores a position from one player's view: ours minus the rival's.

| Term | Weight (fitted; hand value until SIM_VERSION 5) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gig difference | 20 per Gig (20) | Gigs are the win condition: 7 at the start of your turn wins |
| Near-win bonus | 50.8 per Gig above 5 (45) | 6 Gigs means one steal from winning |
| Overtime | Gig difference counted twice | 7 Gigs wins instantly in overtime |
| Units on board | power×13.2 − cost×4.81 + 50.3, Blocker −44.1, Go Solo 0 (fitted −50.6, set to 0 in SIM_VERSION 8), Gear +1.5 each, 10+ power +42.8 (power×1.2 + cost×1.2 + 2, Blocker +2, Go Solo +2, Gear +1.5, 10+ power +6) | Board presence; at 10+ power a Unit steals 2 Gigs. Power that lasts only this turn counts only where it can still matter this turn (below) |
| Spent Units the rival can beat | ×(1 − 0.087), for the player whose turn it is | Attacking leaves a Unit exposed to a counter-attack |
| Cards in hand | 4.54 each, counts up to 10 (3.6) | Card advantage |
| Eddies | 26.9 each, counts up to 10 (2.6) | Permanent mana |
| Face-up Legends | 8.99 each (4.8), plus 2 per Gear on any Legend | Unlocks abilities and passives |
| Gig plan | 0.915 × plan score (1.5) | Rewards dice that match the deck's payoffs |
| Low deck | −8 per card under 4 | Deck-out avoidance |
| Next turn (BOT-3, below) | exposed 0.886, threat 0.971, rival lethal 400 (all 0) | What the side about to move can steal |

**Since SIM_VERSION 6 (2026-11-12) the weights are fitted to who won** (`DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` = the named set `calibrated-2026-11` + `rivalLethal` 400; INFO-4 below and docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D1 / WP-D2). Terms the fit could not identify (spent risk, Gear, Legend Gear, low deck) keep their hand values. The fitted values are an association with winning on the gig scale, not a design: a Blocker scores below a plain Unit because such boards won less often. The fit gave a Legend gone solo −50.6 the same way, but going solo costs what the ordinary Legend play costs and makes the same Unit, only ready and free to attack this turn (11.29.1; the two differ later only if the Unit returns to the Legends area, which no card does), so the weight was a pure bias toward the worse play: the bots played Legends to the field with Lag (Medium 296 times against 29 Go Solos in 257 games). **Since SIM_VERSION 8 `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS.goSolo` is 0** (BOT-A); the named set `calibrated-2026-11` keeps its fitted −50.6.

**This turn's power (BOT-B, SIM_VERSION 8).** `unitValue` counts a Unit's power with `evalPower`: a power mod that ends this turn or with this attack counts only while it can still change something this turn (the Unit can still attack, a Unit of the active player can still attack it, it is a ready Blocker while the active player can still attack, or it is in the attack in progress); otherwise the Unit counts at its lasting power. Before, a +3 on a Unit that had already attacked was worth about 35 points and a −4 on a rival Unit nothing could reach about 50, more than the card spent (4.54), so the 1-ply screen played such pumps and weakens for nothing (Hard did it after all its attacks in the logs). The credit is kept where it matters, so a fight trick before the attack it enables still scores. `evaluateTerms` uses the same power (`tests/eval-terms.test.ts`). The fast policy (Easy, and the sub-choices and reaction clones inside every lookahead) keeps the hand weights (`FAST_WEIGHTS` in `src/ai/heuristic.ts`), because its thresholds are written in those units; the switch was measured with exactly that split. Before that the weights were tuned by self-play (`scripts/tune.ts`): each weight is tried at ×0.5 and ×1.6 against the baseline across every meta pairing. Two early rounds (before the November 2026 fixes, when a variant was adopted at a 54% win rate) halved the Gig, unit-power and spent-risk weights, then raised face-up Legends and halved spent risk again. Since 2026-11-11 a variant is adopted in training only when the lower bound of its paired 95% interval is above zero, and a candidate is applied only when its held-out lower bound clears 1 point (`scripts/tuning.ts`). The re-tune for the fresh baseline (2026-11-11, `--seed 1`, defaults: 168 games per evaluation) adopted unitPower ×1.6 in training, but the held-out check gave +0.6 points [−3.3, +4.5] over 168 games, so nothing was applied (report `reports/tune-weights-1-2026-11-11T21-15-00-176Z.json`). Those hand weights are the named set `hand-2026-10` in `src/ai/weightSets.ts`.

**Tuning the bot that plays events (BOT-7, 2026-11-12).** The default `tune.ts` invocation still means what it did: `--bot plain` (the 1-ply lookahead bot with noise 0.15 and no matchup profiles or card impact, i.e. Medium without its profiles) and `--format unpaired`. `--bot hard` makes both seats Hard (`HARD_SPEC` in `scripts/agent-spec.ts`: Hard's tier settings; at BOT-7's run deep 3, impact 40, reaction rollouts 3, the tuned profiles; since SIM_VERSION 7 deep 3, reaction rollouts 3, sell lookahead 3, the plain mulligan, the tuned profiles, no card impact) with only the weights differing; `--format paired` plays every deal twice with the configs swapped (botcheck's duplicate format, `duplicateSummary`) and adopts a variant only when its paired interval's lower bound is above 50%, then compares the final weights with the starting ones on the held-out seed window. `--keys` picks the weights searched (default `TUNED_WEIGHTS`), `--next-turn` adds `exposed`, `threat` and `rivalLethal` without making them part of `TUNED_WEIGHTS`, `--start '<json>'` sets the starting weights, `--mults` the multiples tried. The report goes to `--out` (default `reports/`), never over an existing file without `--force`; `--dry-run` writes nothing.

**Calibration against results (INFO-4, `scripts/eval-calibrate.ts`).** `evaluateTerms()` returns `evaluate()`'s value together with the unweighted feature of every linear term (`EVAL_TERMS`; `evaluate()` is Σ weight × feature plus the package terms, `tests/eval-terms.test.ts`). `npx tsx scripts/eval-calibrate.ts [--decks all] [--per 2] [--view ender|next|both] [--stages] [--from FILE] [--out DIR]` plays seeded Hard games, records the features at the end of every turn from both seats' view (worker job `evalLeaves`), and reports the win rate per bin of the current value, a one-parameter logistic σ(a·value), a ridge logistic on the features (`src/lib/logistic.ts`, λ chosen on validation games, held-out games for the scores), and the fitted weights next to the hand weights on the gig scale with a candidate set (`src/analysis/evalCalibration.ts`). Named weight sets live in `src/ai/weightSets.ts` and are picked in a bot spec with `"weightSet": "<name>"` (or `"weights": "<name>"`); since SIM_VERSION 6 `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` is `calibrated-2026-11` + `rivalLethal` 400, and `hand-2026-10` gives the old hand weights for comparisons. Results: docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D2 and WP-D1 (status 2026-11-12, INFO-4 / BOT-7).

### Package terms (`src/ai/packages.ts`, docs/SYNERGIES.md "How scoring uses packages")

Four more weights credit the synergy packages the player's **own** list is built around (the deck objective's `synergies` when named for that exact list, else every package `synergiesIn` marks built-around; computed once per list fingerprint and cached outside the state). They are **0 by default**: with all four at 0 the bots play exactly as before (the golden replays, and `tests/package-scoring.test.ts`). They are not in `TUNED_WEIGHTS`; `scripts/package-scoring.ts` measures them.

| Weight | Where it counts | What it credits |
|---|---|---|
| `pkgHold` | sell ranking, card choices (discard/trash/bottom-deck own cards, search and free-play picks), Maman Brigitte's discard | a piece in hand whose partner the player can still get (hand, play, Legends, deck): 1 per `requires` package (½ `helps`), divided by the copies still obtainable that do the same job, at most 2 |
| `pkgAssembly` | `evaluate()` | per package, enabler and payoff (or two members) available together in hand, play or the Legends: 1 (`helps` ½) |
| `pkgSteer` | `evaluate()`, and the Gig choices: roll-in die, steal, swap, adjust/set value, Kerry's reroll | for each steerable quantity of a package with a payoff available (Street Cred below / above the rival's, even Street Cred, min Gig, 8+ Gig, value-pair, mixed values), how far the own Gigs are toward it, in [−1, 1] |
| `pkgProtect` | `evaluate()` | per member in play (at most 3) of a `requires` package assembled in play: losing one costs this |

Only the deciding player's own packages count: inside lookahead clones the rival seat's bots get no package weights (`withoutPackages`), since the bot does not know the rival's list. The roll-in die choice (always the first option before) and Maman Brigitte's discard (`yesno/discardFor`) go through the package terms only when their weight is set. `mode/mode` is unchanged: no package gives a clear reason to pick a mode differently.

**How to measure one.** `npx tsx scripts/package-scoring.ts --out <scratch dir> --configs "name:pkgHold=8;…" [--phase train|heldout] [--per N] [--shadow-games M] [--bot hard|deep]` plays every library deck built around a package against every meta deck, weights on vs off on the same seeds, seats and opponents (opponent: the same bot, weights off). It prints the paired gain overall, per family, package and deck, the apply gate, how many of the deck's decisions the weights change (`--shadow-games`: a weights-off shadow bot is asked at every decision in the same state and random stream), and how often a scarce package piece was sold or discarded while its partner was obtainable. A weight that changes no decision is not worth measuring further.

**Measured 2026-11-11 (SIM_VERSION 5, Hard; `reports/package-scoring-2026-11-11.md`):** in training (546 paired games each) `pkgHold` 8 −0.5 points [−2.1, +1.0], `pkgAssembly` 6 +1.1 [−0.9, +3.1], `pkgSteer` 6 −2.0 [−4.4, +0.4], `pkgProtect` 3 −0.4 [−2.1, +1.4], all four +0.2 [−3.3, +3.7]. Each changes decisions (47, 36, 154 and 43 of about 3,400 in 80 shadowed games; `pkgSteer` changes a quarter of the roll-in choices). Held-out, `pkgAssembly` 6 gave −1.6 [−3.2, +0.0] over 1,092 games, so nothing was applied and every package weight stays 0. `pkgHold` 8 cuts sells of a scarce piece whose partner is obtainable from 55% to 40% of the sells where one was in hand, with no measurable gain.

### Next-turn terms (BOT-3, `nextTurnTerms` in `src/ai/eval.ts`)

The rest of the evaluation is static: it can't see what the next turn will do. Three more weights look one turn ahead from public information. With all three at 0 `evaluate()` never computes them. Since SIM_VERSION 6 the defaults are exposed 0.886, threat 0.971 (the fitted set) and rivalLethal 400 (BOT-3; fitted set + 400 against the set alone 52.0% [51.0, 52.9]); they are not in `TUNED_WEIGHTS`; override them in a `botcheck` / worker `AgentSpec` with `"weights": {...}`.

How the next turn works (engine): a player's Units ready only at that player's start, and lag ends with every turn, so every Unit a player has on the field now can attack on its next turn (unless an attack restriction bans it, or a "can't ready" outlasts that start keeps a spent one spent), while the defender's Units stay as they are now: only Units that are ready now and have Blocker can block, each one attack. An unblocked attack on the Gig area steals ⌊power/10⌋+1 Gigs. 7+ Gigs at the start of your own turn wins.

Whose turn is next (`nextTurnSeat`): the other seat during a turn (the 1-ply screen evaluates in our own main phase; a reaction in the rival's), the active seat once an end phase has run (the end of a deep play-out, where our turn is next).

| Weight | When | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| `exposed` | the rival's turn is next | − `exposed` × the Gig swing (the gig, near-win and overtime terms of `evaluate()`) of the Gigs the rival can steal (`stealableNextTurn`: each rival Unit's steals, minus the biggest one per ready Blocker of ours, capped at our Gigs) |
| `threat` | our turn is next | + `threat` × the Gig swing of the Gigs we can steal, the same estimate the other way round (so `exposed` = `threat` keeps the evaluation zero-sum) |
| `rivalLethal` | the rival's turn is next and it holds 7+ Gigs (not in overtime, where 7 ends the game at once) | − `rivalLethal` points when our turn is over, or when it isn't but our Units that can attack the rival's Gigs now can't steal it below 7 past its ready Blockers |

Being a share of the Gig swing, `exposed` and `threat` are on the evaluation's own scale: 0.5 counts an open steal as half of a stolen Gig. The estimate ignores Units still to be played (they lag), unblockable attackers, steal modifiers and attacks on spent Units (that is `spentRisk`). Since SIM_VERSION 8 it counts each Unit at its power on that next turn (power mods that end this turn, with this attack, or at that seat's start left out; BOT-B) and skips a Unit whose "can't attack" lasts into that turn (Chrome Reverie, Nocturne's mode; BOT-C). Measured: see docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D1 (status 2026-11-12, BOT-3/BOT-4).

## 4. Gig plan (`src/ai/knowledge.ts → gigPlanOf`)

The bot counts how many of its deck's cards reference each pattern:

- **high:** 8+, increase, max Gig. Mostly Red: Industrial Assembly, Carnage, Octant, V Roamer.
- **low:** min Gig, decrease. Mostly Blue: Trust No One, Three Mouths, Pyramid Song, Alt Cunningham.
- **pairs:** value-pair. Green Arasaka: Hanako, Pepe, Sandayu, Goro.
- **diverse / parity:** different values, even/odd. Yellow: Bootleg, Faceplate, Jackie Ride or Die.
- **d20:** Over the Edge, Kerry.

The plan drives which die to roll in, which Gig to steal, and every adjust, set or swap choice. For an adjust, the bot scores every `(die, new value)` pair as `planScore(mine) − planScore(rival)` and also considers "no change".

Where the cards come from (`planFor` in `src/ai/eval.ts`): a seat's own plan is the plan of every card it owns, wherever the card is (`ownedCards`: deck, hand, field, Legends, Eddies, trash, removed, a Program resolving, attached Gear), which is its whole list at every moment of the game; the rival's plan is read from the cards the viewer can see (field, trash, face-up Legends) on every call. The archetype that picks the matchup profile (`seatArchetype`, read by `matchupRead`, the coach's tips and `evalCalibration`) is the whole list's, for both seats. Until SIM_VERSION 8 both were read from the cards in the seat's zones and cached at first use: the own plan on the game object, the archetypes on the agent. So once cards were sold, the live game, its lookahead copies, an Undo copy and a fresh rollout agent or the coach could score one decision with different plans or profiles, and a game continued from a copy did not play out as the game continued in place. Since SIM_VERSION 9 they all read the same values (`tests/gig-plan-consistency.test.ts`; measured in §6).

## 5. Decision procedures (`src/ai/heuristic.ts`)

1. **Mulligan.** Keep the hand only if it has a Unit costing ≤4, three cards costing ≤4, and something to sell. The mulligan costs nothing (a new hand of 6), so a deck's mulligan rate is just the chance its opening hand fails the rule: from about 6% (Rainbow Fixer Stuff) to 27% (RRY Smasher Aggro) for the meta decks (1,200 games each in the 2026-11-11 baseline), almost all of it from having no Unit costing ≤4. BBG Cunningham Econ is at 22%: it has 9 such Units in 40 cards, the fewest.
   - With card impact and `mulligan: 'rule'` (the coach until 2026-11-13; Hard until SIM_VERSION 5) the bot also keeps a hand holding 2+ high-impact cards (Δ when drawn ≥ 0.05) and 2+ cards costing ≤4, which brings Hard's mulligan rate to 10.5% over the meta decks (plain rule 16.6%).
   - **Measured (BOT-6, 2026-11-12, `scripts/mulligan-check.ts`).** Every deal is played twice, the studied seat forced to keep and forced to mulligan, so both outcomes of every opening hand are known and any rule can be scored offline on the same hands. Hard vs Hard, meta decks, 4,200 hands: in the hands Hard keeps, mulliganing gains +0.2 points [−1.8, 2.2]; in those it mulligans, +4.3 [−1.4, 10.0]. So the rule's main split is right. The exception is the card-impact keep: on the 254 hands it keeps and the plain rule would mulligan, mulliganing was worth **+11.0 points [3.6, 18.5]** (BBG, RRB Wakako and RRG Dexter account for most of them). No other class of hands showed a consistent gain across decks (by count of Units ≤4, cards ≤2 or ≤4, cards 5+).
   - `mulligan: 'plain'` drops the card-impact keep; `'rule'` (default) is the rule above exactly. Paired `botcheck` against Hard: meta decks `--per 20` 50.7% [49.9, 51.5]; all decks `--per 1` 50.2% [49.9, 50.5]; again on top of the fitted weights (2026-11-12) 50.7% [49.9, 51.5]. **Hard uses `'plain'` since SIM_VERSION 6** (with no card impact the keep never fires anyway), and the coach since 2026-11-13 (COACH-5: it no longer teaches the keep that measured 48.9%, below).
2. **Sell first.** Sell once per turn until about 9 Eddies. Sell the card with the lowest keep value: a duplicate, something not castable soon, or Gear with no host.
   - `sellLookahead: N` (Hard and the coach use 3 since SIM_VERSION 7; Easy and Medium 0): which card to sell is chosen among the N lowest keep values by rollout (deep) or 1-ply; whether to sell stays the rule. With `deep` and `deepWorlds` > 1 (only the coach) the sells are compared in shared worlds on the main-phase deep scale (rollout mean + 5% of the 1-ply value, `valueMain`), so a sell can be compared with any other move.
   - `sellSearch: true` (BOT-4, off by default and in every tier; needs `deep`): when the rule would sell, "don't sell this turn" and the two lowest keep values are rolled out like deep candidates (in shared worlds when `deepWorlds` > 1) and the best is taken, a sell on ties. The "don't sell" play-out starts with the rest of our main phase (`playOut` move `{ rest: true }`), our clone bot barred from selling that turn (`noSellTurn`); after choosing not to sell the bot doesn't sell again that turn. Costs three rollouts on each turn the rule would sell. It takes precedence over `sellLookahead`. Measured 2026-11-12 against Hard: 25% (it under-sells, as a two-turn rollout can't see what an Eddie is worth later); `sellLookahead: 3` on Hard 50.2%, no gain (docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D1). Re-measured on version 6 (fitted weights, Eddie 26.9): `sellSearch` 51.1% [49.7, 52.6]; `sellLookahead: 3` on Hard 51.7% [50.2, 53.1] (meta), 51.1% [50.3, 52.0] (all decks), 51.7% [50.2, 53.2] (meta, a second seed), on Medium 50.6% [49.7, 51.5]: a small consistent gain for Hard. The owner switched `sellLookahead: 3` on for Hard (SIM_VERSION 7, 2026-11-12); Medium stays at 0 and no tier uses `sellSearch`.
   - `sellEddieByStage: [early, mid, late]` (sellSearch only; null by default): each sell's rollout value counts its Eddie at the stage value (turns 1–4, 5–9, 10+) instead of the weights' `eddie`. With INFO-4's stage fit (177, 38, −16) it measured 49.2% [47.6, 50.8] against Hard: no gain.
   - What the rule does (version 6, Hard vs Hard, meta decks): it sells on every turn it holds a sellable card through own turn 5 and declines only late (5–10% of possible sells on own turns 6–7); Eddies at the start of own turn k are k − 1 to within 0.06 through turn 5. Missed sells come from hands with no Sell Tag card, so there is no room to sell more or earlier.
3. **Main actions, 1-ply lookahead.**
   - For every legal action, clone the game, play the action out, and evaluate.
   - Inside the clone, sub-choices and the rival's reactions use the fast policy.
   - Each clone first hides what the bot can't see (`hideInfo`): the rival's hand is re-dealt from their unseen cards, both decks are shuffled, and the identities of face-down Legends the bot hasn't seen (its own not looked at, and all of the rival's) are shuffled among their slots.
   - Calls on unknown face-down Legends count as one option, valued as the average over the possible Legends. This holds in the main phase, in reactions and in deep rollouts.
   - Take the best action if it beats passing.
   - The ordinary play of a Legend (4.5) is not a candidate when Go Solo for the same Legend is offered at no higher cost (`dominatedLegendPlay`, SIM_VERSION 8): Go Solo makes the same Unit, ready and free to attack this turn.
   - **`turnPlan: N` (BOT-5, off by default and in every tier).** Each candidate is valued by the best sequence of our own actions for the rest of this turn that starts with it, up to N actions long (`src/ai/turnPlan.ts`), instead of by the position right after it, so a first step that looks weak alone (a fight trick before the attack it arms) is credited with what it enables. The search runs in the 1-ply screen's own world (one `hideInfo` re-deal; sub-choices and the rival's reactions by the fast policy) and scores positions with `evaluate()` and the weights in force, in our main phase (so `exposed` and `rivalLethal` apply when set). Every first action is played (its value is exactly the 1-ply value), every resulting position is expanded once, and deeper levels expand a beam of the `turnPlanBeam` (3) best positions. Below the root it never sells, calls or passes. Positions reached by different orders are merged (`positionKey`: zones with hand, field and Eddies sorted, card states, Gigs, mods without ids, once-per-turn keys; the RNG, uids and this turn's history are left out, so a "first time each turn" trigger can be merged away), and a position's value is the best evaluation reachable from it (stopping is always allowed). The budget is `turnPlanNodes` (120) nodes per decision, one node being a clone plus one action; it is a count, not a clock, so games still replay from their seed. A candidate's value is then 0.95 × the plan's + 0.05 × its own 1-ply value: several first actions often lead to the same best line (merged positions), and the 5% breaks that tie toward the step that is best on its own (without it the plan changed the 1-ply pick in 36% of decisions, almost all reorderings of one line; with it 8%); a candidate with no better line than itself keeps exactly its 1-ply value. With `deep` the top few are rolled out as before. The bot plays the first action and plans again at its next decision. `lastPlan` holds the best line. 0 or 1 = the 1-ply screen, exactly as before. Measured 2026-11-12 (`turnPlan: 3`, paired `botcheck`, meta decks, 840 pairs): Hard + turnPlan vs Hard **47.9% [46.2, 49.6]**, Medium + turnPlan vs Medium 48.9% [47.3, 50.4]: it plays worse. A likely reason is that a maximum over many mid-turn evaluations favours the lines `evaluate()` over-rates (the same selection bias as any search on a static evaluation), so it is not to be switched on as it is (docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D1).
4. **Deep mode (turn rollout).** Re-score the top N actions by finishing our turn with a 1-ply bot, then letting a 1-ply rival play its whole next turn.
5. **Reactions.** For each reaction (Call, Blocker, Quick program or ability):
   - clone the game and apply the reaction;
   - play the attack out in the clone (no further reactions, sub-choices by the fast policy) and evaluate the result, so fight and steal effects the reaction arms count, such as Safety Override's "the next time a friendly Unit loses a fight, defeat the opposing Unit" or a Blocker's "when this blocks" trigger. Until SIM_VERSION 4 the bot projected the result statically (fight loser defeated, or N Gigs moved), which valued Safety Override as a lost card;
   - keep the reaction only if it beats letting the attack resolve (also played out) by 0.5.

   With `deepReactions: N` (Hard and the coach), each reaction is instead played out N times: the attack finishes, the attacker ends its turn, and the defender plays its next turn. The N random worlds (hidden-information deal, dice) are different from each other but shared by every candidate reaction, so only the reaction differs between them. Unknown Calls are collapsed into one averaged option, as in the main phase.

   **Play-out options, off by default (BOT-1 and BOT-2, 2026-11-12).** Two `HeuristicOptions` change how deep play-outs are scored. Both are off in every tier, so with default options every game plays exactly as before; both can be set in a `botcheck` / worker `AgentSpec` (`scripts/worker.ts`) to be measured.
   - `rolloutWins: true` — a play-out stops after an end phase, just before the next start-of-turn check, so the 7-Gig win of the seat about to start its turn was never seen (after a main-phase action that is our own win after the rival's turn; after a reaction, the attacker's). With the option, `playOutValue` (`src/ai/playOut.ts`) scores that check as the game result it is: `nextStartResult` gives the winner (or a tie past the turn limit, which the engine checks first; overtime needs no case, as 7 Gigs ends the game at once there). It also clamps main-phase rollouts to ±`playOutClamp` (600), as reaction play-outs always were. The clamp makes a win in 1 of 3 worlds count as a third of a game instead of swamping the average, and keeps a won world at or above every ordinary position. It is not quite "win above everything": in 200 Medium games 0.65% of positions evaluated beyond ±600 (largest 717, 6.4% beyond ±400), so a crushing lead already ties with a win. Those are positions that are close to decided, so this was kept as it is for reactions and the reviewer rather than tuned. With the fitted weights (SIM_VERSION 6) evaluations are larger: at the start of each turn of 300 Medium games 11.8% of positions are beyond ±600 (median |value| 139, p99 1,414, against 39 and 600 with the hand weights); `playOutClamp` 1,500 against 600 for Hard changed 3 of 840 deal pairs (49.9% [49.7, 50.1]), so 600 was kept. Deep reactions use the same win scoring when the option is on. The reviewer can use it too (`ReviewOptions.rolloutWins`, off by default; `scripts/review-debug.ts` prints both values per world).
   - `deepWorlds: N` — score every rolled-out main-phase candidate as the mean over N random worlds (`makeWorlds`: the clone's RNG and the play-out bots' seeds) shared by all candidates, as `deepReactions` does; unknown Calls are averaged over their slots in each world. 0 or 1 is the old single rollout in the game's own random state, drawing nothing from the bot's random stream. With N > 1 each entry of `lastAlternatives` also carries `se` (standard error of its mean) and `seDiff` (paired standard error of its difference to the chosen move, over the same worlds), used by the coach's "close call" (§7). Costs about N× the rollout time.

   **What the bot thought (observe-only, 2026-11-12).** After every main-phase lookahead, sell comparison and reaction the bot keeps `lastScores` (`DecisionScores`): every option it valued on the scale it chose on, the 1-ply screen of a deep decision, and the shared worlds with each option's per-world value. Nothing in play reads it. Decision telemetry records a compact copy (`decisionScores()`, docs/DECISION_TELEMETRY.md), and the coach grades your move with it. `valueMain` / `valueReaction` value any option the deep way in given worlds (the coach's on-demand score of a move it did not roll out).
6. **Targets** (driven by `intent` hints that every card script supplies):
   - "harm" picks the rival Unit with the highest threat; during an attack, the rival Unit in the fight (the attacker when we defend, the Unit our attacker fights) instead, unless the effect spends a Unit that is already spent (BOT-D, SIM_VERSION 8: a "−2 power this turn" during the rival's attack used to land on a Unit not fighting);
   - a friendly Unit given up as the price of an effect (`bottomdeckOwn`, Unlikely Bond) is the least valuable one (BOT-E, SIM_VERSION 8; it used to fall to the "help" rule and bottom-deck the strongest ready Unit);
   - "help" picks the friendly Unit in the current fight, or else the biggest ready Unit;
   - Gear with "when this Unit or Legend is spent" goes on a face-up Legend, which is spent to pay each turn.
   - "May attack this turn" permissions (Adrenaline, Lag attacks) go to a ready, lagging Unit that couldn't otherwise attack, the strongest first.
7. **Modes.** Each mode is scored from its text: removal when targets exist, Draw N worth 3.5N, stun when the rival has ready Units, and so on. When the rival chooses for us (Gunpoint Diplomacy), the bot assumes they pick the weakest mode. Two choices have their own rule since SIM_VERSION 8 (each used to give one answer whatever the position): Misty names the card type most common among the cards left in the bot's own deck (it knows the make-up, not the order); for Fool on the Hill the rival, who chooses for the Fool's player, trashes the two revealed cards only when their mean `cardValue` is above the card pool's mean (two unknown draws), and otherwise lets them be added to hand.
8. **Card choices and yes/no prompts.** Decided by the decision's tag, never by the prompt text.
   - Card choices follow `CARD_TAG_POLICY`: take the best card (search, play from trash, free plays, moving Gear), give up the worst (discard, trash, bottom-deck), pick blind (looking at an unknown face-down Legend), or target the rival's best card (Detonate, Gear removal). Cards are valued with `cardValue`: base stats, roles (removal, sweeper, draw, finisher), choom.gg card ratings, card impact, and whether the card is castable soon.
   - Yes/no prompts have a rule per tag (`yesNo`): mulligan, Kerry's reroll, Jackie's replacement, trashing the top card, drawing (not when the deck is too short), discarding for Maman Brigitte, paying for El Sombrerón. Pure gains are always yes.
   - Gig swaps (Hanako, MaxTac AV) score every (give, take) pair by the Gig plan, and decline an optional swap that gains nothing.
9. **Candidate cap.** When there are more actions than `maxCandidates`, the best attack of every attacker is kept first, then the rest by the fast policy's score (`capCandidates`), so no attacker is dropped unseen.

## 7. Coach (`src/ai/coach.ts`)

In Play, coach mode asks an advisor bot for its move and explains it.

**What it shows**
- Under the prompt (every layout, phones included, as text: touch screens show no tooltips; on phones one line with a "Why?" toggle): the coach's pick, its reason and the best other option it weighed (`CoachBox`, board/CoachPanel.tsx); "Coach: thinking…" while the advice is on its way. One-option decisions are not advised (`coachWorthAsking`). A move made before the advice arrives is not graded, and the board says so once a game.
- After each of your moves, a verdict in the middle of the board, by value (INFO-1, 2026-11-12), with the Game review's names (§8; `verdictRating` in `src/lib/coachWords.ts` reads `RATING_INFO`): ● Best (the coach's pick, a move with the same name such as another copy of the card, or a move it values as high), ○ Close call, ▽ Slip, ▼ Mistake, ▼▼ Blunder (a whole Gig or more), with what the coach preferred and the Gigs given up ± the paired standard error. A close call of 0.15 Gigs or more (inside the noise only because the play-outs disagree) says "too noisy to call", not "slightly preferred". "Checking your move…" while a move it did not value is scored; "Same as the coach's pick (not measured)" or "Coach picked X (not measured)" for a choice it makes by rule.
- At game over, a check against the coach's pick: a grade (A–D) from how many measured moves (main-phase moves and reactions) were Best or a Close call, the close calls and the choices it can't measure said apart, your three costliest moves, and a pointer to 🔍 Game review, which rates every move. With nothing measured it shows no letter.
- **Gig figures** are the coach's estimate in Gig-equivalents (40 evaluation points), not Gigs on the board, and the board says so. A play-out that ends the game scores ±10,000 points (250 "Gigs"), so every figure goes through one formatter (`src/lib/coachWords.ts`: `gapFigure`, `behindText`): a gap past the decided line (`DECIDED_GIGS` = `DECIDED_EVAL` / 40 = 125) says the move wins or loses the game in the play-outs, and any other gap above `CAP_GIGS` (3) reads "more than 3 Gigs", so nothing printed claims more than 3 Gigs. The raw values stay in the grade, the export and the review.
- The board's names: "End turn" and "No reaction" (`shownLabel`), "Keep hand" / "Mulligan" (`optionName`); the engine's labels ("Pass turn", "No (more) reactions") are unchanged for bots, tests and recorded replays.

**How a move is graded** (`gradeMove`). The coach values every candidate in `COACH_WORLDS` (6) shared worlds, and its sells and reactions too. Your move is valued in the same worlds: straight from the advice when the coach valued it, otherwise afterwards, off the board's thread (`scoreMoveRequest`, queued in the coach worker behind advice; about 44 ms mean, p90 80 ms in Node). The gap is the coach pick's value minus yours, in Gigs, with the paired standard error over the worlds. Best: you are not behind; close call: under 0.15 Gigs (`CLOSE_GIGS`) or within 2 standard errors; mistake: 0.375 Gigs or more (`MISTAKE_GIGS`, the reviewer's blunder line); otherwise an inaccuracy. Effect choices (targets, modes, Gig picks) and the mulligan are decided by rules, so any choice there, the coach's own included, is "not measured" (`unscored` since 2026-11-13: agreeing on them used to count as a measured good move in the scorecard, the practice log and the review). A stored or exported older grade that agreed on such a choice is read as not measured (`coachRatingOf`, `restoredGrades`); practice-log summaries already written keep their numbers. A Gig is `GIG_SWING` = 2 × the gig weight = 40 evaluation points; the gig weight stayed 20 when the weights were fitted (SIM_VERSION 6), so the thresholds mean the same share of a stolen Gig, and the other terms are now on the same win-odds scale, which makes gaps between board moves larger and noisier in Gigs (median paired SE of a non-agree gap 0.124 Gigs over 220 graded Medium moves, against 0.026 before; docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md WP-D1).
- The move, highlighted on the board.
- Its value against ending the turn (or against no reaction), in Gigs; when ending the turn or no reaction is the pick, what the best other move would cost (or that it is too noisy to call). The mulligan says what the keep rule looks at and what the hand has (`mulliganWhy`); other rule-decided choices (targets, Gig picks, modes) have no reason. A sell compared by play-out says so (`sellPlayedOutWhy`: "of the 3 cards in hand you can best spare by card value, selling this one came out best"); a sell by rule keeps `sellWhy`.
- Up to three alternatives with their gap ± its spread, scored on the same deep-rollout scale and worlds the decision used ("≈ close call" when within the noise), one per name (never the pick's twin or the same move twice); for sells, the other sells it compared; for reactions, the other reactions.
- Live warnings: steal threat against your ready blockers, rival blockers, 6-Gig alerts and overtime.
- A matchup plan. It comes from the archetype read, your deck's cards most associated with wins ("Δ when drawn", worded as an association, with no instruction to keep them), its tempo stat, and this pairing's bot-test win rate.
- Package notes on a Gig choice are a side note (the coach picks Gigs by rule; `pkgSteer` is 0): when another option suits the package better than the pick it says so; otherwise it names an option that moves away.

**Advisor config** (`COACH_CONFIG`): the fitted `DEFAULT_WEIGHTS` since SIM_VERSION 6 (as a player, the coach's config with them wins 77.0% [72.2, 81.8] against the same config on the hand weights; paired `botcheck`, meta decks `--per 3`, 126 pairs), `deep: 6` over `deepWorlds: 6` shared worlds with the tuned profiles, `impact: 40`, `sellLookahead: 3` (compares the three most sellable cards by rollout, in the same kind of shared worlds), `deepReactions: 6` and `mulligan: 'plain'` (since 2026-11-13). Until 2026-11-12 it was `deep: 12` with one rollout per candidate and `deepReactions: 3`; the worlds cost time, so the candidates were cut (one thread in Node: 29 ms mean per advice before, 126 ms mean, p90 287 ms after). Card impact adds `40 × Δ when drawn` to the sell ranking and card choices (measured neutral, §5); the mulligan no longer uses it. When it explains a sell chosen by rule, it says whether the card values come from this list's own bot matches or are pooled from other decks (`sellWhy`), and claims no telemetry when there is none.

**How it was chosen:** `scripts/coach-eval.ts` scores sampled real decisions against a rollout oracle. The numbers below were measured before the November 2026 fixes, with the earlier config (`COACH_CONFIG_V1`: no sell lookahead or reaction rollouts). The write-up with the full numbers is `reports/coach-eval-latest.md` (the raw table from the deep set is `reports/coach-eval-depth-run.md`).
- Blunders fell from 25% (the old 1-ply coach) to 7.5%.
- Agreement with the oracle rose from 58% to 73%.
- As a player it wins 51% against the in-app Deep bot (168 games), which is not a significant edge.
- So read it as better advice, not a stronger opponent.

Re-run after retuning: `npx tsx scripts/coach-eval.ts --samples 200 --rollouts 6 [--set deep|shipped]`, then `bash scripts/coach-h2h.sh`. Every set includes the shipped coach (`makeCoach`); `--set shipped` compares it with `COACH_CONFIG_V1` only. The script defaults to 120 samples and 8 rollouts, and writes its table to `reports/coach-eval-<set>-latest.md`; the write-up above was made with 6 rollouts. `coach-h2h.sh` plays the older JSON configs; the shipped coach is not a JSON spec.

**Fixed-term gaps (COACH-14, fixed 2026-11-13).** A paired SE of about 0 means every world gives the same difference, which happens when the gap comes from a fixed evaluation term rather than from the play-outs: in 13 sampled games, 17 of 42 Slip-or-worse grades had an SE under 0.05 Gigs, 13 of them "the coach preferred Call a Legend" by 0.19–0.49 Gigs, about the `faceUpLegend` weight (9 points = 0.22 Gigs) plus the 5% 1-ply offset, at an SE of 0 to 0.024. A paired SE under one evaluation point (`FIXED_SE_GIGS` 0.025 Gigs) is now given `FIXED_GAP_SE_GIGS` = 0.25 Gigs instead (about the median paired SE the review measures, 0.29), so such a gap is a close call up to 0.5 Gigs and still counts above it (a key card sold, a game lost in every world). It lives in `withinNoise` (`src/lib/gameReview.ts`), which `isClose` now calls, so the coach and the review cannot drift apart (a test checks them on a grid). On the coach's own sample (the coach section's Easy and Hard runs) 14 of its 42 Slip-or-worse grades become Close calls (12 of 28 for Easy, 2 of 14 for Hard: all the "Call a Legend" grades and one Pepe Najarro); in 12 reviewed Medium-vs-Hard games 3 of 47 oracle Slips (two sells, one Peace Offering) did. The coach's words say so ("only by fixed terms of its evaluation") instead of "its play-outs disagree".

## 8. Match reviewer (`src/ai/reviewer.ts`)

The reviewer replays a game exactly with the code it was played on (same seeds and bots, or a practice game's recorded choices; a recorded answer that is not one of the options asked is a `ReplayDiverged`, never read). It scores each main-phase decision against an oracle: 8 play-outs per candidate, all sharing the same random worlds, each covering the rest of the turn plus the rival's reply. Scores are clamped to ±600, and positions that are already decided are not graded.

- **Candidates:** the move made, the screen bot's top alternatives, and passing. After a sell it also grades the best non-sell move ("don't sell now") and other cards that could have been sold.
- **Reactions:** every option is graded (blocks, reaction Calls and programs, or letting the attack through); unknown Calls collapse into one.

- **Validated** (before the November 2026 fixes; see `docs/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`): exact replays, 94% verdict stability, planted mistakes detected (70% vs Hard's own moves), and game-losing flags that predict losses.
- **Not yet validated:** using per-move "Gigs lost" to predict who wins.

Full write-up and backlog: `docs/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md`.

### Game review: a rating for every move (`src/lib/gameReview.ts`)

After a practice game, **🔍 Game review** (the game-over scorecard; Profile → Game history → Review; Play → Review a recent game; Profile → Game history → Open a game file… for a game exported with ⤓ Export game) rates every decision of both sides. It runs `runGameReview` (`src/sim/gameReviewRun.ts`) in the review worker, with progress and Stop: the game is re-simulated (your seat replays your answers; an online game both seats'), both seats are graded by the oracle above (8 play-outs per candidate, `GAME_REVIEW_ROLLOUTS`), except your moves the coach graded during play, which keep the coach's grade (`ReviewOptions.skip`; only the 1-ply screen looks at them). The rival is graded with what it could know (`hideInfo` for the deciding seat). `scripts/review.ts --from file --file <export>.json` runs the same function and prints the same ratings (and writes `data/reviews/<name>.game.json`); its reports also count the ratings per bot. The dialog's second tab, **Play-out details**, is the match reviewer's report on the same game (every move of your seat measured again with no coach grades), rated with the same `rateOracle` and the same names, with no letter grade or accuracy of its own; it says a move near a boundary can land one step apart. The dialog is rendered at the document body (a portal), so the Play board's CSS never reaches it.

**The replay is strict** (REV-1, REV-2; `src/sim/practiceReview.ts`). New practice and online replays carry `simVersion` and `turns`; a replay from another simulator version is refused before anything runs. Otherwise (older replays too) every recorded answer must be one of the options of the decision asked (`legalAnswer`), the record must not run out before the game ends nor be left over after it, and the winner (and, for a game played to its end, the number of turns) must match the record: anything else is a `ReplayDiverged` whose message is the plain sentence both tabs and the CLI show ("This game can't be reviewed: replaying its recorded moves with this version of the bots and rules engine gives a different game (…)"; for a version: "…it was recorded with version N of the bots and rules engine, and this is version M, so replaying it would not give the same game."). No stand-in bot ever plays a seat. A game the session ended off the board ends exactly where its record runs out, for whichever seat is asked next (`sessionEnding` in `src/lib/practiceVerdict.ts`): you conceded, the rival conceded, an abandoned online game (a draw), or a multi-day clock that ran out (`PvpReplay.timedOut`, the engine seat; older records infer it from `multiDay.timedOut` and the result). The board frames use the same ending, and for the guest of an online game (seat 1) the board is drawn from the guest's side (`FrameBoard viewer`, the state turned round and masked with `viewFor`) and the Play-out details grade the guest's seat.

**Measurements.** The reviewer records, beside `regret`, the paired standard error of the gap over the shared worlds (`se`), every other graded option's gap and SE (`others`), whether the move made is the oracle's top (`top`), the 1-ply screen's first choice with its gap and the move made's rank in the screen's order (`screen`), and `decided`. Gaps are in Gigs: `GIG_SWING` = 40 points per Gig. On 6 Medium-vs-Hard games the median SE of a non-top gap was 0.20 Gigs (p90 0.88); the coach's own gaps are noisier under the fitted weights too (median 0.124, p90 0.9; `docs/IMPROVEMENT_BACKLOG.md` WP-D1), so every band below asks for a gap clear of 2 SE before calling it a loss.

**Ratings** (g = gap below the best move in Gigs, se its paired SE; "within the noise" = g < 0.15 or g ≤ 2 se, tested on the raw gap before the 3-Gig cap (REV-3: a raw 8 ± 1.6 Gig error used to be a Close call because only the capped 3 was tested); an se under 0.025 Gigs counts as 0.25, see COACH-14 in §7; the coach's `isClose` is the same function):

| Rating | Mark | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Inspired | ◆◆ | Sharp, the 1-ply screen ranked the move third or lower (or left it off its 4-move shortlist), and the screen's first choice is clearly a whole Gig worse. Never for ending the turn. |
| Sharp | ◆ | The move is the oracle's top, every other option measured is clearly a mistake-sized gap worse (≥ 0.375 Gigs, each outside its own noise; REV-8: it used to check only the runner-up), and the screen's first choice was another move. |
| Best | ● | g ≤ 0.1 Gigs (the reviewer's `NOISE`). |
| Close call | ○ | Within the noise, g > 0.1. |
| Slip | ▽ | Outside the noise, g < 0.375. |
| Mistake | ▼ | Outside the noise, 0.375 ≤ g < 1. |
| Blunder | ▼▼ | Outside the noise and g ≥ 1 Gig, or a mistake that loses the game in at least 1 in 4 more play-outs than the best move (`gameLosing`). |
| No clear difference | = | The game was already decided in every play-out, or every option measured (the move made included) is within the noise of the best. Not counted. |
| Forced | – | One option. Not counted (the engine rarely asks these; none in the measurement below). |

Ending the turn is at most Sharp: holding back when every play is clearly worse is a real find (the quick read acts whenever any play scores above passing), but the screen never ranks passing at all, so its rank there says nothing about how hard the move was to see (3 of the 6 Sharp/Inspired moves in the review's sample were "End turn: Inspired"). Your moves the coach graded keep its verdict in the names the coach shows (`verdictRating`: agree / as good → Best, close call → Close call, inaccuracy → Slip, mistake → Mistake, or Blunder from a whole Gig), with the same "no clear difference" check over the options it scored (a move where every option was within the noise is not counted), and Sharp / Inspired only for its own pick, using the review's screen and the coach's paired scores. Moves the coach left pending or unscored, and an "agree" where the coach scored no other option (REV-11), are graded by the oracle. Sharp and Inspired need a screen, so reactions and sells chosen by rule are never more than Best. Not measured, so left out: "punished a rival error" as a reason for Sharp, and "gives up material now" for Inspired (a sell is the normal mana growth every turn, not a sacrifice).

**Confirmation** (REV-4): a move the screening play-outs rate Slip or worse is played out again, the move made against the best one found, on `GAME_REVIEW_CONFIRM` = 16 fresh worlds from a seed stream of its own (`ReviewOptions.confirm`), and its gap, SE and game-losing flag come from those fresh play-outs only (the screening value of the best move is a maximum over several noisy means, so its gap is biased upward). Every other decision is measured exactly as without it.

**Accuracy** per side = 100 × e^(−L), L = average Gigs lost per counted move, where a move inside the noise (Inspired, Sharp, Best, Close call) loses 0 and a gap counts at most 3 Gigs (`CAP`): 0.1 → 90, 0.25 → 78, 0.5 → 61, 1 → 37. Your side mixes the coach's grades with the oracle's, the rival's is the oracle's only; the seat cards say so ("n graded by the coach during play"). **Turning point** (REV-5): the losing side's counted move with the largest loss outside the noise, ties at the cap broken by the raw gap, then the earliest; with no winner (a draw, an abandoned game) either side's, called "Biggest error"; it is always in the move list (REV-9). **Rival moves** are listed only when they are a Mistake, Blunder, Sharp or Inspired, or the turning point; after a rival Mistake or Blunder, your counted moves after it in your next turn (the rest of the same turn when the rival erred with a reaction) decide "Missed chance: your next turn had a Mistake or Blunder" or "Followed up: no costly move on your next turn" (all Close call or better); neither claims a cause. The advantage line is the evaluation at the start of each turn from your side, in Gigs, clipped at ±3.

**Words** (`src/lib/reviewWords.ts`): every gap goes through the coach's `gapFigure` / `behindText` (`src/lib/coachWords.ts`), so no review text prints more than 3 Gigs ("more than 3 Gigs", or "decides the game in the play-outs"), and option names through `shownLabel` ("End turn", "No reaction"). A wide close call reads "preferred X by 1.4 ± 0.8 Gigs, but its play-outs disagree too much to call it". The screen says, under the seat cards, what the ratings rest on: the bots' own evaluation about a turn ahead (to the end of the rival's next turn; for a reaction, to the end of your own next turn), with the cards you couldn't see re-dealt, so a rating means best given what you could see, and a plan that pays off later, or that the bots wouldn't follow up, can be rated lower than it deserves (AI-8).

**Stored** with the game in `nct.gameReviews.v1` (`src/lib/gameReviewStore.ts`; `schema: 1`, newest 25, keyed by the replay id): at game over the coach's grades (`CoachRating`, small), then the finished review (`GameReviewRecord`: every rated move, the timeline, and both seats' answers so the board at each move is rebuilt with no bot thinking, `src/lib/reviewFrames.ts`), now kept beside the coach's grades. A review whose answers differ from the replay's (the game was finished again after Undo) is run again. Each review records the rating rules it was made with (`rules`, `REVIEW_RULES` = 2 since 2026-11-13; absent = 1) and, since 2026-11-14, how it was measured (`measure`, `REVIEW_MEASURE` = 1, beside `rollouts` and the confirmation worlds `confirm`). A 14-turn game with 59 rated moves took 9.8 KB (all graded by the oracle) and 15 KB with 33 coach grades and their reasons. The practice log gets your accuracy and counts (`PracticeGame.review`) for the Profile's history; older records without them still open. The store and the log are written together (`keepReview`), after a run and after a re-rating.

**Rated again from storage** (RAG plan 5.2). Every move keeps what its rating was computed from (`ReviewMove.ms`, `KeptMeasure` in `src/lib/gameReview.ts`): for an oracle grade, the screening pass and, when there was one, the confirmation (each as one gap number, exact in points under the cap and the raw gap past it, plus the SE ×100), the top / decided / game-losing bits, every other option's gap and SE and the quick screen's pick (×100 whole numbers: the reviewer rounds these to 0.01 points, so they are exact); for a coach grade, the review's screen of it (the `CoachRating` itself is in the entry's `coach` list). A move is given `ms` only when it reads back to exactly the numbers the rating read (`keepOracle` checks), so a re-rating can never differ from the build by rounding. The reviewer keeps a confirmed decision's screening figures (`DecisionReview.first`) for this. `rerateRecord` then rates a stored review under the current rules with no play-outs (about 0.2 ms for a 70-move review): it applies the current confirmation rule (`confirmFlag`) to each move's screening pass and rates a flagged move from its confirmation, any other from its screening pass (so a move confirmed under older rules that the new ones would not flag goes back to its screening figures); a coach grade is rated by `rateCoach` from the entry's grade and the kept screen. **What a re-rated review is:** the same measurements judged by the new rules. It equals a review run afresh (same game, oracle seed and measurement) whenever the same moves are flagged for confirmation as when it was measured; when new rules flag other moves, a fresh run's confirmations would draw other worlds, because the confirmation worlds come from one sequential stream per seat (`confirmRnd`), so the k-th confirmation of a seat depends on how many of its earlier moves were flagged. A move the new rules flag that was never confirmed has no confirmation to read and one is never made up: such a review cannot be re-rated (`unconfirmed`), nor one stored before the measurements were kept (`not-kept`), nor one whose kept coach grades no longer match its coach-graded moves (`coach`); those are run again, as before. **Measurement versus rating:** a review measured another way than reviews are measured now (`measuredAsNow`: `measure`, `rollouts` = `GAME_REVIEW_ROLLOUTS`, `confirm` = `GAME_REVIEW_CONFIRM`) is never re-rated, since that would pass old numbers off as new ones: it is `stale` whatever its rules. Bump `REVIEW_MEASURE` when the oracle changes (`tests/game-review-rerate.test.tsx` pins the three together); records stored before it count as the current measurement, so nothing changes for them. **Loading:** `loadGameReview` only reads: a review under other rules that can be re-rated comes back as the current `review` with `reratedNow`, and the review screen keeps it under the current rules with its log counts in an effect (`keepReview`), never during render; it is marked `rerated` (the rules it was first rated with) and the screen says, in one muted line, "Rated again with the current rating rules, from the measurements kept with this review." (`RERATED_TEXT` in `src/lib/reviewWords.ts`). Otherwise it comes back `stale` and is run again (its coach-graded moves are kept as they were when the coach's grades are no longer stored); if the game can no longer be replayed (another `SIM_VERSION`), a re-rated review is shown with no refusal on the Game review tab (Play-out details still refuses), and one that cannot be re-rated is shown under the refusal as it was, marked as made with older rules or measured in an older way (`staleText`). ## 9. Known blind spots

- No long-term planning beyond the rival's next turn, even in deep mode. The bot doesn't sandbag removal for a bigger threat on purpose.
- It doesn't bluff or play around specific Quick tricks, apart from its averaged view of the rival's hidden hand.
- High-skill control decks (BBG Econ) are under-played compared with expert humans, so treat their sim win rates as a lower bound.
- Quick Programs are mostly sold. They are the cheapest cards, so they have the lowest keep value (`cardValue` gives no credit for {Quick} or for Gear removal), and the bot spends its Eddies on its own turn, so even a held one is often unaffordable when the rival attacks. In the main phase a fight trick (Safety Override, Synapse Burnout, Reboot Optics) is scored right after it resolves, before the attack it is meant for, so the 1-ply screen never sees "trick, then attack". Crediting such a play with the attack it enables was tried and measured no gain (docs/DECISION_TELEMETRY.md, findings). `turnPlan` (§5, BOT-5) searches whole-turn sequences and does find such lines, but measured worse than the 1-ply screen (Hard 47.9%), so the default bots still have this blind spot. A reserve term valuing ready Eddies for a castable Quick card at the end of the bot's turn measured no confirmed gain (§6, SIM_VERSION 8).
- Gear that gives power is valued since SIM_VERSION 6 (power × 13.2 + 1.5 on a Unit against 4.54 for a card in hand; with the hand weights it was power × 1.2 + 1.5 against 3.6). Gear without power is not: a spend-trigger Gear (Zetatech Faceplate) is sent to a face-up Legend (`chooseUnitOpt`), where the evaluation sees only `legendGear` 2 and never the trigger, and Mandibular Upgrade (power 0, Blocker) scores the fitted `blocker` −44.1, so neither is played much. River Ward's free-Gear ability has not been re-measured.

## 10. Position benchmark (INFO-5: `scripts/benchmark.ts`, `src/ai/benchmark.ts`)

A fixed set of decision positions, each labelled by an oracle that does not use `evaluate()`, so any bot (or any bot option) can be scored on the same positions in seconds.

**The suite** (`data/benchmark/suite.json`, built by `scripts/benchmark-build.ts`). Positions are sampled from seeded Hard-vs-Hard games across the whole library, stratified by decision type (`mulligan`; `main`; `sell` = a main-phase decision with a sell among its options; `react`; `effect` = a choice inside an effect: targets, modes, Gigs, yes/no) and by stage (opening = the mulligan; early turns 1–4; mid 5–9; late 10+), at most 3 per game. Kept only: one-of choices with 2+ distinct options (copies of a card in hand, and Calls of own face-down Legends not looked at, are one option: `optionGroups`), at most 12 distinct options, and no option naming a card in a deck or the rival's hand (the re-deal would move it). A position is stored as the game's decks, seed, first player and the answers given before it; `pauseAt` replays those answers with a scripted agent (no bot thinks, about 1 ms) and pauses the game at the decision, so the file stays small, survives changes to the bots, and a bot is asked in the real game state, pending effects and all. The replay depends on the engine: an engine change that alters the game breaks it (`tests/benchmark.test.ts` rebuilds positions and checks the options), and the suite is then rebuilt with `--force`.

**The oracle** (`oracleOutcome`). At the paused decision: set the engine's random state to the world's, re-deal what the decider can't see (`hideInfo`, the bots' own rule), play the option, and finish the game with two Medium bots seeded by the world. The label is the decider's win rate over the worlds, every option of a position in the same worlds (common random numbers), so a regret (best option's win rate minus this one's) has a paired standard error. Why Medium: it costs about 0.1 s per whole game (Hard about 0.45 s), so a few hundred thousand play-outs fit in half an hour; its bias as a continuation policy is shared by every option of a position; and the outcome is a game result, not a maximum over evaluations, so it does not inherit `evaluate()`'s scale or its over-rated positions (it is not fully independent: Medium's own choices are 1-ply on `evaluate()`). Labelling: every option in 150 worlds, then the best and the options within 3 paired SE of it (at most 4) up to 480 worlds. An option is "among the best" unless it is below the best by more than 2 paired SE; a position where every option is among the best is **indifferent**: counted, but left out of the regret means. The regret of the best by its own sample is biased up (the maximum of noisy win rates), by at most about one SE.

**Scoring a bot.** `npx tsx scripts/benchmark.ts <bot> [<bot 2>] [--reps N] [--out DIR]`. A bot is a tier name, `coach`, an `AgentSpec` JSON (as `botcheck` takes) or a tier plus overrides (`'hard+{"mulligan":"plain"}'`). It reports mean regret in win-rate points with a 95% interval over the non-indifferent positions, by decision type and stage, and the share of picks among the oracle's best; with two bots, the paired difference position by position. A fresh bot is made per position (seeded per position), so plans carried between decisions (a Gig swap's give then take) are not seen. The stats are in `src/lib/benchmarkStats.ts`.

**Built and measured 2026-11-12 — it does not work yet as a yardstick.** Suite: 261 positions from 151 of 200 Hard games over all 47 library decks (31 mulligan, 68 main, 47 sell, 42 react, 73 effect; early/mid/late about equal), 4.8 distinct options on average, 426,660 play-outs in 30 minutes on 15 threads, 348 KB. On the 128 positions that are not indifferent the best-vs-second paired SE is 1.5 points (median; p90 4.1); 133 (51%) are indifferent (react 30 of 42, effect 49 of 73). | Hard + `sellSearch` | 5.6 [3.7, 7.5] | 64% | −0.1 [−0.8, 0.5] |

Easy does not score worse than Hard, so the sanity check fails. Two reasons, both measured: (1) the label depends on the continuation bot: re-labelling the 32 non-indifferent positions where Easy and Hard pick differently with Hard finishing the games (40 worlds) gave the same sign of the Easy-minus-Hard difference at only 12 of 32 (mean +1.2 points with Medium, +0.9 with Hard: neither oracle prefers Hard's picks); (2) the signal per decision is tiny: Easy loses about 13 points of win rate per game to Medium over about 40 of its own decisions, a third of a point per decision, while the labels' noise is 1.5 points and every bot shares the rule-decided effect and most sell choices (identical regret on those rows). Not to be used to accept or reject a bot change. What it would need: a continuation as strong as the bots being judged (Hard both seats: 4–5× the cost), positions where the bots disagree (sample where Easy, Medium and Hard pick differently), and far more worlds per option there.

## 11. Rank stability across bots (SIM-3: `scripts/rank-stability.ts`)

`npx tsx scripts/rank-stability.ts [--bots medium,hard] [--decks meta|all|<prefix>] [--per 50] [--seed S] [--out DIR]` plays the same round robin (every ordered pairing, `--per` deals, first player alternating) once per bot on the same deals, and prints each deck's win rate against the field with its rank and Sim-tier band (`src/lib/simTier.ts`) under each bot, Spearman's rank correlation between every two bots with a 95% interval from a paired bootstrap (deals resampled within each pairing, the same deals for every bot), and the decks that moved beyond the noise: rank (the bootstrap interval of the rank change excludes 0) or band (the letter differs and the win-rate change exceeds 1.96 paired SE). The maths is in `src/lib/rankStability.ts`.

**Re-measured on SIM_VERSION 6 (2026-11-12, both bots on the fitted weights).** All 47 decks, `--per 2`: Spearman rho **0.867 [0.797, 0.888]** (was 0.75); band letter differs for 21 of 47 (7 beyond noise), largest rank change 20, 9 decks beyond 1.96 paired SE (S1-04 Cross (RBG) 54.9% → 38.0%, Keep 'Em Guessing 36.4% → 21.7%, S2-02 Mutant (RGG) 51.6% → 63.6%). Hard cost 516 s against Medium's 26 s. With rho above about 0.8 the deck builders were left screening and confirming on Medium by default; the numbers below are the version-5 run.

**Measured 2026-11-12, Medium vs Hard.** Meta decks, `--per 50` (2,100 deals per bot, 600 games per deck, ±3.4–4.0 points): Spearman rho **0.36 [0.32, 0.76]**; 5 of 7 decks changed win rate beyond the noise (mean |change| 8.3 points). YYB Rogue Gear goes from last under Medium (35.2%, D) to second under Hard (56.2%, A), RRY Smasher Aggro from 66.5% (S) to 52.7% (B), Rainbow from 3rd to 5th; RRG Dexter is first under both. All 47 library decks, `--per 2` (4,324 deals per bot, 184 games per deck, ±7 points; Hard 465 s, Medium 42 s on 15 threads): rho **0.75 [0.71, 0.80]**; the band letter differs for 30 of 47 decks (13 beyond the noise), 19 ranks moved beyond the noise, largest change 28.5 places. The direction is systematic: Medium overrates fast red decks (Red Chrome, Smash the Curve, RRY Smasher, RRG ladder lists, S2-04 Cross) and underrates the slower ones that need decisions to pay off (YYB Rogue Gear +27.7 points under Hard, S2-02 Mutant +21.2, BBG Cunningham Econ +14.1, RRB Wakako +13.0, BBG Draw and Wipe +12.0). So a ranking or a nomination made with Medium is not a reliable stand-in for Hard: across varied decks it agrees broadly (rho 0.75) but not within the strongest field, and its errors line up with deck style, so a Medium screen would systematically nominate aggressive changes and miss control ones.

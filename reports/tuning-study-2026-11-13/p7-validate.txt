The plan and its decision rules are in p7-plan.txt, written before any of these games. The games are replayed by
p7-validate.sh (npm run gate with every stage played, from the game cache when it holds them) and read by
p7-analyze.cjs. Nothing here was recorded as an event, a rating, a ledger batch or an evidence row.

3. The plan's questions
   Q1  Medium keeps out 11 of the 16. Hard keeps out 3 of the other 5 (S1-01, S1-05, S1-08). Two enter: S1-03 Mutant
       and S2-07 Explorer. Medium shows those two better than their reference; Hard shows no deck better.
   Q2  Against the ratings. Medium: same sign 12 of 16, Spearman 0.90, Pearson 0.85. Hard: same sign 13 of 16,
       Spearman 0.81, Pearson 0.90. All 10 decks rated 10 or more points below their reference are kept out. Of the 4
       rated within 5 points (S1-01, S1-03, S2-07, S2-08), two are kept out: S1-01 by Hard and S2-08 by Medium.
   Q3  Medium against Hard. Same sign 13 of 16; Pearson 0.72, Spearman 0.74; Hard = -6.2 + 0.63 x Medium; the mean
       difference is -9.2 with Medium and -12.1 with Hard. No deck Medium shows worse has a Hard range entirely above
       zero, and no pair of plain ranges lies on opposite sides of zero. Shown worse by both bots: 9 decks; by Medium
       only: S2-05 and S2-08; by Hard only: S1-01, S1-05 and S1-08.
   Q4  Per-game spread of the paired difference: Medium median 0.59 (0.55 to 0.63), Hard median 0.60 (0.58 to 0.65).
       The tool assumed 0.70.
   Q5  The staged looks: 9,920 paired Medium games against 20,200 at full size (49%). Hard: 2,160 for the 5 decks
       Medium did not show worse, against 8,080 if every deck played Hard in full.

4. The plan's decisions
   D1  Neither condition is met (no deck shown worse by Medium has a Hard range entirely above zero; the signs agree
       for 13 of 16), so Medium first and Hard second stays the default.
   D2  The spread differs from 0.70 by more than 0.05 with both bots: the tool now assumes 0.59 with Medium and 0.60
       with Hard. At the default sizes that is a deficit of about 4.5 points shown worse 8 times in 10 by Medium and
       about 7 by Hard, where the 0.70 gave 5.3 and 8.3.
   D3  The rule was not changed.

5. Two findings the plan did not ask about
   F1  The parent rule keeps out the strong bred decks. S1-01 Mutant is rated 69.6%, fourth of the 47 decks, and Hard
       shows it 10.2 points worse than its parent, RRG Dexter Midrange, the top deck. S2-02 (61.4%, 8th), S2-05
       (57.5%, 14th), S2-06 (56.7%, 16th) and S2-04 (55.6%, 19th) are kept out the same way: each has a reference
       rated 69.6% or more. The two that enter, S1-03 (47.7%, 30th) and S2-07 (51.6%, 27th), have weak references. The rule
       asks whether a child matches its best parent, and the stronger the parent the harder that is; it does not ask
       whether the child is a strong deck.
   F2  The two bots disagree about individual decks. Hard minus Medium, deck by deck: mean -2.8 points (standard
       error 2.0), standard deviation 8.2, where game noise alone would give 3.2. So beyond noise the two bots differ
       by about 7.5 points from deck to deck, and on average Medium is the kinder one to a bred deck. D1's conditions
       did not look for this. Three of the five decks Medium let through are shown worse by Hard, and the two decks
       Medium showed better are "could not tell" with Hard.

7. What this says
   The gate does what it was built to do: it agrees with the ratings (rank correlation 0.8 to 0.9), it keeps out every
   deck the ratings put 10 or more points below its reference, and the staged looks halve the Medium games.
   Which reference it should use is a choice for the owner. The parent rule (the plan's, and the default as built)
   would have entered 2 of the 16 recorded decks and kept out the breeder's five strongest. The fixed bar would have
   entered 9, among them those five, and kept out six of the seven rated under 48%. scripts/season.ts takes --gate-reference
   parent|middle; the default stays the parent until the owner decides.
   Whether Medium should go first is a second choice. It is cheap and agreed in sign with Hard for 13 or 14 of the 16,
   but it is the kinder bot to a bred deck and it differs from Hard deck by deck. Hard alone is
   --gate-bot hard --gate-confirm-bot none; with five stages that is at most 2,800 Hard games a deck (its reference
   included), about 22,400 for a season of eight, before the staged looks and shared references cut it.

8. A trial of Season 3 (the season step at its real sizes; nothing recorded)
   npx tsx scripts/season.ts --gate-only --out DIR breeds the next season's 8 decks and gates them, and writes nothing
   into the repository. Run once under each reference, 12 threads:
   - its best parent (the default): 8 bred, 2 entered, about 7 minutes. The two that enter are the explorers, which
     play the middle meta deck under either rule (Medium shows both better, +5.8 and +6.7; Hard cannot tell, +1.0 and
     +2.1). Five of the six decks with parents are shown worse by Medium (by 11.8 to 21.6 points); the sixth, S3-04
     Cross, by Hard (-13.3 [-26.2, -0.5] at its first look) after Medium could not tell (-1.6).
   - the fixed bar (--gate-reference middle): 8 bred, 6 entered, about 12 minutes. Kept out: S3-01 Mutant by Hard
     (-8.5 [-14.1, -3.0]) and S3-04 Cross by Medium (-6.6 [-11.2, -1.9]).
   The first run of this trial showed a flaw. Replayed from the lists in its record, the references' games came out
   the same and the bred decks' did not (S3-07: 51.67% recorded, 52.60% replayed over the same 960 seeds). The order of
   a list's cards changes the games a seed deals it, and the season gated each bred list in the breeder's order while
   the record and the deck file hold it regrouped by type and cost. The verdicts were sound (either order is a fair
   deal), but the games could not be replayed. The season now gates each bred list in the order its deck file reads
   back (asWritten, scripts/gate-run.ts). The figures above are from the run after that change, and two of its decks
   replay exactly from the record (S3-07: 52.60% against 46.77%, +5.83; S3-04: 43.17% against 44.75%, -1.58, over
   1,200 games). tests/season-gate.test.ts replays two decks from a small record and fails without the change.
